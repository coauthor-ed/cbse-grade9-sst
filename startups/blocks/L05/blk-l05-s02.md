## Money coming in

You met the profit and loss account (also called an income statement) in the last lesson. Here is ABC Bakery's for March 2026; ABC Bakery is a made-up name. **Revenue** is the total money a business earns from selling its goods and services. The bakery earned ₹1,20,000 from bread and buns, ₹75,000 from pastries and cookies, ₹85,000 from cake orders and ₹15,000 from delivery charges: ₹2,95,000 in all.

<!-- STEP -->
## Money going out

**Expenses** are the money spent on running the bakery. The biggest were flour and sugar (₹1,00,000), rent (₹40,000) and salaries (₹30,000). Smaller ones were packaging, electricity and gas, advertising, a few other costs, and interest on a loan. In all, the bakery spent ₹2,22,000. You do not need to learn these lines by heart; the totals matter.

<!-- STEP -->
## Profit before and after tax

**Profit = Total Revenue - Total Expenses.** For March, ₹2,95,000 - ₹2,22,000 = ₹73,000. This is the **profit before tax**. The bakery must also pay ₹13,000 in tax to the government, which leaves ₹73,000 - ₹13,000 = ₹60,000. This is the **profit after tax**: the money the business keeps.
