# A Rigid Shell on a Soft Layer

<!-- STEP -->
## A hot, soft layer lies under the shell

The crust and the upper part of the mantle are joined into one rigid shell about 100 km thick. This shell is the **lithosphere**.

Under it lies the **asthenosphere**, about 200 km thick. It is hot and semi-molten, and it allows the shell to move.

<!-- STEP -->
## Semi-molten is not liquid

You may have pictured a sea of liquid rock under the crust, and many people do. In fact the asthenosphere is only soft enough to flow very slowly, and most of it is still solid. The mantle below it is mostly solid too.

The crust does not melt, and the fluid outer core lies far too deep to carry the shell.

Drag the shell sideways. It moves as one piece, while the soft layer under it bends.

<!-- STEP -->
## The shell is broken into plates

The lithosphere is not one single piece. It is broken into large and small pieces called **tectonic plates**, which are massive slabs of solid rock.

The theory that explains how these plates move is **plate tectonics**, given by W. J. Morgan in the late 1960s.

**Plates move over the semi-molten layer beneath them, usually a few centimetres a year.**
