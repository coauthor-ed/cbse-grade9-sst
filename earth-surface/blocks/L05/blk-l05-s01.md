## One River, Three Jobs

A river does three jobs all along its way. **Erosion** wears away its bed and banks, **transportation** carries the material, and **deposition** puts it down where the water slows.

Which job matters most changes from the mountains to the sea, so we divide a river into three parts.

<!-- STEP -->
## Upper Course

High up the slope is steep, and the water cuts down strongly. The river makes **V-shaped valleys**, **waterfalls** and **rapids**, which are stretches of fast rough water over rocks.

<!-- STEP -->
## Middle Course

The land is gentler here. The river loses energy, starts to swing in bends called **meanders**, and begins to deposit sediment. It forms **oxbow lakes** and wide flat **floodplains**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Lower Course

Near the sea the river slows further and drops much sediment. It forms **deltas** and **levees**, low banks built by floods. **Alluvial fans** are listed here too, though many form where a steep stream reaches flat ground, as at the foot of the Himalaya.
