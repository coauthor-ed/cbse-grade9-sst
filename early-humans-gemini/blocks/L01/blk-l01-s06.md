# Why the Sindhu Lipi Is Still Unread

<!-- STEP id="open" -->
## Many Attempts, No Agreement

Readings of the Sindhu lipi have been published for decades, and there are dozens of them. None has convinced other specialists.

This is not because people have not tried. The difficulty lies in the evidence we have. Scholars generally agree on three reasons.

<!-- STEP id="key" -->
## Missing: A Text with a Known Script Beside It

Start with what deciphering a script needs. Cuneiform and hieroglyphs each had a text with the same message in a script that could already be read. That gave every first guess something to be checked against.

For the Sindhu lipi, no such text has been found.

<!-- STEP id="short" -->
## Missing: Enough Writing

Next comes the amount of text. A typical inscription is about five signs long, so even thousands of seals add up to very little writing.

Scholars read an unknown script by finding patterns, and five signs in a row hold very few patterns.

<!-- STEP id="language" -->
## Missing: The Language

The hardest gap is this one. A script is a way of writing down a language that people already speak, and nobody knows which language lies behind these signs.

**So even a correct reading could give sounds that nobody recognises as words.**

## Your Turn

Three things are missing. Which one would help most if it turned up? Here are some points to weigh:

- A text with a known script beside it would let scholars check a guess at once.
- Many more long inscriptions would show more patterns.
- Knowing the language would turn sounds into words.

Then find out what has been tried, from your textbook, your teacher or online sources. Scholars have counted how often each sign appears, and they have compared the signs with later Indian scripts. Bring back one attempt and what stopped it.
