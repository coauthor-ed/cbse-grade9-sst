# Four Powers on One Line

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
## Before the Cities

Farming began in this region about 12,000 years ago. Copper tools arrived around 4500 BCE, and they helped farming, crafts and trade.

Farming then spread across the plains of the two rivers. This timeline is drawn to true scale, so a longer bar means a longer time.

<!-- STEP id="bronze" -->
## The Bronze Age Begins

The Bronze Age begins here around 3500 BCE. Bronze is copper mixed with tin, and it gives harder tools. The book's four city-based civilisations flourished from this time on.

<!-- STEP id="sumerian" -->
## The Sumerians (from about 3500 BCE)

The Sumerians came first, at Ur and other cities in the south. They began writing around 3200 BCE, and the book also credits them with the wheeled cart, the sailboat and a number system based on 60.

<!-- STEP id="akkadian" -->
## The Akkadians (2334-2154 BCE)

In 2334 BCE a new city-state, Akkad, overshadowed the Sumerians. Its records describe the world's first dynastic empire, which means one family ruling many regions.

Look how short this bar is, about 180 years.

<!-- STEP id="assyrian" -->
## The Assyrians (2154 to about 1700 BCE)

Around 2154 BCE the Akkadians lost power to Assur, a city-state in the north. Assyrian power spread across Mesopotamia and into neighbouring regions to the west and south.

<!-- STEP id="babylonian" -->
## The Babylonians (from 1900 BCE)

Now look at the dates. Babylonia became strong in central Mesopotamia from 1900 BCE, while the Assyrians were still strong in the north.

You might expect one power to end before the next begins. **These two bars overlap, because two powers held different parts of the land at the same time.**
