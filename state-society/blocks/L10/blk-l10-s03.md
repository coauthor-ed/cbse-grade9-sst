# A Complete Preparation for Life

<!-- STEP -->
## What students studied

Now the same campus has names on it. Students studied the **Vedas**, along with grammar, logic, philosophy, ethics, mathematics, science, medicine and astronomy.

These are practical skills across a wide range of subjects, so a career was certainly possible. That was not the whole aim.

<!-- STEP -->
## Arts and daily practice

Students also trained in music, dance, painting, physical education, and martial arts such as archery.

Alongside their studies, they practised **yoga**, meditation and **service to the guru**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Teacher and student

The teacher, called **guru** or **āchārya**, was highly respected. The teacher was seen as a guide who helped students move from the darkness of ignorance to the light of knowledge.

The teacher-student relationship, the **guru-śhiṣhya paramparā**, was regarded as sacred. It was close and mutually supportive.

<!-- STEP -->
## Values as well as skills

Education also cultivated inner values:

- truth, patience and regularity
- humility, control of the senses and purity of self
- a sense of the underlying unity of life, nature and environment
- reverence for all beings

Good character and moral conduct were seen as the foundation of society.

**Education was a complete preparation for life, not just a path to a career.**
