# The Belts of the Earth

<!-- STEP -->
## Long, narrow belts

The earthquakes fall along long, narrow belts, with wide empty areas between them. Each dot is one earthquake of magnitude 6 or more from 2000 to 2024.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Ring of Fire

The largest belt runs around the Pacific Ocean, an area known as the **Ring of Fire**. Many of the world's earthquakes and volcanoes occur along it.

Around the ring lie Asia, Australia, North America and South America. Countries on it include Japan, the Philippines, Indonesia, New Zealand, Chile, Peru, Mexico and the United States.

<!-- STEP -->
## A second belt

A second belt runs from the Mediterranean through the Himalaya towards Indonesia. It is often called the Alpine-Himalayan belt, and it crosses the north of India.

<!-- STEP -->
## A line in the ocean

A third, thinner line runs down the middle of the Atlantic Ocean, far from any continent. Earthquakes here fall along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, which you have already met.
