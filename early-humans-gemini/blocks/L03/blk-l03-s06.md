# Smaller Tools, Thrown Further

Later the tools become smaller again, and they change in kind. This stage is called the **Middle Palaeolithic**. Its tools are made on thin flakes struck off a stone, not from the whole stone. They are often made of fine-grained rock such as chert and jasper.

<!-- STEP id="types" -->
## Scrapers, Borers, Points

- Scrapers have a steep working edge, for cleaning hide.
- Borers have a sharp tip, for making holes.
- Points have a slim, sharp tip, and they were used in a new way.

<!-- STEP id="point" -->
## The Point

A point is small and light, and held in the hand it would barely cut. Its value comes from what it is fixed to.

<!-- STEP id="haft" -->
## Fixed to a Shaft

Tie the point to a wooden shaft and you have a spear. The small stone is now the tip of a much longer weapon. Your textbook calls such weapons projectiles, which means things that are thrown or shot.

<!-- STEP id="distance" -->
## Hunting from Further Away

A thrown spear can hit an animal while the hunter stays well back. This changes which animals can be hunted, and how much danger the hunter faces.

**The smaller tool made hunting more efficient because of how it was used.**

<!-- STEP id="open" -->
## Still Getting Smaller

From the Lower Palaeolithic to the Mesolithic, the tools got smaller.

The next tools are smaller still, and held in the hand they would be almost useless. In the next lesson we will see how people made them work.
