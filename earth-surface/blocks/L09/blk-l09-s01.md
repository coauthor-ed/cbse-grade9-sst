## Rain That Goes Underground

Not all rain runs off over the ground. Some of it soaks in through cracks in the rock.

This matters most in hills of **limestone**, a rock made from the shells and skeletons of sea creatures. The hills of Meghalaya are one example.

<!-- STEP -->
## Water Turns Slightly Acidic

Rain takes up a little carbon dioxide from the air, and much more as it passes through soil. This makes a **weak acid**.

The acid is far too weak to hurt your skin, but limestone dissolves in it. In this way solid rock is slowly carried away in the water.

<!-- STEP -->
## Cracks Become Passages

Press **More years**. Acidic water widens each crack it runs through, until cracks become passages and passages join into caves.

Landforms made this way by underground water are called **karst topography**, or simply karst. It comes from chemical weathering and erosion working together.
