## A settlement in Sapta-Sindhu

You are looking down on a settlement in **Sapta-Sindhu**, the north-western region of the Indus, its five tributaries and the Sarasvatī. The earliest sections of the **Ṛigveda** were composed here.

Historians still debate the date. Estimates run from the fifth millennium BCE to the second.

<!-- STEP -->
## Bound by kinship

Early Vedic society was organised into **janas**, groups of people bound by kinship. The Ṛigveda mentions about thirty of them, and counts five together as the **pañchajana**, the five peoples: the Yadu, Turvaśha, Puru, Anu and Druhyu.

One was the **Bharata jana**, the people ruled by the family of the Bharatas. The name Bharata first appears here.

## No written law, but still government

You will see no written law here, no list of names and no office. Yet having no written law does not mean having no government.
