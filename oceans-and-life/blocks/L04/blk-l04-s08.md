## The Bay of Bengal, and how warm the surface is

Here is the Bay of Bengal seen from above, with a slider that sets how warm the sea surface is. Move the slider and watch what the sea does.

<!-- STEP -->
## Push the warmth and the sky changes

Keep the sea cool and the water stays as it is. Push the slider past the warm mark and the water gives off more heat into the air above it, and that warm, moist air rises. The clouds over the bay begin to turn, and a spiral starts to form.

Nothing was switched on by hand. You only made the sea warmer, and the air did the rest.

<!-- STEP -->
## The same water, two different jobs

The warm water that drives the currents in this lesson is the same warm water that feeds the plankton the fish eat. A current carries warmth across the sea; a storm takes that warmth and turns it into wind and rain.

So the next lesson starts with a real question, and not an easy one.

**If the sea is warm enough to breed a storm, can a fisherman out at sea be warned in time?**
