# One Explanation for the Changing Face

Your textbook shows that faces changed, but it does not say why. Here is one well-known explanation, which links the change to food and tools. It is an idea researchers discuss, not a proven fact, so read every step with "probably" in front of it.

<!-- STEP id="jaw" -->
## Tools Did Part of the Chewing

Chewing raw meat and tough roots takes a strong jaw and big chewing muscles. Stone tools could cut and pound food before it reached the mouth, so there was less chewing to do.

Ancestors with smaller jaws could now survive just as well, and over a very long time jaws became smaller.

<!-- STEP id="brow" -->
## A Smaller Brow Ridge

Some researchers link the heavy brow ridge to the strain of chewing as well. If they are right, softer food would explain why the ridge shrinks along the same row.

Others think the ridge changed for different reasons, so this step is less certain than the jaw.

<!-- STEP id="brain" -->
## Energy for the Brain

The brain uses a lot of energy. Food that has been cut, pounded and later cooked costs the body less to digest. Researchers suggest this could have left more energy for a larger brain.

The skulls show that braincases grew while jaws became smaller. They do not show that one caused the other.

<!-- STEP id="loop" -->
## A Loop, If the Idea Is Right

A larger brain could plan better tools, and better tools could prepare food further. So each change could have supported the next one over a very long time.

**The skulls show the changes, and the loop is one explanation of them.**
