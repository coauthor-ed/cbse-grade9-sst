# Counting the Days Between Floods

<!-- STEP id="count" -->
## A Year of 365 Days

By counting the days between Nile floods, the Egyptians developed a calendar. They also watched the rising of Sirius, the Dog Star, and this gave them a year of 365 days.

<!-- STEP id="months" -->
## Twelve Months, Five Left Over

The year had 12 months of 30 days each, which makes 360 days. They added 5 extra days at the end to reach 365.

<!-- STEP id="seasons" -->
## Three Seasons, Not Four

The year had three seasons of four months each, each named for what was happening on the land:

- **Inundation**, the flood: autumn
- **Peret**, the growing time: winter
- **Shemu**, the harvest: summer

The river began to rise in summer, and the flood season ran into autumn.

<!-- STEP id="drift" -->
## Why It Slowly Went Wrong

This calendar was quite accurate, but the real year is about a quarter of a day longer than 365 days. Every four years, the calendar slipped one day behind the seasons. A slip of one day does not matter much, but try 120 years.

**Small errors add up: after 120 years the calendar was a whole month off.**
