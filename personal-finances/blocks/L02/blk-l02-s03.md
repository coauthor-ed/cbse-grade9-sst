# Safe Coins, Smaller Value

## ₹60 short

Over the year, the money in the gullak stays at ₹1,000, because a gullak pays nothing. The price of the bag climbs by 6%, from ₹1,000 to **₹1,060**.

Meera lost no coin, yet she is ₹60 short of the bag.

<!-- STEP -->
## Inflation and purchasing power

A rise in the prices of goods and services over time is called **inflation**. It is about prices in general, not the price of one bag.

What a sum of money can buy is its **purchasing power**. When prices rise and the money does not grow, the same ₹1,000 buys less than before. Its purchasing power has fallen.

## A higher rate, a bigger gap

Try the three rates on the picture. At 0%, the price stays at ₹1,000 and the gullak keeps up. At 6% the bag costs ₹1,060, and at 10% it costs ₹1,100.

**A gullak keeps every rupee, but not what the rupees can buy.**
