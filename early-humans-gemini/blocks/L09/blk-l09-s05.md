# Same Signs, Different Language

<!-- STEP id="akkad" -->
## A New Power in the North

In 2334 BCE, the power of the Sumerians was overshadowed by a new city-state centred on Akkad, further north in central Mesopotamia. The people there spoke Akkadian, a language different from Sumerian.

<!-- STEP id="language" -->
## One Sign, Two Words

Both peoples still used the same cuneiform script. Think of the sign 5: a Hindi speaker says "paanch", a Tamil speaker says "ainthu", and both read the same sign. In the same way, a Sumerian reader said "an" for the sign you made, an Akkadian reader said "shamu", and both meant sky.

<!-- STEP id="empire" -->
## The First Dynastic Empire

Akkadian records show one power spreading over different regions of Mesopotamia. The book calls this the world's first dynastic empire, which means many regions ruled by one family.

<!-- STEP id="gilgamesh" -->
## Stories in Clay

This was also a time when creative literature grew. The Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the earliest written stories, comes from Mesopotamia.
