## sundarbans-delta-from-above.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L05/blk-l05-s06.json` (`image.src`).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.13` (p.13 / book p.25). The
  textbook's printed photograph is **not** to be extracted.
- To produce: download a satellite or high-altitude aerial image of the
  Sundarbans delta from Wikimedia Commons, NASA Earth Observatory, or
  another open-licence repository. Save it here and record below: source
  page URL, file name on the source, author or credited institution if
  any, and the exact licence (NASA imagery is usually public domain;
  Commons satellite crops are often CC BY-SA or CC0 - check the file page,
  do not assume).
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.13: the **Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna
  delta in West Bengal and Bangladesh** seen from directly overhead.
  Three details are load-bearing, and every one of them is a claim the
  prose makes:
  1. **A dense lattice of channels, not one river.** The slide's first
     half is that the land is green *islands in a net*, and a single
     trunk channel would contradict it.
  2. **Dark green mangrove islands**, visibly vegetated and distinctly
     bounded on all sides by water.
  3. **Silt-green water running out into deep blue open water** at the
     seaward edge - that colour contrast is the visible evidence of the
     sediment load the slide's fifth beat is about.
- Orientation: the delta should run to the bottom of the frame, with the
  open Bay of Bengal at the bottom edge.
