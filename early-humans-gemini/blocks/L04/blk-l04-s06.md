# How Much Can a Painting Prove?

<!-- STEP id="shared" -->
## Signs That Others Could Read

Beads and engraved marks are older than most of these walls, so the habit of using signs did not begin with paint. A sign is useful only if other people understand it. For this reason, historians think that the meaning of these signs was shared within a group, even though we cannot read it today.

<!-- STEP id="repeat" -->
## The Same Scenes, Painted Again

Similar scenes repeat across many shelters, and they were painted in different periods. One good explanation is that people were passing something on, such as a story or a custom. This is a reasoned guess, because the rock keeps the scene and not the reason for it.

<!-- STEP id="spare" -->
## Days of Work

Grinding pigment and painting a large wall takes many hours. This suggests that the painters had some time to spare from finding food. It does not tell us who fed them, or whether anybody did.

<!-- STEP id="ladder" -->
## Three Rungs of Evidence

- A stone tool: *they made this and used it.* It does not say who used it.
- A bead: *they made ornaments.* It does not say what a bead meant to its wearer.
- A painting: *they painted these scenes, again and again.* It does not say what the scenes meant.

The book calls this **symbolic communication**. We know it happened, and we can only guess at what was said.
