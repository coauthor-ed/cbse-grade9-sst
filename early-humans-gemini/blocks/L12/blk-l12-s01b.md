# Why Ask a Bone?

A Shang king had real decisions to make: whether to attack, when to plant, whether a sick child would get better. Why would he ask a cracked bone? It looks strange today, but people had reasons for it.

<!-- STEP id="riggable" -->
## Getting the Bone Ready

The Shang believed that gods and dead ancestors could affect rain, harvests and war, so asking them seemed sensible. Advice from a living person may also have been doubted, because an adviser might want something for himself. So diviners got a bone ready, and they cut rows of small hollows into its back.

<!-- STEP id="unriggable" -->
## A Sign From Outside

A hot rod against a hollow made a crack. To the court, the crack seemed to come from outside the palace, from the spirits. But people did shape it, because the hollows guided where the cracks ran.

<!-- STEP id="function" -->
## What the Bones Could Do

Kings often read the cracks themselves. Nobody knows how far the court trusted them, and we cannot ask.

**One explanation: a sign from outside the king could make a hard decision easier to accept.**
