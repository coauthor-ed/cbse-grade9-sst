# One Country, Two Monsoons, Two Halves of the Year

<!-- STEP -->
## Start from the months, not the totals

Put the two stations' twelve rainfall columns side by side and read only the tall ones.

- **Chennai:** June 4.5, July 8.7, August 11.3, September 11.9 - then **October 30.6 and November 35.0**.
- **Delhi:** June 7.4, July 19.3, August 17.8, September 11.9 - then October 1.3, November 0.2.

Chennai's summer is the driest part of its monsoon. Its year is decided in the last quarter.

<!-- STEP -->
## Which wind is doing the work in October

By October the south-west monsoon has withdrawn from the whole country - you watched it leave, north-west first, seventeen weeks after it arrived.

What still rains on the Coromandel coast is the other wind. It has turned, gone out to sea, **crossed the Bay of Bengal**, and come back onshore carrying what it picked up on the water.

That is why Chennai's late rain is heavy: it fell on water, not on land.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why Delhi's is not

Delhi's rain has been over land the whole way. The south-west monsoon crosses the Arabian Sea and the subcontinent before it gets there, and Delhi stands inside it for a few weeks in the middle of the year.

**Same wind, two destinations, two different months, two different amounts.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The lesson the total cannot carry

Chennai 128.6 cm. Delhi 67.0 cm.

Those two numbers, on their own, tell a learner that Chennai is wetter. They do not tell them that the two stations are wet at **opposite ends of the same year**, from **opposite directions**, for **opposite reasons** - and those are the facts that make Chennai Chennai.

**A climate is the whole year, not the year's sum.**
