# A District Office and Its Five Members

<!-- STEP -->
## Five people, one office

Some of our best evidence comes from metal. The **Damodarpur copper plates**, from the period of Kumāragupta I, record that a Gupta district office had five members:

- the head district officer
- the chief banker
- the chief caravan trader
- the chief artisan
- the chief of revenue collection

So the banker, the trader and the artisan sit in the office beside the officials.

## Why a banker has a seat

District officers often made decisions after consulting important residents such as **bankers, caravan leaders, artisans and scribes**. These people knew about money, roads and work in the district.

## What the Guptas kept and added

The Guptas retained much of the earlier form of administration. As in Kauṭilya's Arthaśhāstra, the **mantri** headed the civil administration, and other important officers included the commander-in-chief, the general and the chief of the palace guards.

Only one post was new: a **minister of peace and war** (sāndhivigrahika). The amātyas also evolved into a broader category that included the **kumārāmātyas**, administrators at the local or provincial level.
