## Find the shared action

Before deciding whether the practices are related, write down what each one physically *does* to the water. Not what it is called - what happens to the water.

Then look for the one action that appears in every row.

<!-- STEP -->
## The three named measures

- **Contour trench** - dug along a contour line. Water reaches it and cannot get out, so it stops, soaks downward and recharges groundwater instead of running off with the soil.
- **Bund** - an earthen embankment built along a contour line. The run-off meets the bank, slows, and infiltrates.
- **Terrace** - level or gently sloping steps cut up the slope, so the flow runs across each step and never straight down a face.

<!-- STEP -->
## Zabo and the check dams

**Zabo** in Nagaland is an integrated farming approach using earthen bunds on hillslopes, for soil and water conservation together.

**Check dams** were built across small streams to reduce water velocity, prevent soil erosion and allow sediment to settle - and they slow the flow so the water goes back into the ground.

## The one sentence

Every one of them does the same two things: it **slows the water down the slope**, and it **lets the water sink in rather than carrying soil away**.

That is the mechanism the chapter carries forward. The Arthaśhāstra's own contribution sits alongside it: sorting land by fertility and productivity is a soil-science question, answered before soil science had a name for itself.

**Same rule, written down in one era and still in use in another.**
