## Aśhoka's edicts

Are these ideas only found in old books? Records from the Mauryas to the Cholas link ethics with government.

**Aśhoka's edicts**, from the 3rd century BCE, promoted dhamma by emphasising:

- moral conduct
- respect within the family
- ethical behaviour in everyday life
- non-violence and compassion

## A rule for candidates

The **Uttaramerur inscription** of the 10th century CE specifies that candidates for village assembly elections should have "honest earnings" and be "pure" of mind.

This is the rule you read in Lesson 6. Here an ethical idea becomes a condition for local office.

## Advice in literary works

Literary works from different periods also discuss moral conduct in public life. Two examples are Kāmandaka's **Nītisāra** and Bāṇabhaṭṭa's **Kādambarī**, which is widely celebrated as one of the world's first novels. Both contain reflections and advice on kingship and governance.

**Inscriptions and literary works together show a lasting connection between ethics and political authority.**
