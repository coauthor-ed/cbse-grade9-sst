# Still With Us

The book ends this chapter by saying that many of our ways of life have their roots in the Bronze Age civilisations. Here are three clear examples: three problems that any large group of people must solve.

<!-- STEP id="records" -->
## Problem One: Who Owes What?

A clay tablet from Sumer lists goods and who received them. A shop bill, a bank statement and a tax return do the same job today.

<!-- STEP id="standards" -->
## Problem Two: Trusting a Stranger

A cubical Harappan weight meant the same thing in one town as in the next. Egypt's calendar and Hammurabi's written law also fixed one rule for many people. A stamped weight at a kirana shop and a printed rupee note let you deal with people you have never met.

<!-- STEP id="legitimacy" -->
## Problem Three: Why Obey?

A ziggurat, a god-king and Heaven's appointment each gave people a reason to accept their rulers. A constitution and an election answer the same question today, with a different reason.

<!-- STEP id="close" -->
## What the Book Concludes

The Bronze Age civilisations emerged independently in fertile river plains. They shared common features, and each built them in its own way.

**Some tools you use today answer the same problems.**
