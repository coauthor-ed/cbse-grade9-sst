# The Land and Its Crops

<!-- STEP -->
## The backbone of the economy

Agriculture remained the backbone of the economy, sustaining both rural livelihoods and state revenue. Trade was also a major activity, and we will come to it later in this lesson.

<!-- STEP -->
## Five kinds of village land

For the purpose of revenue assessment, land was classified into categories. Revenue assessment means working out how much tax is owed. **Kauṭilya** describes these types of village land:

- cultivated land
- wasteland or fallow land
- high and dry land
- sown fields
- groves

<!-- STEP -->
## Crops by region

A wide range of crops grew in different regions, including rice, pulses, wheat, linseed, mustard, saffron, sugarcane, vegetables and fruits.

As farming spread in the Deccan, cotton growing probably increased, because the black soil was particularly suitable for this crop. Sangam texts describe the Chera region as rich in jackfruit, pepper and turmeric, and they describe the growing of ragi and sugarcane.

## Writing about farming

The *Milindapañho*, a Buddhist text, describes eight stages of agricultural work, from preparing and weeding the fields to harvesting and winnowing.

The *Amarakoṣha*, a standard Sanskrit lexicon, has a special chapter on forest, crops, plants and manure. A lexicon is a dictionary, so people took care to record the words of farming.
