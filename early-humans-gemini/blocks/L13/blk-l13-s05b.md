# The Road Ran Both Ways

You also studied China's contact with India through Buddhism in Grade 7. Silk went west along these roads, and Buddhist teaching travelled east along them.

<!-- STEP id="portable" -->
## A Teaching That Could Travel

Why did Buddhism travel so well? One reason is that its practice did not depend on one holy place or on costly rituals. A monk needed little more than a robe and a bowl, so he could walk with a merchant caravan. A caravan is a group of traders travelling together.

<!-- STEP id="monasteries" -->
## Monasteries by the Road

Monasteries grew up along the trade routes. Merchants could give gifts to a monastery, and caravans could rest and exchange news there. Historians think religion and trade helped each other in this way.

<!-- STEP id="both-ways" -->
## Monks Who Came to India

Centuries later, Chinese monks such as Faxian and Xuanzang travelled to India to study and to collect Buddhist texts. Xuanzang studied at Nalanda.

**The road built up by trade also carried ideas, in both directions.**
