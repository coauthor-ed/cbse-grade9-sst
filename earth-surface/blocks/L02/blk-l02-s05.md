# Plates That Slide Past

<!-- STEP -->
## No crust made, none destroyed

At a transform boundary nothing is built and nothing sinks. You might expect such a place to be quiet, but it is not. A fence built across the fault shows why.

<!-- STEP -->
## Lock and slip

The plates try to move, but rough rock holds them. The fault stays locked for years while the strain builds up.

Press **Let it slip**. The rock breaks free in seconds and the ground shakes. The two parts of the fence now no longer meet.

<!-- STEP -->
## Where the strongest quakes are

The San Andreas Fault is more than 1000 km long, and its earthquakes are a real danger.

Even so, the strongest earthquakes of all come where an ocean plate sinks. The 2004 earthquake near Sumatra, which sent out a tsunami, was of this kind.
