# Interest on Interest

<!-- STEP -->
## The second year

Sameer leaves his ₹540 in the deposit for a second year. The bank pays 8% again, but now 8% is worked out on ₹540, not on ₹500.

8% of ₹500 was ₹40. 8% of ₹540 is **₹43.20**. The extra ₹3.20 is interest earned on last year's interest.

<!-- STEP -->
## What each bar is made of

Each bar on the picture splits into the ₹500 Sameer put in and the interest on top. After two years he has ₹583.20: his ₹500, plus ₹40 from year one and ₹43.20 from year two.

The ₹500 never changes. The interest part grows every year, because it is worked out on a bigger amount each time.

<!-- STEP -->
## Compounding

Earning returns on your earlier returns is called **compounding**. You met it in Class 7, in the story of the king and the sage. It starts slowly: after two years the extra is only ₹3.20.

Over many years it grows large. The next lesson shows what that means for two people who start ten years apart.
