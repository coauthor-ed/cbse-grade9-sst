# Why Meteors Burn Up

<!-- STEP -->
## Stopped at 50 to 80 km

Most meteors burn up in the mesosphere. A rock burning in the air is a **meteor**, and a piece that reaches the ground is a **meteorite**.

Press **Send a meteor** to watch one.

<!-- STEP -->
## Cold rock, squeezed air

You may think a meteor arrives hot, but space is cold and so is the rock. It heats up only when it reaches the air.

It moves at tens of thousands of km per hour, so the air ahead of it is squeezed and becomes very hot. That heat turns the rock to vapour, and we see a bright streak.
