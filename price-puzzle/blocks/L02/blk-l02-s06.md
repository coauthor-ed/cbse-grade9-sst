# Seasons, Expectations and the Extra Mango

<!-- STEP -->
## Seasons

Bookshops are crowded when the school year begins. Sweet shops are full at festival time, and people buy sweaters and jackets in winter. The demand for many goods changes with the time of year, because of the weather, festivals and habits, even when the price stays the same.

<!-- STEP -->
## What buyers expect

What buyers expect about future prices changes what they buy today. If they expect prices to fall, they wait, so demand today falls. If they expect prices to rise, they buy now, so demand today rises. For example, many families wait until Diwali to buy a TV or a fridge, because they expect festival discounts.

<!-- STEP -->
## Each extra mango adds less

The first mango tastes delicious. The second is good. By the third, you hardly want it. Each extra mango gives you less extra satisfaction than the one before. Economists call this **diminishing marginal utility**. So you are willing to pay less for each extra mango, and this is one reason people buy more only at a lower price.
