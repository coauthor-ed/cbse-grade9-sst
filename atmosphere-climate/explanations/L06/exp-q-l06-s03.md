# Two Diagonals, Not One Road Backwards

<!-- STEP -->
## Put the two maps on top of each other

The advance runs **south-east and south, inland, out to the north-west.** The last lines on that map are 5 and 8 July, over western Rajasthan.

The retreat starts from that same corner. The first lines on the second map are 17 and 20 September, over western Rajasthan and western Punjab.

So the two sweeps run in **opposite directions along the same country**, and they cross. The advance's last line and the retreat's first line are in the same place at different times of the year.

**The retreat is not the advance played backwards.** It is a different journey.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why it cannot retrace its own steps

Here is the thing to notice: the reversal has a **direction**, and that direction decides everything.

In autumn the land cools before the sea does - and it cools at different rates in different places. The far north-west, which has been hot and dry all summer and has the least water anywhere near it, gives up its heat first.

So the north-west is the first part of the country the monsoon leaves, and the far south - still warm, still next to open water - is the last.

**The place the wind reached last is the place it leaves first.** That is why the two diagonals cross, and it is a consequence of the same land-sea difference that started the season.

<!-- STEP -->
## What it means on the ground

Two consequences worth having, because both are checkable against ordinary experience.

- **October and November in the north-west are usually cloudless.** The line has gone. There is nothing left to rain with, in the driest part of the country.
- **The far south gets its rain then instead.** Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and parts of Karnataka - because the north-east monsoon leaves the land, crosses the Bay of Bengal, and lands on the far shore carrying what it picked up on the water.

And that second one is the closing of a loop. **The same cold, dry, offshore wind that won you nothing on the west coast is the reason the east coast has its winter rain.** The only difference is what it crossed on the way.
