# A Temple That Was Also a Treasury

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
## The Ziggurat

Each city built a grand temple for its chief god, called a ziggurat. It was a tower-like stepped building with several floors, and the main temple stood at the top. The city grew around it.

This picture is a drawing of how such a building may have looked.

<!-- STEP id="stairway" -->
## Who Could Go Up

Stairways led up the levels. The top was a holy place, and entry was restricted to high priests and priestesses. The book says they were probably part of the ruling class.

<!-- STEP id="gate" -->
## One Entrance

The walls around the ziggurat had only one entrance, because the ziggurat also served as the city's treasury. A treasury is the place where a city keeps its wealth. Palaces and royal storehouses stood in the area around it.

<!-- STEP id="economy" -->
## Temple and Work

The temple's power went beyond worship. Farming, trade and the transport of goods were all tied to the temple authority.

<!-- STEP id="gods" -->
## Many Gods

The Sumerians worshipped many gods, and believed these gods had power over natural forces such as floods and winds. People whose lives depended on a river they could not control had good reason to care what such gods wanted. So they built a temple for each god.
