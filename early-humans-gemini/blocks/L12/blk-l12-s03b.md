# Why Did the Qin Last Only Fifteen Years?

The Qin conquered every rival state and then fell within 15 years. How do we know why? This slide goes beyond the book, because it shows how historians check a story like this one.

<!-- STEP id="labour" -->
## Very Heavy Work

The Qin carried out huge projects at the same time: walls joined, roads built, and a vast tomb for the First Emperor. Huge numbers of people were called up to work on them by order, and many had no choice.

<!-- STEP id="harshlaw" -->
## The Story of the Late Workers

The best-known account comes from Sima Qian, a historian of the Han dynasty. He wrote about a hundred years later. He says a group of workers was held up by heavy rain, and the penalty for arriving late was death. So they rebelled, because they had nothing left to lose.

<!-- STEP id="revolt" -->
## Who Told Us?

Sima Qian wrote under the Han, the dynasty that replaced the Qin, so his side had a reason to show the Qin as cruel. Bamboo strips with real Qin laws have since been dug up, and they list smaller penalties for lateness, such as a fine.

The revolts did happen, and the Qin fell in 206 BCE. The reason is less certain.

**Always ask who wrote a record, when, and for whom.**
