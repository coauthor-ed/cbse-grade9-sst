## A storm that starts over warm water

Cyclones are caused by atmospheric disturbances over warm ocean waters. Scientists say the sea surface needs to be about 26 to 27 degrees Celsius or warmer to feed one. Move the slider and see when the clouds begin to turn.

<!-- STEP -->
## The shape from above

A cyclone is a large rotating storm, and north of the Equator it turns anticlockwise. In this drawing, bands of cloud wind in a spiral round a calm centre called the **eye**. Real satellite images show the same shape.

## Every storm gets a name

Cyclones in the North Indian Ocean are named in turn from a list of 169 names, sent in by 13 member countries including India. Clear names help everyone follow the same storm. Biparjoy (2023) and Remal (2024) are recent examples. You can track cyclones on the School Bhuvan-NCERT Geoportal.
