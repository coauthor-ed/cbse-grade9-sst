## The Pallavas

Like other kingdoms of this period, the **Pallavas** (about 275 to 897 CE) in the south combined a centralised monarchy with decentralised local governance. The king was the supreme head, and the kingdom was divided into provinces, districts, taluks and villages.

In the scene, grain goes from the village to the capital as tax.

<!-- STEP -->
## A tax-free grant

One of the most striking features of the Pallavas was the **tax-free land grant** to villages. These were known as **Brahmadeya** villages.

Press **Lay the grant** and watch the tax stop.

A king who gave up this tax did not lose his power: he remained the supreme head of the state.

## Agrahārams and learning

The **Chālukyas** of Badami (about 543 to 753 CE) followed a similar pattern. They gave land grants to Brahmin settlements known as **agrahārams**.

Some agrahārams, such as those at **Aihole** and **Badami**, gradually developed into prominent centres of learning.

**Pallava grants created Brahmadeya villages, and Chālukya grants created agrahārams.**
