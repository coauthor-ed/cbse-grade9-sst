# Land Between Two Rivers

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
## The Land in Between

Mesopotamia is a Greek name that means "the land in between". The land is between two rivers of West Asia, the Euphrates and the Tigris. Today it is mainly Iraq and Kuwait, with some parts of Turkey and southwestern Iran.

To understand this civilisation, start with what this land has and what it lacks.

<!-- STEP id="crescent" -->
## The Fertile Crescent

Foothills of the Zagros and Taurus mountains curve from the Mediterranean to the Persian Gulf, in the shape of a crescent moon. This is the Fertile Crescent, named for its good farmland. Farming began here about 12,000 years ago.

<!-- STEP id="anomaly" -->
## A Hard Plain

Now look at the plain between the rivers, further south. Little rain falls there, and the rivers flood at unreliable times and sometimes shift their course.

Stone, timber and metal ores are also scarce on this flat land.

<!-- STEP id="challenge" -->
## What Could They Build With?

A city needs walls, roofs and tools, and this plain has few of the usual materials. What it does have is what the rivers bring: water, mud and clay.

**Whatever people built here had to start with what the rivers left.**

<!-- STEP id="cities" -->
## Cities Grew Here Anyway

Even so, it is here in the south, in a land called Sumer, that the earliest cities grew: Ur, Uruk, Eridu, Lagash and Nippur.

How could cities grow in a place like this? The rest of this lesson answers that.

<!-- STEP id="westasia" -->
## West Asia Today

The book asks which countries make up West Asia today. The old Fertile Crescent lies across parts of today's Iraq, Kuwait, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Israel, Palestine, Turkey and Iran.

These names are modern. None of them existed when Ur was a city.
