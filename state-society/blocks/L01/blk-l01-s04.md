# How We Know Any of This

<!-- STEP -->
## The earliest texts we can read

We know about the assemblies from the **Vedas**, the earliest known body of Indian literature. They hold that place especially because the Harappan script has not yet been deciphered, so we cannot read what the Harappans wrote.

Vedic literature was also not written at first. It existed in **oral form for several centuries**.

<!-- STEP -->
## From teacher to pupil

A pupil learned each hymn from a teacher, sound by sound, and repeated it until it was exact.

You might expect a spoken text to change each time it is passed on. Instead, **trained reciters passed the hymns on with great exactness for centuries.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Then written down

Later the hymns were written down. This page is a Ṛigveda manuscript in the Devanagari script.

Historians read texts like this to learn about the people who composed them: their lives, their social and political systems, their work and their religious practices.

<!-- STEP -->
## Four Vedas

There are four Vedas. Tap each book to see what it holds.

- **Ṛigveda**: the earliest, with 1,028 hymns
- **Yajurveda**: how rituals are performed, with explanations in prose
- **Sāmaveda**: hymns from the Ṛigveda arranged for singing
- **Atharvaveda**: a wide range of hymns, some to ward off evil, others to treat illness

<!-- STEP -->
## Four parts in each

Each Veda has the same four parts.

- **Samhitā**: hymns used to call on a deity and make offerings
- **Brāhmaṇa**: prose that explains ritual performances
- **Āraṇyaka**: thoughts of sages living in forests
- **Upaniṣhad**: questions about the Self and the Ultimate Universal Being
