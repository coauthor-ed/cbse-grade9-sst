# One Organ Stayed

<!-- STEP id="body" -->
## Preparing a Body

When Egyptian embalmers prepared a body for burial, they took out the brain and did not keep it. They also removed the internal organs, dried the body with natron, a natural salt, then oiled it and wrapped it in strips of linen.

<!-- STEP id="jars" -->
## Four Organs, Four Jars

The lungs, stomach, liver and intestines each went into a separate stone jar, so four organs were kept in four jars. One organ was treated differently from all the others.

<!-- STEP id="heart" -->
## The Organ Left Behind

The heart was left in the chest. A carved stone beetle called a scarab was often laid over it.

**Everything else came out. The heart stayed.**

<!-- STEP id="pyramid" -->
## A Mountain Over One Grave

Here is a second puzzle, on a much larger scale. At Saqqara, six shrinking tiers of limestone were raised over the burial place of one ruler, King Djoser. Thousands of people must have worked on it.

<!-- STEP id="puzzle" -->
## One Belief Behind Both

Why leave the heart in? And why raise so much stone over one body?

**Both puzzles lead to what Egyptians believed happens after death.**
