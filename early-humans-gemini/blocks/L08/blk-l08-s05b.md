# Two Kinds of Flood

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
Canals and dams could manage an ordinary flood, the kind that comes at about the usual time and depth. That still left another kind of flood.

<!-- STEP id="freak-flood" -->
## The Flood That Came Without Warning

Melting snow in the far-off mountains could send a sudden surge down the rivers. People on the plain had no way of knowing it was coming. A bank built for an ordinary year could not stop it.

<!-- STEP id="two-answers" -->
## Two Risks, Two Answers

Here is one way to read what the Sumerians did. Historians suggest that they met the two kinds of flood in two ways. The flood they could plan for got a canal and a dam, and the flood they could not plan for got prayer and offerings to the gods.

This is a reading, not a record. The book's fact is simpler: they believed gods had power over floods and winds.

<!-- STEP id="not-superstition" -->
## A Reasonable Belief

You might think offerings show that people did not understand the world. Look at their position, though. They had done all that work could do, and a danger still remained that no work could stop.

**Asking a god for help was a reasonable response to a danger they could not control.**
