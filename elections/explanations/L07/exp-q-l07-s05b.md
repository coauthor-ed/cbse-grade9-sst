# Three Lists, Not One Ladder

<!-- STEP -->
## The three national routes, one at a time

A party is recognised as national if it secures **not less than 6% of valid votes polled in four or more States** *and* wins **four Lok Sabha seats**; **or** wins **at least 2% of the Lok Sabha seats** with candidates elected from **at least three States**; **or** is recognised as a **state party in at least four States**.

This party has two States, six seats, and two state records:

- The **6%** it polled is in the right range but in **two** States, not four or more - and six seats are more than the four the first route needs, so this route fails on the States alone.
- The **2%** route is a seat route: 2% of 543 is about **eleven**, and the party has **six**.
- The third route needs state party recognition in **four** States. This party has it in **two**.

**No national route. It is not a national party.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why it is not in the third class either

This is the half learners miss. Failing to be national does **not** mean being a **Registered Unrecognised Political Party**.

That class has two members only: parties that have **not secured enough percentage of votes** in the Legislative Assembly or the general elections, and parties that have **never contested an election** since registration.

This party has secured enough percentage of votes in two States - enough to be a state party there, on the **8%** route. And it contested seats and won them. **Neither half of the registered unrecognised description fits it.**

<!-- STEP -->
## What "one condition is enough" does not mean

The phrase above both panels says **any one condition should be fulfilled**. It does not say the three classes are steps.

- A party can be a state party in several States and never be national.
- A party can be national without ever being a state party anywhere - the seat route needs no state record at all.
- A party is a registered unrecognised party only when it clears **no** route in either list, or has never stood in an election.

**The classes are three separate lists. A party belongs to whichever list catches it, and it can belong to two of them at once.**
