# Written About, But Not Read

<!-- STEP id="wall" -->
## Two Kinds of Record

Historians can learn about old Egypt from two kinds of record: what visitors wrote about it, and what Egyptians wrote themselves.

<!-- STEP id="greek" -->
## What Visitors Wrote

Egypt was known to the Greeks and Romans. Greek writers such as Herodotus visited and wrote about it as early as the 5th century BCE. A 19th-century collection, *de L'Egypte*, added many more details.

<!-- STEP id="papyrus" -->
## What Egyptians Wrote

Egyptians kept their own records too, on papyrus, an early kind of paper. These records hold stories and practical details, and they show how Egyptians understood the world.

<!-- STEP id="temple" -->
## Seen, But Not Understood

After the last readers died, the writing was still there on temple walls and scrolls. For about 1,400 years, people could look at it but could not read it.

**Being written about is not the same as being read.**
