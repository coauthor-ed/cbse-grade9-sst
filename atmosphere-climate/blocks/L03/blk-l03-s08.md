# The Same Machine, With the Sun Turned Up

<!-- STEP -->
## The coast you already met

One lesson ago this coastline ran once a day. Land, sea, warm surface, rising air, low, wind - and then the whole thing reversed when the sun went down and the sea became the warmer surface.

That was the mechanism. This is the same mechanism with the reason visible, because now you know what the light is doing before it gets there.

<!-- STEP -->
## Same sunlight, different surfaces

The two surfaces take exactly the same beam, at exactly the same angle, at exactly the same moment. The insolation landing on this beach and the insolation landing on the water beside it are the same number.

They still end up at different temperatures.

Not because one got more light. **Because they are built differently.** The sun's energy goes into a shallow layer of dark ground and a deep body of water, and the two do not let it go at the same speed.

That difference in temperature is the whole of the sea breeze, and it was never about the light arriving at an angle. It was about what the light arrived on.

<!-- STEP -->
## Once a day, or twice a year

This coast turns around once a day, because the ground cools faster than the sea does, and once a day is how long that takes.

**The Indian landmass against the Indian Ocean is the same picture with the clock changed.**

Same rule. Same reversal. But the landmass is enormous and the ocean is enormous, and neither one gets back to where it started in twelve hours.

**What makes the Indian coast turn around - and why it only does it twice a year - is the next lesson.**
