## Papads made at home

There was no factory at first. Every morning, each woman took raw dough home. She rolled papads through the day, between her household tasks, and brought the finished papads back by evening. There was one condition: the quality had to be consistent, so every papad had to be as good as the last one.

## Equal owners

The women started a **cooperative society**. Every member who joined would be an equal owner, and profits would be shared equally, whether a member was senior or new. In 1962 they registered it as Shri Mahila Griha Udyog Lijjat Papad. In Gujarati, *lijjat* means tasty.

## From ₹80 to ₹1,600 crore

Many branches opened across India, and thousands of women joined. In the 1980s and 90s, Lijjat began to make flour, masalas and soaps, and to sell abroad. Its **turnover**, the total money from its sales, crossed ₹100 crore. By 2023 it had over 45,000 women members, 83 branches and a turnover of ₹1,600 crore a year.
