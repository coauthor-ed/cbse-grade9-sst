# Reading the Rain

<!-- STEP -->
## Why people measured rain

Crop production often depended on the seasonal monsoon rains, so knowing the rain mattered to every farmer.

Kauṭilya's *Arthaśhāstra* contains records of rainfall measurements. They were used in managing the country's revenue and relief.

<!-- STEP -->
## Reading the sky

The *Kṛiṣhiparāśhara* predicted rain mainly from the positions of the Moon and the Sun. Varāhamihira's *Bṛihatsaṁhitā* used lunar mansions, or **nakṣhatras**.

A nakṣhatra is one of 27 equal parts of the sky along the Moon's path. The Moon moves through about one part each day.

<!-- STEP -->
## A poet's date

Kālidāsa's *Meghadūtam*, written around the 5th century CE, mentions the date the monsoon begins over central India and traces the path of its clouds.

Even today, many farming practices in India are based on these ancient methods.

<!-- STEP -->
## Forecasts today

Under the **National Monsoon Mission**, the Ministry of Earth Sciences has developed weather and climate prediction models.

**Mission Mausam** aims to make India "Weather Ready" and "Climate Smart", with timely forecasts for farming, disasters and rural development.
