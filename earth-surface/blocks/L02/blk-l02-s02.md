# Three Kinds of Plate Boundary

<!-- STEP -->
## Continent against continent

What happens depends on what each plate carries. The edges where plates meet are **plate boundaries**, and a **convergent boundary** is one where two plates move towards each other.

Continental crust is thick and light, so neither plate sinks far. The rock bends and rises in **folding**, and this makes fold mountains such as the Himalaya.

<!-- STEP -->
## Ocean against continent

Now one plate carries ocean floor, which is thin and dense. The oceanic plate sinks beneath the continental plate, and this leads to volcanoes and earthquakes.

The Andes formed this way, where the Nazca plate sinks under South America.

<!-- STEP -->
## Ocean against ocean

When two ocean plates meet, one sinks under the other. A deep trench forms, and volcanoes rise from the sea floor as a curved line of islands.

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands lie on such an arc, where the Indian plate sinks under the plate to the east.

<!-- STEP -->
## Moving apart

At a **divergent boundary**, two plates move away from each other. Magma rises from below and forms new crust, which builds a mid-ocean ridge. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is a good example.

<!-- STEP -->
## Sliding past

At a **transform boundary**, two plates slide past each other. No crust is made or destroyed here. The crack along which the rock moves is a **fault**, such as the San Andreas Fault in the United States.
