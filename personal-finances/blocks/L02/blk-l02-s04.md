# Priya and Sameer

<!-- STEP -->
## The same ₹500, two endings

Priya and Sameer each get ₹500 as a birthday gift. Priya keeps hers in a gullak, and a year later she still has ₹500.

Sameer puts his ₹500 in a bank deposit that pays **8% a year**. A year later he has **₹540**. The extra ₹40 is **interest**: what the bank pays him for the use of his money.

<!-- STEP -->
## Investing

Sameer invested. **Investing** means putting your savings into assets, such as a bank deposit, gold or shares, to protect their value and earn a **return**. It is like planting a mango tree: the fruit does not come at once, but then it comes for many years.

At 8% a year, Sameer's money grows faster than prices rising at 6%.

<!-- STEP -->
## Liquidity

Priya's cash has one strength: she can spend it any day. How quickly something can be turned into cash, without losing value, is its **liquidity**. Cash is the most liquid of all.

Sameer's deposit grows, but to get the full ₹40 he must leave it in for the year. Both are careful choices; which suits you depends on when you need the money.
