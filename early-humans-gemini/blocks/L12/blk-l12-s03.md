# Four Dynasties

Historians organise China's history into dynasties. A dynasty is a line of rulers from one family. Four names carry this part of the story: Shang, Zhou, Qin and Han.

<!-- STEP id="bronze-age" -->
## Shang and Zhou

The Shang ruled from 1600 to 1046 BCE, and the Zhou from 1046 to 256 BCE. The book calls them two well-known dynasties of the Bronze Age. Together they cover more than 1,300 years.

<!-- STEP id="iron-age" -->
## Iron Arrives

By 600 BCE the use of iron had become popular throughout China, so the Chinese Iron Age is dated from about this time. The Zhou were still ruling then, so one dynasty can run across two ages.

<!-- STEP id="qin" -->
## Fifteen Years

The Zhou ended in 256 BCE, and for about 35 years rival states fought for power. In 221 BCE the Qin (say "chin") conquered the other states and became the first imperial dynasty, which means one emperor over the whole country. The book credits it with unifying China. It lasted only 15 years, to 206 BCE, but the name "China" probably comes from it.

<!-- STEP id="han" -->
## Four Centuries of the Han

The Han followed, from 206 BCE to 220 CE, which is more than 400 years.
