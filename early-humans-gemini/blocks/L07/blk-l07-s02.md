# The Same Places, Earlier

<!-- STEP id="rivers" -->
## Two River Basins

A river basin is all the land that drains into one river and its streams. The Harappan settlements grew in two of them.

The Indus, or Sindhu, flows down from the Himalayas to the Arabian Sea. Further east lies the Ghaggar-Sarasvati basin, and today much of it is a dried river belt.

Both have fertile alluvial plains, which means flat land covered in fine soil laid down by rivers. This soil is good for farming.

<!-- STEP id="early-sites" -->
## Villages Before the Cities

Long before the big cities, Early Harappan settlements already stood across this region.

- In the north and west: Mehrgarh, Rehman Dheri, Jalilpur, Kot Diji and Amri.
- Near the coast and in Gujarat: Balakot, Datrana, Loteshwar, Padri and Somnath.
- In the Sarasvati belt: Bhirrana and Kunal.

Some radiocarbon dates suggest that Bhirrana and Kunal go back even further, to between 7000 and 5500 BCE. Radiocarbon dating measures the age of old plant or bone material in a laboratory.

<!-- STEP id="mature-cities" -->
## Cities in the Same Region

The Mature Harappan cities did not rise in empty land. They grew in the same region as those older settlements.

- Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro stood in the Indus plains.
- Rakhigarhi and Kalibangan stood in the Sarasvati belt.
- Dholavira and Lothal stood in Gujarat.

Harappa itself has Early Harappan remains, and so does Kalibangan.

**The cities grew in a region people had farmed and known for a long time.**
