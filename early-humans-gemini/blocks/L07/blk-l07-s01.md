# The Tidy Cities

<!-- STEP id="grid" -->
## Cities That Look Alike

In Grade 6 you met the Harappan cities: Mohenjo-Daro, Harappa, Rakhigarhi and Dholavira. They stood hundreds of kilometres apart, and yet their plans are very similar.

- Streets run straight and meet at right angles.
- Covered drains carry away dirty water.
- Bricks are made in the same proportions.
- Weights and seals from one city match those from the next.

You might think some ruler ordered all this at one time. **Cities this alike do not appear suddenly.**

<!-- STEP id="early-layers" -->
## Villages Came First (7000-3300 BCE)

To see how this began, archaeologists studied older sites in the same region. The Neolithic stage in this region starts around 7000 BCE at Mehrgarh, with farming villages.

The book's chart starts the Chalcolithic stage at 4500 BCE. The word means copper and stone together. By about 4000 BCE some settlements had learned to get copper from its ores, and pottery was already different from one region to the next.

<!-- STEP id="early-harappan" -->
## The Early Harappan Stage (3300-2600 BCE)

The Bronze Age begins here around 3300 BCE. Copper tools on the fertile river plains helped people produce more, so these communities grew richer. Historians call this stage Early Harappan because the sites show a steady link with the cities that came later.

- Pottery was made on a large scale.
- Beads, shell bangles and copper work improved slowly.
- Walls around settlements probably began in this stage.
- Seals and the Harappan script probably began here too.

<!-- STEP id="mature-late" -->
## The Cities, and After (2600-1300 BCE)

Only after this long stage do the famous cities appear. The Mature Harappan stage runs from 2600 to 1900 BCE. Many regional styles had become one shared style by about 2500 BCE.

The Late Harappan stage follows, from 1900 to 1300 BCE. This chapter does not tell that story, but we will use its dates.

<!-- STEP id="runup" -->
## The Long Run-Up

Think of a fast bowler. The ball leaves the hand in a moment, but the run-up behind it is long. These cities were like that.

From 7000 BCE to 2600 BCE is 4,400 years. The Mature stage lasted 700 years, so the run-up was more than six times as long.

**The cities came after more than 4,000 years of villages and crafts.**
