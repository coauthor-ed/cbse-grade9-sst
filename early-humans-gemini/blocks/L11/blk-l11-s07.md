# Whose Sandals Were These?

<!-- STEP id="objects" -->
## Three Objects, One Question

Suppose reed sandals, a painted jar and a woven box are found together. Whose were they: a noble's, or an artisan's? Three tests help us work it out.

<!-- STEP id="material" -->
## Test 1: What Is It Made Of?

Reeds, clay and wicker grew or lay along the Nile and cost little, so owning them says little about wealth. Gold and other materials brought from far away were much harder to get.

<!-- STEP id="wear" -->
## Test 2: Was It Used?

Scuff marks and a mended strap show that someone wore an object day after day. An object with no wear at all may have been made for the burial.

<!-- STEP id="context" -->
## Test 3: Where Was It Found?

A tomb shows what a family chose to set aside for the dead. That may not be what they used every day.

<!-- STEP id="close" -->
## What the Tests Tell Us

These three objects are made of cheap materials, so they could belong to a free farmer or an artisan. Wear would show that they were used. They do not prove who owned them.

**The best reading: ordinary things from an ordinary home, but we cannot be sure.**
