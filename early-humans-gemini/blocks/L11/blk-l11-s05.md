# Who Did the Work?

<!-- STEP id="pharaoh" -->
## The Pharaoh

The book draws Egyptian society as a pyramid with four levels. At the very top stood one person, the pharaoh, who was treated as a god on earth.

<!-- STEP id="officials" -->
## Officials, Nobles and Priests

Below the pharaoh came government officials, nobles and priests. Priests led the rituals in the temples, and scribes kept track of grain and taxes.

<!-- STEP id="artisans" -->
## Free Landholders, Artisans and Merchants

The third level held free landholders, artisans and merchants. These were the people who made fine pottery, carved statues and traded goods.

<!-- STEP id="labourers" -->
## Serfs and Slaves

At the base were serfs and slaves, the largest group. A serf is a farm worker tied to land that someone else owns. They grew the food that fed everyone above them.

<!-- STEP id="direction" -->
## Who Built the Pyramids?

Pyramids were made for the top level, but the work came from below. Skilled crafts came from the third level, and the heaviest work from the base. Historians think many builders were farmers, called up when floods covered their fields, and that some were paid in food.

**Food and labour moved up the pyramid, from the many to the few.**
