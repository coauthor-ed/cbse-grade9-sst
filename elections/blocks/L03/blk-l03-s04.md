# Three Election Laws

<!-- STEP -->
## The Act of 1950

Two major laws govern elections in India, and a third covers the President and Vice President. The **Representation of the People Act, 1950** deals mainly with:

- allocation of seats and delimitation of constituencies
- preparation and revision of electoral rolls, which are the lists of voters
- the right to vote without discrimination, for citizens of 18 years and above

<!-- STEP -->
## The Act of 1951

The **Representation of the People Act, 1951** deals with all the other aspects of conducting elections and with disputes after them. It lays down rules for:

- nomination of candidates
- election campaigns
- voting procedures
- resolution of disputes

<!-- STEP -->
## The Act of 1952

There is also the **Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952**, for those two offices.

Together, these Acts ensure the integrity, transparency and accountability of the process. The Act of 1951 also lists electoral offences and corrupt practices. Here are three examples.

<!-- STEP -->
## Example 1: A Gift for a Decision

**Any gift, offer or promise made by a candidate, or with their consent, to make a person contest, withdraw, vote or refrain from voting.**

A gift may be meant to make a person contest, withdraw, vote or stay away. The law covers all four.

<!-- STEP -->
## Example 2: An Appeal to Identity

**Appealing to people to vote or refrain from voting on the basis of religion, caste, race, community or language.**

A candidate may ask for votes on what they will do, and not on who a voter is.

<!-- STEP -->
## Example 3: Help From Officials

**Candidates, or anyone on their behalf, may not take assistance in favour of the candidate from government-related personnel.**

This includes gazetted officers, judges and magistrates, armed forces personnel, police personnel and excise officers. Tap the examples to go back to any one of them.
