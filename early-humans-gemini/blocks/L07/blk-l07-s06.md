# Weights Agreed, Water Held

<!-- STEP id="weights" -->
## Weights That Matched

Farming was not the only skill that grew. Trade between regions needed agreed measures, and the Harappans had their own system.

Cubical stone weights have been reported from several sites. The smaller ones go up in doubling steps: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on. The larger ones go up in multiples of ten.

<!-- STEP id="dholavira" -->
## Keeping Water at Dholavira

Dholavira stood in dry Kachchh, so water had to be saved. Its people built dams and canals to turn water from two nearby streams into a series of deep tanks.

The tanks were joined to each other and built of stone and mud brick. Some were cut deep into the bedrock, the solid rock under the soil.

<!-- STEP id="lothal" -->
## The Dockyard at Lothal

At Lothal in Gujarat stands a huge dockyard, a basin of water where boats could be kept and loaded. It is built entirely of burnt bricks, which do not melt in water as mud bricks do.

<!-- STEP id="waterchain" -->
## Water Skills Have a Run-Up Too

Like the crafts in this lesson, these water works did not appear suddenly.

Early Harappans in the foothills of Baluchistan built gabarbands, which are small check dams across streams, to water their fields. The tanks of Dholavira are a much more developed form of the same skill.

**Saved water and agreed weights both grew slowly, over many centuries.**
