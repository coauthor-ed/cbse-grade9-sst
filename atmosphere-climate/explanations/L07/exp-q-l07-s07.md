# Leh: Not Skipped, but Emptied

<!-- STEP -->
## Half of the claim is right

Look at the advancing isochrones again. The sweep runs up from the southern tip and out across the Gangetic plain, and the last lines it reaches are in the far north-west.

Leh at 34 degrees north sits beyond that edge. So far, so good - **the front does not sweep over Leh.**

But the claim goes on to say the monsoon *never reaches* it, and that is where it stops matching the table.

<!-- STEP -->
## Read Leh's monthly column

It is not empty. Every month from January to October carries between 0.5 and 1.3 cm, and November is blank. The annual total of 8.5 cm is the sum of a thin drizzle spread almost evenly across the year.

**There is no season in it at all.** That is not what a skipped station looks like; it is what a station whose air has arrived already emptied looks like.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the altitude does

To reach 3506 m the air has climbed a long way. Air cools as it rises, and water vapour condenses and falls out on the way up - so by the time air is at Leh's height it has already given up most of what it was carrying.

**Leh is behind the mountains, but the mountains are not the reason it is dry. The height is.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The same number shows up in the temperature column

And there is a second consequence in the table. Leh's January is -8.5 °C and February -7.2 °C. **It is the only station in all ten whose temperature drops below zero.**

Height explains both columns at once. If the cause were latitude alone, Leh would be cold and dry with no rain; if it were distance from the sea alone, it would be dry but still mild.
