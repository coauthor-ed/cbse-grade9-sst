## Rock Changes Where It Stands

Rock at the surface slowly breaks into smaller pieces where it stands. This is **weathering**, and it has three main types.

**Physical weathering** comes from temperature changes, frost or wind. In the Thar, rock heats by day and cools at night until it cracks. In Ladakh, frost pushes cracks wider.

Press **Day and night** until the boulder splits.

<!-- STEP -->
## Chemical Weathering

In **chemical weathering**, minerals in the rock react with water, air or acids and change into new substances. The rock is no longer quite the same material.

This is why old stone carvings go soft in the rain: sharp edges round off and fine lines fade.

Press **Rain** and watch the carving.

<!-- STEP -->
## Biological Weathering

**Biological weathering** is caused by plants, animals and very small living things. A peepal seed lands in a crack in an old wall. Its root grows into the crack, thickens, and pushes the stones apart.

Press **Grow** to see the root at work.

## Where Soil Begins

Weathering also helps to form soil. The top of the rock breaks into smaller and smaller grains, and these mix with rotted plants.
