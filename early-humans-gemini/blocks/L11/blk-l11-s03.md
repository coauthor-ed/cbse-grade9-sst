# Weighed Against a Feather

<!-- STEP id="ka" -->
## A Double That Lives On

The book says Egyptians believed each person had a ka, a spiritual double that lived on after death if the body was preserved. So keeping a body whole was a way of caring for the person who had died.

<!-- STEP id="balance-detail" -->
## A Test After Death

The heart stayed for a reason. Egyptians believed it held the record of a person's life, and that it would be needed in a test after death. The jackal-headed god Anubis checks the scale in this painting.

<!-- STEP id="scribe" -->
## The Scribe and the Beast

An ibis-headed god, Thoth, stands ready to write down the result. A beast with a crocodile's head waits nearby for any heart that fails.

<!-- STEP id="balance-sim" -->
## Weighed Against Truth

The heart went on one pan of the scale, against the feather of truth on the other. Egyptians believed a heart heavy with wrongdoing would sink. Try both hearts.

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## Why People Believed This

A heart that balanced with the feather let the person pass on to the afterlife. Every society has seen cruel people live well and good people suffer. This belief gave an answer: every life is judged in the end.

**The heart stayed because Egyptians believed it still had a test to pass.**
