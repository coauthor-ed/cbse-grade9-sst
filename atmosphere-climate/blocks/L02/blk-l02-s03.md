# Five Layers of Air

<!-- STEP -->
## It starts to rise

Above about 12 km the temperature stops falling. It stays cold for a few km, then it starts to rise. Falling all the way up is a fair guess, but the line turns back.

Changes in temperature and density define the layers of the air. The lowest is the **troposphere**, about 12 km high. It holds the air we breathe, most of the clouds, and nearly all the weather, such as rainfall, fog and hail.

<!-- STEP -->
## The stratosphere

Above a thin zone called the **tropopause** lies the **stratosphere**, which extends up to 50 km. Temperature rises through this layer.

It holds the ozone layer. The **stratopause** marks its top.

<!-- STEP -->
## The mesosphere

The **mesosphere** extends up to 80 km. Temperature falls again here, down to about -90 °C near the **mesopause**, the coldest air in the whole column.

Most meteors burn up in this layer.

<!-- STEP -->
## The thermosphere and exosphere

The **thermosphere** extends from 80 km to 700 km. It is much thicker than the layers below it, yet its air is very thin. Temperature rises very rapidly here.

Above it lies the **exosphere**, the uppermost layer.

**Five layers, and the temperature falls in only two of them.**
