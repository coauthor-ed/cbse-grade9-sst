# The ECI and Political Parties

<!-- STEP -->
## Who Can Contest?

Only political parties registered with the ECI can contest elections. Candidates who are not affiliated to any political party can also contest, and they are called **independent candidates**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Elections Inside a Party

The ECI ensures **inner party democracy**. It insists that political parties hold their organisational elections periodically, so a party's own posts are filled by an election at regular times.

<!-- STEP -->
## Three Classes of Party

The ECI classifies parties as recognised national parties, recognised state or regional parties, and registered-unrecognised parties.

National and state parties are allotted election symbols, and a **reserved symbol** is used by that party's candidates alone. Other candidates choose a free symbol from a list. A later lesson shows how a party is recognised.

<!-- STEP -->
## When There Is a Dispute

Sometimes there is a dispute about a party's recognition or about who gets a symbol. In such a dispute the ECI acts as a **quasi-judicial** body: it hears the matter and decides it, much as a court would.
