# Temples in Sumer and in India

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
## A Question From the Book

The book asks you to compare. Do you find any similarities between temples in India and those of the Sumerians, as centres of social and economic life?

Think of a large temple you know, or one you have read about. Keep it in mind for the next three points.

<!-- STEP id="economic" -->
## Work and Wealth

Many large Indian temples held land, grain and treasure, and gave work to many people: priests, cooks, musicians, guards and accountants. A ziggurat was tied to farming and trade in a similar way.

<!-- STEP id="access" -->
## Who May Enter

The top of a ziggurat was closed to all but high priests and priestesses. Many Indian temples welcomed crowds of devotees. But many also kept the inner room for priests, and many barred people of some castes for a long time.

**Who may enter a temple tells us about the society around it.**

<!-- STEP id="anchor" -->
## The Town Around the Temple

Both kinds of temple could shape a town, with markets, houses and roads growing around them. The book says the Sumerian city grew around its main temple.
