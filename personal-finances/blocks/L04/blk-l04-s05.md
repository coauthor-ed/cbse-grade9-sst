# Bonds

<!-- STEP -->
## A loan you give

When you buy a **bond**, you lend money to a government or a company for a fixed period. The borrower pays you interest regularly and returns the money on the maturity date.

Ravi buys a bond of ₹10,000 that pays 5% a year. After one year he gets ₹500 interest. If the bond matures then, he receives **₹10,500** in all.

<!-- STEP -->
## From 1 day to 30 years

Real bonds have maturity periods from **1 day to 30 years or more**. A bond can often be sold to someone else before maturity, which makes it more liquid. The price on the day of sale is not known in advance.

<!-- STEP -->
## Government and company bonds

**Government bonds** are generally safer than **corporate bonds** (bonds issued by companies). They suit people who want a steady income they can plan around.

A company can run short of money, so its bonds usually pay a higher rate for that extra risk.
