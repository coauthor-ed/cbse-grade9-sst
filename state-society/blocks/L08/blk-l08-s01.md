# Four Social Categories

<!-- STEP -->
## The earliest reference

The earliest reference to four social categories, understood by historians as **varṇa**, appears in the **Puruṣhasūkta**, a hymn in Book 10 of the **Ṛigveda**.

Over a period of time, these categories came to be associated with specific roles and tasks. Here are the roles as the texts describe them.

<!-- STEP -->
## Brāhmaṇas

The **brāhmaṇas** were mainly expected to study and teach the Vedas and other sacred texts, to perform and officiate at **yajñas**, and to give and receive **dāna** (gifts).

<!-- STEP -->
## Rajanya (Kṣhatriyas)

The **Rajanya** (Kṣhatriyas) were tasked with warfare, protecting people and administering justice. They were also expected to study the scriptures, sponsor yajñas, make donations and uphold social order.

<!-- STEP -->
## Vaiśhyas

The **Vaiśhyas** were associated with economic activities such as agriculture, pastoralism and trade. They also took part in scriptural study, sacrificial rituals and acts of charity.

<!-- STEP -->
## Śhūdras

The **Śhūdras** were expected to assist the other varṇas. In practice, they were also engaged in economic activities such as agriculture, animal husbandry, trade, and arts and crafts.

These are the roles the texts describe. A verse from the Ṛigveda gives us a different kind of evidence.
