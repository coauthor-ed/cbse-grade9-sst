# Income, Taste and Population

<!-- STEP -->
## Income

When a family's income rises, it can buy more, or buy better goods, at the same prices. So the demand for many goods usually rises. Usually, but not always: a family with more money may buy less of a plain, cheaper good and switch to a better one.

<!-- STEP -->
## Taste and preference

Every buyer likes some goods more than others. Srivalli likes mangoes. Even when oranges are cheaper than mangoes, she does not buy oranges in their place. A buyer's tastes and preferences shape what they demand.

<!-- STEP -->
## Population

Demand also depends on how many people live in a country, and who they are. India has more people than any other country, so its people buy a great deal. More children means more demand for sports shoes. More working adults means more demand for formal shoes, and more elderly people means more demand for comfortable shoes.
