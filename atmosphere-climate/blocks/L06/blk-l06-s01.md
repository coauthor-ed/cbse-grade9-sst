# It Is Not One Day

<!-- STEP -->
## A line on a map, and a dial that turns the year

The map is the subcontinent. The line drawn across it is the leading edge of the summer monsoon - the boundary between the air that is already raining and the air that is not.

There is a dial underneath the map. Turn it, and the line moves.

Nothing on this slide carries a date. Not the line, not the dial. **Watch where the line goes first, and hold on to the question of how long the crossing takes.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Turn it, and watch the walk

Drag the dial slowly from one end of the year towards the other.

The line comes up off the sea south-east of the mainland, over the islands. It touches the southern tip first. A little later it is over the west coast - Mumbai, the Konkan - and over the eastern shore at the same time, Kolkata and Odisha. Then it is inland, over the plateau and across the central plain. Then over Delhi, over Punjab, over Himachal Pradesh.

**And last of all, the far north-west: western Rajasthan, the Thar.**

Every one of those is a place people live in. Nobody picked them for the map. They are on it because that is the order the line arrives in.

<!-- STEP -->
## The belief this map is about to break

Here is what most people walk in with.

One wind. One season. It arrives, it rains, and every part of the country is inside it on the same day.

That is not a stupid belief. A monsoon *is* one wind and it *is* one season. Last lesson, a coastline reversed twice a day, and from a distance it looked like a thing that happened all at once to a whole shore. Extend that to a whole country and the belief makes complete sense.

The map says otherwise, and it will keep saying so for as long as you turn the dial.

<!-- STEP -->
## So the question the dial is really asking

The line is never in two places at once. Kerala has it while Delhi does not; Delhi has it while the Thar does not.

And the dial that moves the line is sitting right there under the map.

What is not yet on the slide is **how much time sits between those two positions** - whether a place gets the monsoon a day after its neighbour or a month after. The dial has no numbers on it, and that is the next question.
