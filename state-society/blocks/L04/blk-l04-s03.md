## The council, and the whole state

The third answer is right. An inscription shows a council of ministers taking decisions in the emperor's absence. We will come to it on the next slide.

The army and the treasury are real parts of a state, but the inscription refers to decisions taken by the council. To see all the parts, Kauṭilya listed seven constituents, the **Saptāṁga**.

<!-- STEP -->
## The first three

- **swāmi**: the king
- **amātya**: councillors, ministers and other high officials
- **janapada**: the territory and the people living in it

You met janapada in Lesson 2.

<!-- STEP -->
## The other four

- **durga**: fortified towns and cities
- **koṣha**: the treasury or wealth of the kingdom
- **daṇḍa**: the forces of defence and law and order
- **mitra**: the allies

<!-- STEP -->
## Seven parts, one body

Tap any limb to light it. The state was seen as an organic whole, like a body.

**The state can function well only if these seven remain properly integrated.**
