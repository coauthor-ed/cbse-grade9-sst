# Five Crafts You Can Follow

<!-- STEP id="pottery" -->
## Pottery in Regional Styles

Pottery was one of the main crafts, and it helped the economy of these settlements. From about 4000 BCE the styles of different regions become easier to see, because each region had its own shapes and painted designs.

<!-- STEP id="beads" -->
## Beads of Semi-Precious Stone

Making stone beads is slow, skilled work. The ground shows that Early Harappan people did it at several places: Harappa, Kunal in Haryana and Datrana in Gujarat.

These are the same kind of beads that the later cities are famous for.

<!-- STEP id="bangles" -->
## Shell Bangles and Copper Tools

Shell work and copper work also improved step by step. Shell comes from the sea, so shell bangles found far inland probably mean that people were carrying goods over long distances.

Copper tools helped people produce more, in farming and in crafts.

<!-- STEP id="graffiti" -->
## Marks on Pots

Potters at Harappa, Rehman Dheri and Kunal scratched marks on their pots. There are many such marks, and the book calls them graffiti.

The book says they could be seen as forerunners of the script, which means they may have come before it and led to it. Since nobody can read the Harappan script, this is a careful guess.

<!-- STEP id="seal" -->
## The First Seals

A few seals from these same sites carry geometric patterns and simple animals. They could also be seen as forerunners, this time of the famous inscribed seals of the cities.

<!-- STEP id="continuity" -->
## No Sudden Start

Now follow each craft from the Early Harappan stage into the Mature stage, on the rows here. You will not find a sudden break in any of them.

The book says it was these improvements in making things, together with regions trading and working with each other, that led to the cities.

**The cities grew out of older crafts and older trade.**
