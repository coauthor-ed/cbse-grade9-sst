# Why do we have summer and winter?

<!-- STEP -->
## Nearest the Sun in January

The Earth's path takes it about 15 crore km from the Sun, but not always the same distance. We are nearest around 3 January and farthest around 4 July.

So if distance made summer, Delhi would be warmest in January. Delhi's January is its coldest month, so distance cannot be the cause.

<!-- STEP -->
## June: leaning towards the Sun

The Earth's axis is tilted. In June the northern half leans towards the Sun, so the noon Sun stands high over India and the days are long. That brings our summer.

<!-- STEP -->
## December: leaning away

In December the northern half leans away. The noon Sun stands low, its light spreads thinner and the days are short, which brings our winter.

**Seasons come from the Earth's tilt, not from its distance from the Sun.** This is the slant from the Arctic again, now changing through the year.
