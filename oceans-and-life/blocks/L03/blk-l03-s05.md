## High tide helps ships

High tide raises the water near the shore, so ships can enter harbours more easily. A ship's **draught** is how deep its hull sits in the water, and it can cross only where the water is deeper than that. Move the tide and watch the two numbers on this made-up ship.

## Check before berthing

Tidal ranges in some ports reach 4 to 5 m, and the Gulf of Khambhat has one of the highest, about 12 m. Before a ship berths, it must check both the water depth and the tidal range, especially for low tide.

## Waiting, and tidal currents

Some channels, such as the Singapore Strait, have shallow areas. Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) often wait there for hours, and cross only at high tide, so that they do not run aground.

Large volumes of water flow in and out as well. These **tidal currents** can reach 5 to 6 knots, about 9 to 11 km/h.
