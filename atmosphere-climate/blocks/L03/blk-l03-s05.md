# How water comes back down

<!-- STEP -->
## Air rises and cools

Air that rises expands and cools, and cooler air can hold less vapour. At some height the air is full, or **saturated**. The extra vapour then condenses into tiny drops, and we see a cloud.

<!-- STEP -->
## Five kinds of precipitation

When the drops grow heavy, gravity pulls them down. This falling water is **precipitation**: drizzle, rain, snow, sleet and hail. Tap each one.

Rain is the most common kind. It lowers the temperature of a place, and most groundwater comes from it. A long absence of rainfall gives a dry climate.

<!-- STEP -->
## What decides where it falls

Three main factors decide how much precipitation a place gets:

- **Prevailing winds**: the wind that usually blows there may or may not carry vapour.
- **Mountains**: they force air to rise and cool.
- **Seasons**: some times of year are wetter than others.
