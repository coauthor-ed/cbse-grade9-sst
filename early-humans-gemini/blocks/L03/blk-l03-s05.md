# The Age of Big Tools

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## The Shape Is a Clue

The best reading is the first answer. A heavy lump with a rough edge chops, and a pointed tip digs. A thin steep edge scrapes, and a broad straight edge cleaves, which means splits.

You read the shapes just as archaeologists do. This first part of the Palaeolithic, the age of large cutting tools, is called the **Lower Palaeolithic**.

<!-- STEP id="material" -->
## Quartzite and Limestone

At Attirampakkam these tools were made of quartzite, and at Isampur of limestone. The pear-shaped handaxes are worked on both faces, until the two faces meet in a cutting edge.

<!-- STEP id="meal" -->
## Four Jobs

Your textbook lists what these tools were used for:

- chopping animal meat
- digging out tubers
- scraping animal skin
- cleaving animal bones to get at the protein-rich marrow inside

<!-- STEP id="layer" -->
## What the Layer Shows

At these sites, animal fossils lie in the same layers as the tools. So the toolmakers and those animals belong to the same time and place.

The layer cannot show which tool did which job. That part comes from the shape of each tool and from experiments with copies, as you saw in Lesson 1.

**The list of jobs is firm, and matching each job to a tool is the best reading.**
