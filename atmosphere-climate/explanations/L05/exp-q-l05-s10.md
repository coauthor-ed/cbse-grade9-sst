# Why the Missing Dates Are a Puzzle

<!-- STEP -->
## What has actually been established

Three facts, all from this lesson:

- The landmass and the seas heat and cool at different rates.
- That difference makes the wind reverse, and it reverses on a schedule rather than at random.
- The summer half of the cycle blows from the sea onto the land, across the Indian Ocean, the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal, between June and September.

The mechanism explains **why** the wind reverses. It does not, by itself, say **where the rain begins**, **on what date**, or **how it moves across the country**.

## Why that gap is odd rather than natural

Here is the argument in four steps.

- The wind starts in the south-west, in June.
- The same wind is withdrawing again by October.
- So between those two dates it travelled - it went somewhere.
- **Anything that travels has a starting point, a direction, and a schedule.**

Nothing about the mechanism forbids those three things from being known. They are simply not in this lesson. A mechanism that explains the reversal and is silent about the route has told you *what* happens and left *when* and *where* untouched.

## What a single date for the whole country would contradict

Suppose the answer were "the summer rain begins on one day everywhere".

Then a farmer in the far south and a farmer on the northern plains would face the same season on the same day, and the difference in their rainfall totals would have to be explained by what the air did *after* it arrived. That is possible in principle - but it would mean the rain reaches the far end of the country already weakened, purely by distance and mixing.

Now suppose instead that the rain reaches one end several weeks before the other. Then a single wind is arriving **progressively**: it starts in one place and crosses to the other. Both accounts fit the mechanism this lesson taught. **The mechanism does not choose between them.**

That is what makes the gap a puzzle. The next lesson is about which of those two things actually happens - and its answer is the second one.
