# Delimitation

## The Hill Voter Has More Weight

The hill voter's vote carries more weight. Each voter has one vote, but the hill voter is one of 5 lakh people choosing an MP, and the city voter is one of 25 lakh.

So one vote in the hill seat counts for five times as much in choosing an MP. This defeats the aim of **equal representation**.

One vote each is equal in number. Equal weight needs constituencies of about the same size.

<!-- STEP -->
## Delimitation

**Delimitation** is the process of determining the number of seats and fixing the territorial boundaries in each State for elections to the Lok Sabha and the Vidhan Sabha.

Its aim is that the ratio of seats to population should be as close to equal as possible across all constituencies. Here the line has moved, and each MP now represents **15 lakh** people.

<!-- STEP -->
## Boundaries Change

Boundaries are not permanently fixed. They change over time as population density shifts, so the work has to be done again.

**Article 82** of the Constitution of India mandates a Delimitation Commission. India has had four, in 1952, 1963, 1973 and 2002.
