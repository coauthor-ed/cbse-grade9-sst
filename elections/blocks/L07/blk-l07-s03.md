# Any One Condition

<!-- STEP -->
## Party Q Can Be National

Yes, Party Q can be recognised as a national party, because the ECI's list of conditions has this line at the top:

**Any one condition should be fulfilled.**

A party does not need all of them. One is enough.

<!-- STEP -->
## National Party: Three Routes

- **Route 1:** not less than 6% of valid votes in four or more States, and four Lok Sabha seats.
- **Route 2:** at least 2% of Lok Sabha seats, with candidates elected from at least three States.
- **Route 3:** recognised as a State party in at least four States.

2% of 543 seats is about 11. Party Q won 12, from four States, so **Route 2** carries it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Now Check Party P

Party P has more votes, but look at each route.

- Route 1 needs four States. It has one.
- Route 2 needs about 11 seats. It has 3.
- Route 3 needs State party recognition in four States. It has one State.

**The ECI's routes look at where votes and seats come from, and not only at how many there are.**

<!-- STEP -->
## State Party: Five Routes

Any one of these, in that State:

- 6% of its Assembly votes, and two Assembly seats
- 6% of its Lok Sabha votes, and one Lok Sabha seat from it
- 3% of its Assembly seats, or three seats, whichever is more
- one Lok Sabha seat for every 25 seats, or fraction of 25, allotted to it
- 8% of the valid votes polled in the State

Party P polled 40% of the valid votes in its one State. That passes the last route, 8%. **Party P is a State party.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Registered Unrecognised Parties

**Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPP)** are of two kinds:

- parties that have not secured enough percentage of votes in the Assembly or General Elections
- parties that have never contested elections since their registration

Their candidates can still contest. They are not allotted a reserved symbol, so they choose a free symbol from a list.
