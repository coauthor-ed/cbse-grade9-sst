# What the Evidence Suggests

<!-- STEP -->
## Occupations were flexible

The first answer is right. The verse shows three occupations in one family, and it is often used as evidence that occupations were flexible.

Early Vedic texts do not indicate any fixed social status strictly based on birth. Historians generally agree that social identity was shaped by several factors, including region, village, language, **gotra** (a kinship-based subgroup) and especially occupation.

<!-- STEP -->
## A system of values

The concept of varṇa was based on a system of values. In it, **knowledge** was given the highest status, followed by **political power** and **wealth**.

For this reason, historians describe the division of the four varṇas as a **functional** one, which means it was based on the work a group did.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Buddhist text

The Buddhist text **Sutta Nipāta** gives an example of this idea. It states:

"No brāhmaṇa is such by birth. No outcaste is such by birth but only by his deeds. A brāhmaṇa is such by his deeds."
