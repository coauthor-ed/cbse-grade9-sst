## Reading the Basin, Feature by Feature

The survey lists five things. Four of them are the wind's, one is not, and telling them apart is the entire question.

### 1. The streamlined rock ridges

Low ridges lying **parallel to the prevailing wind**, with troughs between them.

The alignment is the evidence. A shape built by a wind runs along the direction that wind came from, because the wind cuts hardest across its own path and leaves material standing along it.

The ridges exist because the softer rock between them was removed - abraded by the sand the wind was carrying.

**Wind's work: material removed, leaving rock standing.**

### 2. The shallow bowl with water in it

A broad bowl in the flat floor, lowest point carrying **water and palms**.

The wind lifted loose material off the floor until it reached the water table, and stopped there because damp ground cannot be lifted.

The water itself was in the ground all along. The wind removed the cover above it and nothing more.

**Wind's work: material removed. Not the wind's work: the water.**

### 3. The dark stony surface

Fine material swept away, coarse material left behind and darkened in the sun.

This is a place where the *finest* grains went and the pebbles stayed - the reverse of the bowl, where everything loose went.

**Wind's work: material removed, leaving a surface that is harder and flatter than the one it found.**

### 4. The crescent mound

A barchan, standing alone with bare sand around it and its horns pointing downwind.

Isolated crescents form where the sand supply is **limited**. Where sand keeps arriving there would be a long ridge running across the wind instead, not a single crescent.

**Wind's work: material put down. Its shape says the supply is small.**

### 5. The dust off the field

A wall of dust lifting from ground with **thin vegetation cover**.

Dust needs loose, dry, unbound soil to lift. Thin vegetation cover is a drought and grazing condition - the ground got bare before the wind ever arrived.

**Not the wind's work: the bare ground. The wind's work: lifting it.**

## Putting them together

Four features are the wind's, split by what it did to the material:

- **Removed** - the rock ridges (survivors), the bowl (a hollow), the stony surface (fines gone).
- **Put down** - the crescent mound, standing above the flat ground around it.

Two things in the survey are not the wind's, and both are the same kind of answer: **something was there first**, and the wind's contribution was only to reach it.

- The water was in the ground before the hollow was cut. The wind cleared the cover off it.
- The bare field was bare before the dust lifted. The wind carried what was already loose.

**The wind is the chapter's carrier for what is loose and exposed. It cannot supply water, and it cannot create the bare ground that its finest job depends on.**
