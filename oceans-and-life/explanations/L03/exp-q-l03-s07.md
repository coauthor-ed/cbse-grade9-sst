# From the pull to the port

**Twice a day.** The Moon pulls the near water most, the centre of the Earth less and the far water least, so the sea is stretched into two bulges. The Earth turns under them, and most coasts meet two high tides and two low tides in a day.

**Bigger and smaller.** The Sun pulls too. On full moon and new moon days the Sun, the Earth and the Moon lie in a straight line and the pulls combine, so high tides are unusually high and low tides unusually low: a **spring tide**. At the first and third quarter the pulls act in different directions and the difference becomes smaller: a **neap tide**.

**Who needs to know.** A ship can cross only where the water is deeper than its draught, so a large ship may wait for high tide. Many fish move closer to the shore at high tide for food and shelter, which gives fishers a better catch.
