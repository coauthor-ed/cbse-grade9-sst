## Coastal architects

Waves are often called **coastal architects**, because they shape the shoreline. A **headland** is high land that juts out into the sea.

Breaking waves find small cracks in its rock. **Hydraulic action**, the force of moving water, loosens and removes the rock and soil, and a crack opens up.

<!-- STEP -->
## Cave, then arch

Rock and sand carried by the waves now grind the cliff. This grinding is called **abrasion**. Hydraulic action and abrasion together turn the crack into a **sea cave**, which gets larger.

In time the back of the cave wears through, and a sea **arch** is formed.

<!-- STEP -->
## Stack, then stump

The top of the arch falls by gravity and leaves a tall **sea stack**. Over time the stack is eroded and weathered into a **stump**. The dashed line shows where the original headland stood.

Sometimes the sea also breaks a hole up through the roof of a cave: a **blow hole**.
