# Built With What the River Left

## Dams and Canals

The Sumerians were the first to build a system of dams and canals for irrigation. Irrigation means bringing water to crops by human work. Rain could not be relied on, so they led river water to their fields.

An army, a temple and a store of grain all mattered, and Sumerian cities had them later. But none of them puts water on a field.

<!-- STEP id="brick" -->
## Bricks From River Clay

The river was the danger, and it was also the building material. The Sumerians were the first to use mud bricks and burnt bricks for houses and walls, and clay and mud lay all along the river.

**They built with what the river left.**

<!-- STEP id="citystate" -->
## From Village to City-State

Over about 2,000 years, some large cities developed ways of governing. They became city-states. A city-state is a sovereign state centred on a city that rules the land around it, and sovereign means it answers to no higher ruler.

The city needed the food from the land around it, and that land depended on canals that had to be kept in repair.

Ur and several other cities stood in Sumer, in present-day southern Iraq. Each one grew around its main temple, with farmland and canals around it.
