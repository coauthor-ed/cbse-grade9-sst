# Four Stages of One Life

<!-- STEP -->
## The student

Texts of this period divide a person's life into four stages. Each stage is called an **āśhrama**.

The first is **Brahmacharya**, the stage of studentship. The person is young and learns from a teacher.

<!-- STEP -->
## The householder

The second stage is **Gṛihastha**, the stage of the householder. Now the person has a home to run and a family to look after.

<!-- STEP -->
## Life in the forest

The third stage is **Vānaprastha**, life in the forest. An older person moves away from the busy life of the household.

<!-- STEP -->
## Renunciation

The fourth stage is **Saṁnyāsa**, renunciation, which means giving up home, belongings and worldly ties by choice.

The last two stages both turn away from household life. Later in this lesson we meet people who lived this way.

<!-- STEP -->
## Duties and sixteen rites

Everyone was expected to perform the duties of their varṇa and their stage of life. In principle, this gave a person fulfilment in social, moral and spiritual life, and it would lead to harmony and balance in society.

Note the words "in principle": this is a plan set out in texts, and it does not tell us how each person really lived.

Rituals and ceremonies went with every stage, particularly the **ṣhoḍaśha saṁskāras**, the 'sixteen rites of passage'. A rite of passage is a ceremony that marks a change in a person's life.
