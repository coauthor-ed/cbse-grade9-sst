# The Exception, Not the Rule

<!-- STEP id="intro" -->
## Rights at Home, Not at the Top

Egyptian women could own property and run businesses. The very top of the same society tells a different story.

<!-- STEP id="rare" -->
## Very Few Women Pharaohs

In about 3,000 years, only a handful of pharaohs were women. Hatshepsut ruled around 1479 to 1458 BCE, and Cleopatra more than a thousand years later.

<!-- STEP id="beard" -->
## A Ruler Had to Look Like a King

Hatshepsut ruled as pharaoh, not as a queen beside a king. Official statues show her with the false ceremonial beard and royal signs of a male pharaoh. The pictures of kingship had been made for men, so she used them too.

<!-- STEP id="whytop" -->
## Two Different Kinds of Power

Owning property was a legal right that many women had. The throne was different, probably because it was tied to command of the army and to a royal line of male kings. Much later, Cleopatra is shown as a queen.

**Hatshepsut had to be shown as a king.**
