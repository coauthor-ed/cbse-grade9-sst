## The sea floor jolts

People sometimes say "tidal wave", but a tsunami has nothing to do with tides. Most tsunamis start with an undersea earthquake. The sea floor jolts up and lifts all the water above it, from the bottom to the surface, and that lifted water spreads out as waves.

<!-- STEP -->
## Fast and low in the deep sea

You might picture a tall wall of water far out at sea, but the deep-sea wave is usually under about 1 metre high. It moves at about 700 to 800 km/h, as fast as a jet plane, and a ship above it hardly notices.

<!-- STEP -->
## Slower and higher near the shore

In shallow water the wave slows down, and the water behind it piles up, so it grows much taller. A tsunami comes as a series of waves, so the first one is not the end.
