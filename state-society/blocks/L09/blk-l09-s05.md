# Women in the Sources

<!-- STEP -->
## Vedic period: learning and public life

The Vedic period is often described as a time when women held a high and respectful position in society. Women took part in scholarly learning and, in certain contexts, performed rituals alongside men.

Texts also mention them in chariot races and at social gatherings, including the **sabhā**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Vedic period: hymns and goddesses

Several hymns of the Ṛigveda are traditionally attributed to women sages, most notably **Apālā**, **Viśhvavārā**, **Ghoṣhā** and **Lopāmudrā**.

Goddesses such as **Uṣha**, the goddess of dawn, and **Aditi**, the mother of the gods, held revered positions in Vedic culture.

<!-- STEP -->
## Post-Maurya period: gifts on record

A **votive inscription** is a text on stone, metal or other material that dedicates a gift to a deity or sacred place.

Women appear as donors on several votive inscriptions from the post-Maurya period, so these records name women as givers.

<!-- STEP -->
## Sangam literature: work and poetry

Sangam literature shows women as active participants in society and the economy. They planted, weeded, husked and winnowed paddy, and they reared cattle, spun, fished, made salt and sold garlands.

Sangam texts also mention the celebrated poetesses **Avvaiyar** and **Vennikuyattiyar**, a woman potter.

<!-- STEP -->
## Gupta-Vākāṭaka period: a regent

Literary works of the 4th to 6th centuries CE show female characters who are highly educated and skilled in the arts.

Records of real people add more. **Prabhāvatī Gupta**, daughter of the Gupta ruler Chandragupta II, ruled as a regent in the Vākāṭaka kingdom and issued land grants in her own name.

<!-- STEP -->
## Chola period: temples

Later, Chola inscriptions record the activities of royal women such as **Sembiyan Mahādevī**, who supported temple building and religious institutions.

Each card here is one piece of evidence from one time and place. Together they run over many centuries, so we should be careful before making a single claim about all of them.
