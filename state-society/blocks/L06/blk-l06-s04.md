# The Imperial Cholas

<!-- STEP -->
## Four levels

You met the early Cholas and King Karikala in earlier grades. The **imperial Cholas** came later, in the 9th to 11th centuries CE, and they were noted for efficient administrative and revenue systems.

Their empire had four levels:

- **manḍalams**, the provinces
- **valanāḍus**, the districts
- **nāḍus**, the groups of villages
- **Urs**, the individual villages

<!-- STEP -->
## The same words, one step lower

Look again at **valanāḍu** and **nāḍu**. In the general list for 300 to 800 CE in Lesson 5, a valanāḍu is a division and a nāḍu is a district. In the Chola list, a valanāḍu is a district and a nāḍu is a group of villages.

The words are used one step differently, so read each term together with its period.

## What an assembly did

Village councils or assemblies settled disputes and managed local affairs. They were responsible for all local administrative functions:

- revenue collection
- land management
- irrigation
- road construction
- record maintenance

**Village assemblies were independent, self-reliant institutions, and they did not depend on the government for patronage.**

Patronage here means support and payment from a ruler.
