# ₹30,000 at 8% for Five Years

<!-- STEP -->
## The quick method

A family keeps ₹30,000 in a bank deposit at 8% a year for five years.

A quick method takes 8% of ₹30,000, which is ₹2,400, and adds it five times. That gives ₹42,000. On the picture this is the flat bar: it adds the same ₹2,400 every year.

<!-- STEP -->
## 8% of the new amount

After one year the account holds ₹32,400. In the second year, 8% is worked out on ₹32,400, so it adds ₹2,592, not ₹2,400.

Year by year the account reads ₹32,400, ₹34,992, ₹37,791.36, ₹40,814.67 and **₹44,079.84**. The formula $A = P(1 + r)^n$ gives the same answer: P = ₹30,000, r = 0.08 and n = 5.

<!-- STEP -->
## The difference

The real interest is ₹44,079.84 - ₹30,000 = **₹14,079.84**. The quick method gave ₹12,000, so it missed ₹2,079.84.

That ₹2,079.84 is interest earned on interest. Over five years it is small; over forty years, as for the cousins, it is most of the money.
