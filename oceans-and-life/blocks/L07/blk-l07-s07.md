## The Blue Economy

The World Bank defines the **Blue Economy** as "the sustainable use of ocean resources to promote economic growth, improved livelihoods, and jobs, while preserving the health of the marine ecosystem".

Sustainable use means using a resource so that enough is left for later. So the idea has three parts that must go together: economic growth, ecosystem health, and improved livelihoods and jobs.

## A made-up choice: close the season

This policy card is a made-up example. Fishing stops for three months so that the fish can breed. Ecosystem health rises, but livelihoods fall for those months because the boats stay in harbour.

<!-- STEP -->
## A made-up choice: more boats

This card is made up too. More boats go out, so growth and jobs rise for now. Ecosystem health falls because fish are taken faster than they breed, and later the jobs fall with it.

A choice that helps one part can harm another, so a country has to weigh all three.

## Seven aims

The UN's Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 14, Life Below Water, carries this idea forward. Its key aims are to:

- reduce marine pollution
- protect marine ecosystems
- address ocean acidification
- regulate fishing
- conserve marine areas
- eliminate harmful subsidies
- increase economic benefits
