## Water Runs Faster on the Outside

You might expect a river to wear both banks equally. It does not. In a bend, the water on the outside moves fastest, and the water on the inside moves slowly.

<!-- STEP -->
## One Bank Cut, One Bank Built

The fast water erodes the outer bank and leaves a **steep bank**. The slow water deposits sediment on the inner bank and builds a **bar**.

Drag **Years** and watch the bends swing out into large loops.

<!-- STEP -->
## From Loop to Oxbow Lake

As a loop grows, its neck of land gets narrow. In a flood the river cuts across the neck and takes the short way. The old loop is left behind as a curved **oxbow lake**.

Drag **Years** to the end.

## Life on a Meander

Fertile soil along the banks makes these areas good for farming, and villages and towns often grow on the gentle slopes nearby. Meandering rivers are also used for navigation, irrigation and tourism. The **Grand Anicut**, or **Kallanai**, on the Kaveri in Tamil Nadu is an old example of using a river for irrigation.
