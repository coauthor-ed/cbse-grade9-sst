# Out of Africa

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## The First to Leave

It was *Homo erectus*, not *Homo sapiens*. It left Africa around 2 million years ago, long before *Homo sapiens* existed.

<!-- STEP id="spread" -->
## Into Asia and Europe

Between about 2 million and 0.5 million years ago, *Homo erectus* spread out of Africa into Asia and Europe. The routes on this map are possible routes, because nobody recorded the real ones.

<!-- STEP id="evidence" -->
## The Evidence Is in the Stone

So how do we know? Nobody wrote an account of this journey, but handaxes and cleavers have been found in other parts of Asia and in Europe. They point to tools that began in Africa and spread beyond it.

**The spread of the tools points to the spread of the people who made them.**

<!-- STEP id="corridor" -->
## A Wetter Time

People crossing a land need water and food. During the warmer times called interglacials, the Sahara and Arabia were wetter than they are now. So the way out of Africa was probably easier then.

<!-- STEP id="secondwave" -->
## The Second Wave

Another major movement out of Africa came around 125,000 years ago. This time it was early *Homo sapiens*, our own species, which had evolved in Africa about 300,000 years ago.

<!-- STEP id="waypoints" -->
## Places on the Map

- Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania, where *Homo habilis* lived.
- The Nile Valley and the Levant, the way north out of Africa.
- Hathnora in India, in the valley of the Narmada.
- Denisova Cave in Siberia and north China, far to the east.
- Australia, which *Homo sapiens* reached much later.
