# Sandals, Board Games and a Queen at Eighteen

<!-- STEP id="jar" -->
## More Than Death and Stone

Egypt was not only tombs. A jar painted with marsh birds and fish shows people who looked closely at the living world around them.

<!-- STEP id="sandals" -->
## Reeds From the River

Sandals like these were woven from river reeds. Early Egyptians also enjoyed leisure: they swam, went out in canoes, played board games, and enjoyed music and dancing.

<!-- STEP id="box" -->
## Who Used These Things?

Look at this woven box for keeping oils and eye paint. Which social class do you think used it? Woven reed is cheap, so it could belong to many homes. It tells us little about wealth unless we find finer materials with it.

<!-- STEP id="sistrum" -->
## Music and Great Festivals

Festivals were central to Egyptian life. Each god had a festival in which a statue was paraded with music, played on instruments such as this rattle, the sistrum. The Sed festival marked a king's 30th year on the throne.

<!-- STEP id="cleopatra" -->
## Women and Power

Egyptian women in general had more rights than Greek or Roman women, because they could own property and run businesses. Cleopatra (69-30 BCE) lived more than 2,000 years after the first pyramids. She was trained from childhood to rule, and she became queen at the age of eighteen.
