# How Slow Rock Moves Plates

<!-- STEP -->
## A pot of dal

Watch a pot of dal on the stove. Dal at the bottom heats up and rises, cooler dal at the top sinks, and the loop keeps turning. A loop like this is a **convection current**.

<!-- STEP -->
## The mantle does the same, very slowly

Heat from the core does the same thing to the mantle. Hot rock rises very slowly, and cooler rock sinks.

Unlike dal, this rock is mostly solid, so the loop is far slower. It moves only a few centimetres a year.

<!-- STEP -->
## Currents push and pull the plates

These convection currents push and pull the plates, so the plates move in different directions. Where rock rises and spreads, plates are pulled apart. Where rock sinks, plates are drawn together.

Scientists now think a plate's own heavy sinking edge also pulls it along. The chapter gives convection currents as the main cause.
