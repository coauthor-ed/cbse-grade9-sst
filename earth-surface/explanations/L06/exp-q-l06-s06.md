## Find the Headland in the Sequence

The arch and the stack are not two separate events on this coast. They are **two stages of one sequence**, and the sequence is worth reading in order.

Headland. Cave. Arch. Stack. Stump.

Each stage is what is left after the sea has removed something, and by the time the coast reaches the stack stage it has removed a great deal.

<!-- STEP -->
## Ask What the Stack Has Lost

Compare the headland with the stack, and the difference is not height or size. It is **what is behind them**.

- A **headland** is part of a coast. It takes the sea on one face and has solid land behind it.
- A **stack** is alone. The rock that joined it to the shore is gone, so the sea reaches every face of it at once.

**A stack is the most exposed piece of coastline that a coast can have.** It is not protected by its own size; its size is exactly what gives the waves a long time to work on it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Then Ask What Is In Front of the Town

The town stands back from the headland, so nothing is going to fall on it.

But the sequence is still running. The stack is standing now; it will be a stump, and then it will be gone. Every stage of the coast's retreat takes the town's protection away a little further, and when the stack finally goes there is nothing at all between the open sea and the shore behind it.

**The town's risk is not collapse. It is the loss of the coast that was standing in front of it.**

## And Why the Tide Band Still Matters

One last piece, because it looks like an objection and is not.

You are right that the sea cannot cut high above the water. That is exactly why cliffs are attacked at one narrow height - but "the town is above that height" answers a question nobody asked. The question is not what happens to the ground the town stands on. It is what happens to the ground **in front of it**.

**A coast protects what lies behind it, and that protection is finite. The chapter's own line about beaches acting as barriers against strong waves is the same point in the opposite direction.**
