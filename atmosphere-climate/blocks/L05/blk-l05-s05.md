# Taking the Name Apart

<!-- STEP -->
## A borrowed word, and nobody is born knowing it

Say this once here and let it go for the chapter: **the hard names here are borrowed, and nobody arrives already holding them.**

*Aurora* is Latin, and it means *dawn*. *Troposphere* is a Greek compound that means *the turning sphere*. And *monsoon* is an Arabic word for a season, carried here by traders who had a practical reason to want it.

None of them is hard because the thing it names is hard. They are hard because they came from somewhere else, in a ship or a book, and no family has always known them.

The scene breaks the word into its pieces and their plain meanings. Take the piece, work out what the whole word must be pointing at, and watch the name assemble.

## Then the way round that matters

The round worth doing is the reverse one. If somebody describes the thing to you, can you get its word?

- The wind that blows from the sea towards the land between June and September
- The wind that blows from the land out across the sea between October and February
- The name for the **season** in which both of those happen, and not for either wind

The third one is the trap. **The name belongs to the cycle, not to the wind** - and a learner who has it the other way round has every fact and none of the structure.

**A name you can build from its pieces is a name you cannot get wrong.**
