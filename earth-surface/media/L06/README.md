## rocher-perce-arch-and-stack.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L06/blk-l06-s06.json` (`image.src`).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.15` (p.14 / book p.26), **right-hand
  panel only**. The textbook's printed photograph is **not** to be
  extracted.
- To produce: download a photograph of **Rocher Perce (Perce Rock)**, the
  limestone promontory on the Gaspe Peninsula, Quebec, Canada, from
  Wikimedia Commons or another open-licence repository. Save it here and
  record below: source page URL, file name on the source, author or
  credited institution if any, and the exact licence (Commons photos are
  usually CC BY-SA or CC0 - check the file page, do not assume).
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.15. Three details are
  load-bearing, and every one of them is a claim the prose makes:
  1. **The arch cut clean through the rock body**, with open sea visible
     through it. This is the stage the predict on s02 asked about and the
     verdict on s03 named, so the photograph has to show the *tunnel*,
     not a cave in a face.
  2. **The detached sea stack standing just off its seaward tip** - a
     separate pillar of the same rock, visibly *not joined* to anything.
     The gap of water between arch and stack is what makes the sequence
     legible in one frame.
  3. **Real scale.** The slide's whole argument is that a stack is not a
     diagram-sized object but a building-sized piece of coastline. Pick a
     wide shot from the water, not a close-up of the arch: the frame must
     show the whole promontory from base to top with sky or sea around
     it, so the viewer can read the height. A tight crop of the arch
     defeats the slide.
- A caption identifying the location, if the photograph itself has no
  visible signage, is welcome in the file name and in the block's `alt`
  text (already written); no text should be overlaid onto the image.
