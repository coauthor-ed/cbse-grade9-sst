## manu-smriti-women-scroll.png

- Used by: `blocks/L09/blk-l09-s06.json` (`image.src`), shown from the first
  beat.
- Stands in for the source's *Manusmriti Verse on Women Scroll* (p.28 /
  book p.122), recorded in `FIGURES.md`, which prints Manu-Smriti 3.56 in
  transliteration with an English rendering. The textbook's own scroll
  graphic is **not** to be extracted or reproduced.
- To produce: generate original art from the prompt in
  `manu-smriti-women-scroll.prompt.txt`.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md`. Two details are load-bearing and
  every one of them is a claim the prose makes:
  1. **An unfurled scroll, both ends rolled, lying flat and seen from
     directly above.** The slide's whole point is that this is a *document*
     being looked at - a source, not a scene. A scroll held in two hands, or
     photographed on a desk at an angle, would make it an event instead.
  2. **Verse-marks that are present but not legible.** The verse is set in
     the slide's markdown in words, with its citation. If the image carries
     readable Devanagari it becomes a second, worse copy of the quotation -
     and an unfaithful one. Non-legible marks are correct here.
- The image must carry **no hand, no reader, no worshipper, no celebratory
  or marginalising imagery**. The slide contrasts a stated ideal against a
  changing practice, and art that signals honour on one side would settle an
  argument the slide exists to leave open.
- Licence: original work produced for this course; no third-party source to
  record beyond this file.
