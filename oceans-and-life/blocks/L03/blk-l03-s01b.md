## Why two tides, and not one?

You might think the Moon simply lifts the water on the side facing it. That would give one high tide a day, but most coasts get two.

The Moon pulls on the whole Earth, and it pulls harder on what is nearer. So the near water is pulled most, the centre of the Earth less, and the far water least.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two bulges

The near water is pulled ahead of the Earth, and the far water is left behind. So the sea is stretched both ways, into two **tidal bulges**: one towards the Moon and one away from it. In this drawing the bulges are far too large.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Earth turns under them

The bulges stay lined up with the Moon while the Earth turns. A place on the coast is carried through one bulge, then the other, once each in about a day. So it meets two high tides and two low tides.
