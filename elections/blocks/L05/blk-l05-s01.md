# Reading the Machine by Touch

<!-- STEP -->
## Braille Beside the Buttons

Raised **Braille** numbers sit beside each button. A voter who cannot see feels for the number, and presses the button on that row.

So no officer has to press the button, the machine does not read names to the room, and this voter does not have to vote by post.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two Units and a Cable

This is an **EVM**, an electronic voting machine, with two parts. A cable joins them. The polling officer keeps the **control unit**. The voter uses the **balloting unit**, which has one row for each candidate: a number, a name, a symbol and a button.

<!-- STEP -->
## Press a Button

Tap **Names off** to see only what a finger can find. A sheet in Braille at the booth lists the candidates by number, so the voter knows which number to look for.

Find a row and press its button. A lamp lights beside it and the machine gives a beep, which tells the voter that the vote is cast.

## Why the ECI Does This

The ECI has taken several steps to make elections more inclusive. These steps focus on Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), senior citizens, transgender persons and Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs).

Braille helps a voter who has reached the machine. **What about a voter who cannot reach the polling station at all?**
