## karst-cave-interior.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L09/blk-l09-s03.json` (`reveal.images`,
  key `cave-interior-photo`; `alt` text already written on the block).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.24` (p.19 / book p.31), **right-hand
  panel only**. The textbook's printed photograph is **not** to be
  extracted, and its left-hand labelled diagram is **not** to be
  extracted either - `CaveGrowScene` draws that, and the diagram's whole
  job is the labels the scene has to place itself.
- To produce: download a photograph of a **limestone cave interior with
  stalactites, stalagmites and at least one full-height pillar**, from
  Wikimedia Commons or another open-licence repository. Save it here and
  record below: source page URL, file name on the source, author or
  credited institution if any, and the exact licence (Commons photos are
  usually CC BY-SA or CC0 - check the file page, do not assume).
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.24's right panel. Four
  details are load-bearing, and every one of them is a claim the prose
  makes:
  1. **Both kinds of formation in one frame** - forms hanging from the
     roof and forms rising from the floor, in the same photograph. The
     slide's verdict is that these are the same mineral and the split is
     in the name; a frame showing only one of them cannot carry it.
  2. **At least one pillar**, where a hanging form and a rising form have
     joined into a single column from floor to roof. This is the third
     beat of the slide and the one landform a two-form photograph misses.
  3. **A person for scale** - the source's own figure is a caver in a
     bright headlamp standing on the cave floor. Some sense of how big
     the chamber is, without which every formation looks like a
     curtain ornament.
  4. **Enough of the ceiling and the floor to see where each formation
     attaches**, so a viewer can tell which end grows from which. A
     close crop of the formations alone loses the slide's actual
     question.
- It should read as a **cave that is genuinely underground** - the roof
  overhead, the floor underfoot, no daylight except a distant mouth if
  one happens to be in frame.
- **No people in any pose other than standing and looking**, no
  scaffolding, no lighting rig or stage visible, no signage overlaid, and
  no text on the image. A caption identifying the cave system is welcome
  in the file name and in the block's `alt` text (already written).

## Images this lesson does NOT need

- **`Fig. 2.25`'s two sinkhole panels** (the 3D block diagram pitted with
  funnels, and the reddish collapse amphitheatre with pinnacles). The
  block diagram is a diagram, and `SinkholeScene` draws a better one
  because it lets the learner hold the stages apart; the photograph is a
  collapse in a red soil that is not the limestone this lesson is about
  and would confuse the substrate the whole lesson rests on.
- **`Fig. 2.26`'s underground river photograph** - the cavern with the
  daylight entrance and the two silhouetted figures on the bank. This is
  the closest call in the lesson and it was **deliberately left out**:
  `OUTLINE.md`'s conventions list the figures that may be carried as
  images, and Fig. 2.26 is not among them, while Fig. 2.24 is. The load
  bearing detail it holds - that the cavern is far larger than a person -
  is carried on the scene instead, and `UndergroundRiverScene`'s
  `Must get right` requires the two figures to be drawn with the kit
  figure small enough to read as specks against the gallery. If a human
  decides the photograph is worth the extra dangling reference, it is a
  new entry here, not a substitute for anything above.
- **`Fig. 2.29`'s Kedarnath aerial photograph.** It is a real event and
  the conventions list permits it, and `FIGURES.md` names it on this
  lesson's closing hook - but the closing hook asks what happens when a
  roof gives out rather than teaching what a flood does, and the
  photograph would answer the next lesson's opening question and give away
  the chapter's hazard vocabulary inside this one. `L10 s05` is the slide
  that teaches the process and the slide that should carry the image.
