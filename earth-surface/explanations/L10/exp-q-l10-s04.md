## What the Dam Actually Was

This is the lesson's own GLOF scene, and the three things it shows are the three the question turns on.

**A terminal moraine is rubble.** A glacier that carried till along its two margins and dropped it as it retreated left a wall of loose rock and soil thrown right across the valley. That wall was the only thing between the lake and the valley, and it was resting at the angle rubble rests at, with nothing binding it.

**A lake with no other exit leaves in one go.** There is no basin overflow here, because the wall is the basin. When it fails, the whole stored volume moves down the valley as a single front - water, morainic debris and boulders together.

## Why the Temple Is the Evidence

The photograph shows scouring on **both sides** of the temple, in wide boulder-strewn channels, with the buildings gone from the ground between them. So the water arrived. It went around the temple rather than through it.

The flood is what was unusual; the building is what was ordinary for a plinthed stone temple and unordinary for everything around it.

**The lesson is not "the temple was high". It is "the temple was built for the load".**
