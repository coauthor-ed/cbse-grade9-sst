# Characters That Look Like What They Mean

The book asks you to find characters that look like what they mean. First, think about what a script like this gives and what it costs.

<!-- STEP id="dialects" -->
## One Sign, Different Speech

You met this idea in Sumer: the sign 5 is "paanch" to a Hindi speaker and "ainthu" to a Tamil speaker. Chinese works in a similar way. A Mandarin speaker and a Cantonese speaker may not follow each other's speech, yet both can read the same newspaper.

<!-- STEP id="speed" -->
## Faster Writing

Cutting signs into bone was slow, so the early signs could stay like pictures. Later writers used brushes on bamboo strips and silk, and speed mattered more. The curves became straight strokes, and the pictures faded.

<!-- STEP id="cost" -->
## The Cost

English needs 26 letters. Hindi needs about 50. A reader of Chinese must learn thousands of characters, which takes years of school.

This affected who could learn to read in old China.

<!-- STEP id="roots" -->
## Your Turn to Look

Tap the three signs here: sun, moon and mountain. Their oldest forms look like drawings of the thing. Find two more characters and explain how the shape connects to the meaning. Most modern characters also carry a clue to the sound, so not every one will be a picture.

**One script can serve different kinds of speech, but it takes thousands of signs.**
