# Three Stages Side by Side

<!-- STEP id="tools" -->
## Tools

The book's three stages can be compared one topic at a time.

| Palaeolithic | Mesolithic | Neolithic |
|---|---|---|
| Handaxes, cleavers, scrapers; later, flake tools and microblades | Microliths set in handles | Tools for food production, such as polished celts |

<!-- STEP id="homes" -->
## Where People Lived

| Palaeolithic | Mesolithic | Neolithic |
|---|---|---|
| Caves or open camps | Temporary settlements near rivers and lakes | Permanent villages, with pottery and weaving |

<!-- STEP id="art" -->
## Art

| Palaeolithic | Mesolithic | Neolithic |
|---|---|---|
| Cave paintings, beads | Art flourished; Bhimbetka | Ornaments, pottery shapes |

<!-- STEP id="food" -->
## Food

| Palaeolithic | Mesolithic | Neolithic |
|---|---|---|
| Hunting and gathering | Fishing above all, with small game and wild grains | Farming and herding |

<!-- STEP id="silent" -->
## Where the Book Is Silent

The book names no art from the early Palaeolithic: the paintings and beads come from its later part. It does not describe Neolithic wall paintings.

One of the earliest farming villages in our own subcontinent stood beside the Bolan river. How can we tell that its people farmed?
