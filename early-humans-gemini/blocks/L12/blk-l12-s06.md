# Words, Not Sounds

In English, letters stand for sounds. Hindi works the same way, with signs for sounds. You write down the sounds, and the reader says the word. Chinese took a different path, and these two old signs show it: one means person and one means tree.

## Pictures That Became Signs

Some characters began as small pictures. This drawing shows the two signs changing over time, from bone to bronze, to stamped signs, and then to the brush. The book's two examples are these same words, person and tree.

<!-- STEP id="modern-character" -->
## Logographs

The book calls the Chinese script logographic. Logo means word and graph means writing. Each character stands for a whole word or a morpheme, and not for a sound. Other early scripts also used some word signs. The book describes Chinese this way because whole-word signs are its main method. A morpheme is the smallest unit of language that has a meaning: "unkind" has two, "un" and "kind".

<!-- STEP id="logograph-sim" -->
## Person

In the oldest form, the sign for person is a side view of someone standing. Writers then moved from knife on bone to brush and ink, and over many centuries the sign became two strokes.

<!-- STEP id="tree" -->
## Tree

The sign for tree began as a trunk with branches above and roots below. It changed in the same way, and its branches became one straight stroke.

**The picture is harder to see now, but the sign still stands for a whole word.**
