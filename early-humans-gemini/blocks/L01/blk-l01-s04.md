# What the Ground Can and Cannot Say

<!-- STEP id="answers" -->
## What Objects Can Answer

The answer is what they ate and how they built. Objects answer questions like these:

- Bones, seeds and charred grain show diet.
- Tools show what people could make.
- A shell or a stone from far away suggests contact with another region.
- Post holes and wall bases show the plan of a house or a settlement.
- Teeth and healed breaks in bones show a person's health.

**Each of these is a question about what people did.**

<!-- STEP id="silence" -->
## What Objects Cannot Answer

Nobody's name is in the soil, and neither is what they believed or who gave the orders. A grave can be very rich and still not tell you the title of the person lying in it.

This is why your textbook says it is generally difficult to understand the thoughts and ideas of people from before writing.

**Objects show what people did, but rarely what they thought.**

<!-- STEP id="writing" -->
## What Writing Adds

Before writing we know little about names, events and dates. After writing, records give names, events and dates such as a coronation or a war.

- Before writing: material remains only, and dates that are approximate.
- After writing: material remains and written documents together, and dates that are more exact.

**Writing adds to the objects and does not replace them.**

<!-- STEP id="proportion" -->
## How Much of the Past Has No Writing

Writing is about 5,000 years old, and humans have been making tools for about 3 million years (the earliest tools are about 3.3 million years old).

That puts more than 99 out of every 100 years of human history before writing.

**For almost all of human history, objects are our only source.**
