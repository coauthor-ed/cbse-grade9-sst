# Sunlight at a slant

<!-- STEP -->
## The slant makes the difference

The Arctic is colder because sunlight reaches it at a slant. Near the equator, the midday Sun shines almost straight down.

Take two equal beams of sunlight. At the equator the beam lights a small patch of ground. At 60°N the same beam lights a patch about twice as wide.

<!-- STEP -->
## The same energy, spread thinner

The energy from the Sun that the Earth receives is called **insolation**. At 60°N each piece of ground gets only about half the share that it gets at the equator. Tap 30°N to compare.

**Insolation decreases from the equator towards the poles, and so does temperature.**

<!-- STEP -->
## A longer path through the air

A slanting beam also crosses more air on its way down, so a little more sunlight is lost before it reaches the ground. Spreading is still the main reason.

Thinner air could not explain the cold anyway, because thinner air would stop less sunlight.

<!-- STEP -->
## What about distance?

"Further from the Sun" is a sensible guess, and it fails on one number. The Sun is about 15 crore km away, and the Arctic is only a few thousand km further than the equator.

That difference is far too small to matter. The angle of the sunlight matters much more.
