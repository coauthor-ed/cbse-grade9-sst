# Demand Can Change When the Price Does Not

<!-- STEP -->
## When the whole curve moves

When a new model of a popular smartphone comes out, people stand in long queues to buy it, even at a high price. So price is not the only thing that changes demand. When something else changes, the whole demand curve moves: to the right if people want more at every price, to the left if they want less.

<!-- STEP -->
## Substitute goods

Some goods can replace each other, like tea and coffee. They are called **substitute goods**. If coffee becomes costlier while tea's price stays the same, some coffee drinkers switch to tea, so the demand for tea rises. In the same way, if Srivalli cannot afford mangoes, she may buy bananas.

<!-- STEP -->
## Complementary goods

Some goods are used together, like smartphones and earphones, or cars and petrol. They are called **complementary goods**. If movie tickets become costlier, fewer people go to the cinema. So the demand for popcorn at the cinema falls, even though the price of popcorn has not changed.
