# Assur, Babylon, and a Law Put Where People Could See It

<!-- STEP id="stele" -->
## A King Before a God

A stele is a tall stone with carving on it. At the top of this one, King Hammurabi stands before Shamash, the Babylonian god of justice, and receives the law. Showing the law as a gift from a god gave it great authority.

<!-- STEP id="registers" -->
## The Law in Cuneiform

Below the carving, the laws are written in cuneiform. Hammurabi brought together rules for civil and social conduct, known as the Code of Hammurabi. It was set up in public, though few people could read it, and historians still debate how far it was applied. It became a model for many later legal systems.

<!-- STEP id="assur" -->
## How Babylon Got There

Hammurabi came late in a long line of powers. The Akkadian empire lost its top place around 2154 BCE to Assur, a city-state in northern Mesopotamia. Assyrian rule spread to the west and south, and it lasted until early in the 1700s BCE.

<!-- STEP id="babylon" -->
## Babylon's Turn

While the Assyrians were strong in the north, Babylonia gained power in central Mesopotamia from 1900 BCE. So for about 200 years the two overlapped. Babylonia's great time began when Hammurabi came to power in 1792 BCE and turned a small city-state into a large empire.

<!-- STEP id="decline" -->
## Why Babylon Weakened

By the end of 1400 BCE, Babylonia had lost its leading place. The book gives several reasons, working together:

- repeated attacks by the Hittites and other rising powers, who had newer technology and used resources better
- damage to the environment and pressure on farmland
- political and economic problems inside the state

The Hittites were a people who built a powerful empire in Anatolia, today's Turkey.
