# The Deccan: Three Levels

## An empire of āḥāras

In the Deccan, the Mauryan Empire was followed by the **Satavāhana Empire**, which lasted nearly 500 years, from the 2nd century BCE to the 3rd century CE.

The empire was made up of **āḥāras**, which were administrative divisions, and each āḥāra had its own ministers, the **amātyas**. Below the āḥāras were the villages, and each village was led by a headman called the **grāmika**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Different officers, different work

Early Indian kingdoms gave different work to different officers:

- **District governors**, or **pradeśhikas**, had judicial and administrative work.
- **City governors** were in charge of revenue collection and law and order.
- The **grāmika** led his village.

## The king stayed at the top

Supreme authority rested with the king, but he did not run every office himself. The general administration was carried out by officers working at provincial and local levels.

**The king held the highest authority, and his officers did the daily work of government.**
