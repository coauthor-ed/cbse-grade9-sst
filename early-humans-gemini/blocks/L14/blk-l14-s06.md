# Rivers: Gifts and Dangers

The book asks you to think about both sides of living near a river. Rivers gave fresh water, a way to move goods and new soil. They could also destroy what people built.

<!-- STEP id="floods" -->
## Floods and Moving Channels

Floods on the Tigris and Euphrates came at unreliable times and depths. A river could also change its course, and that could leave a city far from water.

When the Nile flood was low, harvests failed. Floods on the Huang He could destroy fields.

<!-- STEP id="salt" -->
## Salt in the Soil

Irrigation in a dry land has a slower danger. Water dries up and leaves its salts behind, so the soil can slowly get worse. The book names damage to the environment and pressure on farmland among Babylonia's troubles, and historians think salt may be one part of that.

<!-- STEP id="crisis" -->
## One River, One Risk

When everybody depends on one river, one bad year hurts everybody. Historians think this is one reason why people worked together on shared embankments and canals.

**A river gave people a great deal, and it also put everything they had built at risk.**
