# The Whole Subcontinent as an Idea

<!-- STEP -->
## Beyond the kingdom

Early Indian monarchs did not think only of the boundaries of their own kingdoms. Their awareness extended to the **whole subcontinent**.

The solid colour shows land that one king held. The soft outline shows the larger land that kings thought about.

<!-- STEP -->
## Words for the whole land

Tap each word. **Jambudvīpa**, **Bhāratavarṣha** and **Prithivi** were all used for this land.

Aśhoka says in an edict that his "energetic exertions" led to "significant changes in the spiritual life in Jambudvīpa". The Arthaśhāstra defines Prithivi as "the area lying between the Himavat (Himalayas) and the sea", and it equates this with the chakravarti kśhetra, the "domain of a universal paramount ruler".

<!-- STEP -->
## Two royal rites

Two yajñas expressed the same idea.

- In the **aśhvamedha**, a horse was set free to roam, with guards walking behind it. Lands it crossed unchallenged were claimed by the king.
- The **Rājasūya** was a royal consecration rite.

<!-- STEP -->
## The same aim, again and again

This ideal was expressed again and again over about 1300 years, by each king on the strip.

- **Aśhoka**, 3rd century BCE, on Jambudvīpa
- **Nedunjeral Adan**, a Chera king of the Sangam period, described as winning conquests up to the Himalayas
- **Rajendra I**, a Chola ruler of the 11th century CE, who took the title **Gangaikonda**

A description of a king's aim is not a map of the land he held.
