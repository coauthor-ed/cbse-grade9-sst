# When the Water Failed

This slide goes beyond the book's chapter. It reports what many researchers argue, and they still disagree on the details.

<!-- STEP id="drought2150" -->
## A Dry Time

Around 4,200 years ago, the Akkadian power lost its top place, and Egypt's Old Kingdom ended. Many researchers link these events with a long dry period. Other causes mattered too: the book says Assur took over from Akkad.

<!-- STEP id="riverleaves" -->
## A River Changes

The Mature Harappan stage ended around 1900 BCE, and the book does not discuss why. Many researchers link it with the Ghaggar-Sarasvati drying up and weaker monsoon rains. The cities do not seem to have been destroyed all at once, and many settlements simply became smaller.

<!-- STEP id="today" -->
## Still True Today

A city does not grow its own food. Today's arguments about water and climate come back to the same fact.

**Every government depends on food from farms.**
