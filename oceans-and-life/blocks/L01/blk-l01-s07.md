## How were these shapes formed?

The ridge, seamounts and volcanic islands were built by volcanic activity. At a ridge, the plates of the Earth's crust are thought to move apart, and magma rises to fill the gap. At a trench, one plate is thought to slide down under another. The shelf is simply the edge of the continent, covered by the sea.

<!-- STEP -->
## Which parts are deep and which are shallow?

The shelf is the shallowest part, and sunlight reaches much of it. Slope, rise and plain go down into the dark. Ridges and seamounts rise high, yet most stay under water, and trenches go deepest of all.

<!-- STEP -->
## What does the shape change?

Sunlit shallows crowd with marine life, so fishing boats work over the shelf. A ridge or a shelf edge stands in the way of moving water and turns it aside. For people, the shape decides where ships go, where oil rigs stand and where undersea cables are laid. Deep trenches are the hardest places to explore.
