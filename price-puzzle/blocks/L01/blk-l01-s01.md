# Why Do Prices Change?

<!-- STEP -->
## Prices that do not stay still

Mangoes that cost ₹150 per kg last week may cost half that now. Vegetables are often cheaper in the evening than in the morning. The same seat on the same flight can cost ₹3,000 on one day and ₹9,000 on another.

These changes are not random. This chapter shows what moves a price.

<!-- STEP -->
## Buyers on one side, sellers on the other

A price is set where buyers and sellers meet. How much buyers want to buy at each price is called **demand**. How much sellers offer at each price is called **supply**.

The chapter asks four Big Questions:
- What changes demand and supply?
- How do demand and supply set a price?
- What is market equilibrium, and does it exist in real life?
- How and why does the government act in a market?

<!-- STEP -->
## Wanting is not enough

Demand is more than a wish. It is the amount people are **willing and able** to buy at a given price. "Able" means having the money to pay. How much your money can buy is called **purchasing power**.

A buyer who wants mangoes but cannot pay for them adds nothing to the demand for mangoes.
