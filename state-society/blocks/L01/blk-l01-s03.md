# Three Assemblies

<!-- STEP -->
## It depended on the matter

The third answer is right. The Ṛigveda and the Atharvaveda refer to **three assemblies**, and each played its own part.

Many people picture one meeting for every matter. The texts name three assemblies, so a matter had to go to the right one.

<!-- STEP -->
## The sabhā

The **sabhā** was a smaller body made up of select elites. It mainly served a judicial function, which means it heard cases and gave judgements.

<!-- STEP -->
## The samiti

The **samiti** was a larger assembly that represented the broader population. It focused on policy decisions and political affairs.

<!-- STEP -->
## The vidhata

The **vidhata** was a popular gathering attended by members of the janas. People discussed war and other political matters there.

<!-- STEP -->
## A question to think about

Today, heads of government hold power, but constitutions and parliaments bind them. Do the rājā and the assemblies suggest a similar idea? Weigh these before you answer.

- The assemblies had real work to do, so the rājā did not hold every decision.
- The sabhā was made up of select elites, so not everyone took part in the same way.
- The texts tell us what the assemblies did, but not how often they stopped a rājā.
