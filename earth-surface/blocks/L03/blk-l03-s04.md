## Bhuj, 2001

This is a street in Bhuj, in Kachchh, in Gujarat, after the major earthquake of 2001.

The foreground heap is not rubble from one collapsed building. It is a whole street filled to the rooftops with masonry. Behind it the buildings are still standing, with their outer walls gone and their floors hanging over open rooms.

## A Map Cannot Show You This

Everything so far has been a map or a cross-section, and this slide is neither.

That is deliberate. A cross-section can show you a plate boundary. It cannot show you what a plate boundary does to a town full of people. This is a drawing problem right up until it stops being one.

**The shaking is the same wherever it happens. The damage is decided by what was built on top of it.**

## The Answer Has a Shape

The poster beside this photograph is a National Disaster Management Authority guidance poster, and the hazard it is about is not this one. Look at its shape anyway, because the shape is the answer.

Three columns. **Before, During, After.**

Almost everything in the *During* column is a thing you cannot do while the ground is moving. The work is in the *Before* column, and the *After* column is mostly about not turning one disaster into a second one.
