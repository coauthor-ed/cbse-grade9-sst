## Food and livelihoods

Marine resources are vital for food security, which means that people can always get enough to eat. Fish are a major source of protein, and fishing and aquaculture, the farming of fish, support important industries.

Fishing, trade and tourism give livelihoods to millions of people, so a country with a long coast like India gains greatly from the sea.

## Energy and materials

Oil and gas lie in reserves offshore, under the sea bed. Tides and waves offer renewable **blue energy**, and so far only a little of it is used.

The sea also gives sand, gravel and deep-sea minerals, which are materials that people build and make things with.

## Trade and tourism

About 90% of global trade by volume is carried by sea. Volume means the amount of goods, not the money they are worth.

Coastal tourism is also a multi-billion dollar industry, which is why a beach or a reef can matter as much to a town as a harbour does.

## Culture and living things

The sacred śhaṅkha (conch) and kapardikā (cowrie shell) were once used as currency. They are regarded as divine treasures from the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean in mythology.

The ocean also supports biodiversity, the great variety of plants and animals that live in it. Marine resources must be used responsibly, without disturbing marine ecosystems.
