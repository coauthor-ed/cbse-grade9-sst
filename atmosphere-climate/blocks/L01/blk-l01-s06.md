# The Same Mix Higher Up?

<!-- STEP -->
## Three jars

Imagine filling a jar at sea level, another at 8 km on a high mountain, and a third at 50 km.

The higher jars hold far less air. **The density of air is highest near the Earth's surface and falls as you go up.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The main shares stay nearly the same

There is less of every gas in the higher jars, but nitrogen, oxygen, argon and carbon dioxide keep nearly the same shares up to about 100 km.

You would expect the heavier gases to sink to the bottom. The moving air keeps them mixed.

<!-- STEP -->
## What changes with height

**Altitude** is the height of a place above mean sea level. Some gases do not spread evenly, so the mix does change with altitude.

- **Water vapour** stays low, close to the ground.
- **Ozone** gathers high up, in the stratosphere.
- **The lightest gases** are found at the very top.
