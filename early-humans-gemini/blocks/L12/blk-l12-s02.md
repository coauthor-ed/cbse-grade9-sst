# Two Rivers

China's early civilisation grew along two rivers: the Huang He, or Yellow River, in the north, and the Yangtze further south. You have seen how rivers helped people in Mesopotamia and Egypt, with water, rich soil and an easy way to move goods.

<!-- STEP id="farming" -->
## The First Villages

The book says these two valleys were centres of early Neolithic cultures from around 7000 BCE. The chart in the farming lesson showed their two crops: millets in the north and rice in the south. Fine yellow soil called loess covers much of the land around the Huang He.

<!-- STEP id="bronze" -->
## Metal Arrives

Around 2000 BCE, copper and bronze working reached many of these settlements, above all in the Yellow River basin. You might expect cities to follow at once. They did not, and these places stayed villages for about four more centuries.

<!-- STEP id="city" -->
## The First Cities

Urban centres began to appear around 1600 BCE. The book gives the reasons: farms produced more, and metal work and crafts improved. Cities such as Zhengzhou and Anyang grew up near the Huang He.

**Metal alone did not make cities. They also needed more food and better crafts.**
