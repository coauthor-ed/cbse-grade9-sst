# The List, One Item at a Time

<!-- STEP id="water" -->
## River Plains

All four grew up on fertile river plains. Each river brought water and soil, and people learned to manage it with canals, embankments or tanks.

<!-- STEP id="surplus" -->
## More Food Than Farmers Needed

Good land and better methods gave a surplus, which means more food than the farmers needed. The crops differed from river to river. But a surplus could feed people who did other work, such as potters and metal workers.

<!-- STEP id="urban" -->
## Cities

The book says this growth led to cities in all these centres. Their plans differed: straight streets and covered drains are a Harappan feature, so they are not on the shared list.

<!-- STEP id="writing" -->
## Writing, With Different First Jobs

All four had writing, but it did not begin the same way everywhere. In Sumer the oldest tablets are mostly lists of goods. In China the oldest records are questions on bones. The Harappan script cannot be read, so we do not know what it was used for first.

<!-- STEP id="government" -->
## Social and Administrative Systems

Each one developed ways of running things, with people at different levels. The book says they did this independently. For Harappan cities, we still do not know who ruled.

<!-- STEP id="metal" -->
## New Resources

The book also speaks of "new resources". All four are called Bronze Age civilisations because copper and bronze were now in use, for tools, weapons and fine objects.
