# Two Written Laws

<!-- STEP id="setup" -->
## A Similar Problem

Hammurabi came to power in 1792 BCE, and his empire took in many older city-states. The Qin took power in 221 BCE, after conquering many rival states. These two moments are more than 1,500 years apart, and thousands of kilometres apart.

Both rulers made written law for a large state. Nothing suggests that either copied the other.

<!-- STEP id="design" -->
## Two Different Laws

Hammurabi's law graded the penalty by rank:

- a free citizen's eye: the offender's own eye
- a commoner's eye: a fine in silver
- a slave's eye: half the slave's value, paid to the owner

Qin law, in the account of a Han historian, was harsh for everyone: late workers faced death. The strips dug up later show smaller penalties for lateness, so that account needs care.

<!-- STEP id="outcome" -->
## What Happened Next

Babylon lost its leading place by the end of 1400 BCE, for several reasons. The Qin fell in 206 BCE, and historians still argue about how much the law had to do with it.

**Two rulers reached for written law on their own, and the two laws were very different.**
