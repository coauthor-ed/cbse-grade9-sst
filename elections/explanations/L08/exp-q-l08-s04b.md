# What a Vote Is Worth

<!-- STEP -->
## The two countries are identical, except for one word

Both have a written constitution. Both hold periodic elections. Both have voting rights.

One runs on a party system that varies from **two-party to multi-party**. The other has **one political party**.

Everything in this question turns on that one difference, and nothing else in the table changes with it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the number of parties decides what a vote is worth

Take a voter whose first preference is a small party.

- In the **one-party** country, there is nothing to vote for instead. The result cannot turn on this person's choice, so the vote changes nothing - not because it was counted wrongly, but because there was never another outcome available.
- In the **multi-party** country, the small party may still be the one that decides who forms the government. **The vote is not wasted even when the first preference loses.**

That is the whole mechanism. It is about what a single vote can do, not about how any country is run afterwards.

<!-- STEP -->
## The option that is real but answers a different question

One wrong option says a single-party country **can decide policy faster**, because there is no opposition to negotiate with.

That is a genuine argument, and it is one of the two sides this chapter puts on the table. It is still not the answer here, for two reasons.

- It is a claim about **governing**, not about the value of a **vote**. The question asks what follows from the difference between the two party systems.
- The chapter states it and then **stops**. Answering with it means answering the question the chapter deliberately leaves open.

<!-- STEP -->
## The two options that invent consequences

Neither the table nor the chapter says that a party system changes **how often** elections are held, or that more parties means **more officials** touching the count.

Read a table by its columns. It varies the party system on one side and the living standard on the other, and it never joins them up - so a link between those two rows has to be supplied by the reader, not found in the table.
