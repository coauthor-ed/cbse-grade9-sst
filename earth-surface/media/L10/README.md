# Media provenance - L10

Per `docs/copyright-and-sourcing.md`: never extract a textbook's own
illustrations or photographs; source an openly-licensed equivalent and
record its source and licence here.

**None of the image files below exist yet.** Each is referenced by a block
in this lesson, and nothing was downloaded or generated at this stage.
Three of the five are **real photographs of real events or landforms** -
they may not be generated or synthesised, and they may not be a scan of
the textbook page. The poster is a different case and is marked below.

Each of the four `Fig. 2.2x` files stands in for the source figure of that
number, and in each case the textbook's printed photograph is **not** to be
extracted. `FIGURES.md` carries the full inspection notes for all four.

## landslide-road-blockage.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s01.json` (`image.src`).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.27` (p.21 / book p.33).
- To produce: download a photograph of a **landslide blocking a mountain
  highway in Himalayan terrain**, from Wikimedia Commons or another
  open-licence repository. Save it here and record below: source page URL,
  file name on the source, author or credited institution if any, and the
  exact licence (Commons photos are usually CC BY-SA or CC0 - check the file
  page, do not assume). Places `FIGURES.md` names as candidates: Uttarakhand,
  Himachal Pradesh, or the Kashmir valley road approaches.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.27. Three details are
  load-bearing, and every one of them is a claim the prose makes:
  1. **A bare slide scar above the road** - the hillside face stripped to
     rock. The slide's opening is about what is *missing* from the slope,
     not about the pile of debris at the bottom, and a photograph showing
     only a heap of rubble on a road loses the slide's actual subject.
  2. **Stopped traffic** - stranded vehicles on the road below. This is the
     slide's closing turn: the danger is the road and the vehicles on it,
     which sit on the *side* of the road rather than inside it.
  3. **A recognisable mountain road cut**, so the road is visibly a thing
     somebody cut into the hillside. The slide's claim is that the cut is
     what removed what was holding the slope up, and a photograph of an
     untouched hillside could not carry that at all.
- **No casualties and no visible injury** may be in the frame, and **no
  text, no signage and no watermark** overlaid on the image.
- **The block uses this file only at `blk-l10-s01`.** `blk-l10-s02` and
  `blk-l10-s03` deliberately carry **no photograph**: s02 is the predict and
  the learner must compare four unlabelled slopes without a photograph
  telling them what a landslide looks like, and s03 teaches the three
  conditions. Neither may borrow this file.

## avalanche-on-steep-face.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s04.json` (`reveal.images.avalanche-photo.src`,
  key `avalanche-photo`, shown from the slide's first beat).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.28` (p.22 / book p.34). The source
  figure is a **two-panel composite** (the avalanche on the left, a rescue
  team on the right); this file is **one photograph**, and which one is a
  judgement call left open here.
- To produce: download a photograph of either a **powder snow avalanche
  billowing down a steep snow-covered face**, or a **snow patrol / rescue
  team in cold-weather gear trekking through deep snow on an alpine slope**.
- **One of the two panels must be chosen deliberately, and logged**, because
  the two carry different slides' arguments. The avalanche panel shows the
  *event*; the patrol panel shows the *trigger* this slide turns on - that
  people on a snow slope can be the whole trigger, which is why the chapter
  counts skiing and trekking as causes. **The patrol panel is the stronger
  fit for `blk-l10-s04`** and is the recommended choice; the avalanche panel
  would repeat what `SnowpackScene`'s own slab release already draws.
  If the avalanche panel is used instead, note it here.
- What the slide needs either way: **a steep face**, and people or a rescue
  team at a size that reads as small against it.
- **No groomed piste, no ski lift, no resort building and no artificial
  snow** may be in the frame - the scene must not read as a ski area, and
  no mitigation structure may appear.

## kedarnath-glof-aftermath.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s05.json` (`reveal.images.glof-photo.src`,
  key `glof-photo`, shown from the slide's first beat).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.29` (p.23 / book p.35).
- To produce: download an **aerial photograph of the Kedarnath temple town
  after the June 2013 flood** from Wikimedia Commons or another open-licence
  repository, and record source URL, file name, author and exact licence.
  This is the one figure in the lesson where the **specific real place is
  load-bearing**: the slide's argument is that the temple survived while the
  town around it was scoured away, which is a claim about *that* building on
  *that* plinth in *that* valley, and it cannot be carried by a photograph
  of a different flood at a different site.
- What the slide needs. Four details, every one of them a sentence of the
  prose:
  1. **The temple standing**, recognisably a stone temple on a plinth, and
     **still intact**.
  2. **Wide boulder-strewn scour channels on both sides of it**, so the
     viewer can see that the water reached the ground the temple stands on
     and went around it. A photograph of an intact temple on untouched
     ground would leave the slide's actual point - that the water arrived -
     unstated.
  3. **The buildings that were there now absent**, visible as cleared
     ground, foundations or scour rather than as a rebuilt town. The
     *absence* is the evidence.
  4. **Steep green valley slopes flanking the scene**, so the frame reads as
     a high mountain valley rather than as a plain.
- **No reconstruction, no rebuilt town and no later restoration** may be in
  the frame; a photograph showing the site after rebuilding would contradict
  the slide.
- **Note:** `media/L09/README.md` deliberately declined this same figure for
  L09's closing hook, on the grounds that L10 s05 is the slide that teaches
  the process. That reasoning holds and this file is the one that gets made.

## dust-storm-wall.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s06.json` (`reveal.images.dust-storm-photo.src`,
  key `dust-storm-photo`, shown from the slide's first beat).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.30` (p.24 / book p.36).
- **A separate file from `media/L08/dust-storm-wall.jpg`**, which L08's
  closing hook already declares and which is a *different claim* (what the
  wind doing its smallest job looks like). If a human decides the same
  photograph serves both, the two references can be pointed at one file and
  this entry deleted - **that is a decision for a human, not for this pass.**
- To produce: download a photograph of a **severe dust storm / haboob as a
  dense wall of airborne dust**, from Wikimedia Commons or another
  open-licence repository. Places the source's own entry suggests: the Thar
  of Rajasthan, the Rann of Kutch, or Rajasthan generally. Record source
  URL, file name, author and exact licence.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.30:
  1. **The dust as a wall, thick enough to take the horizon and much of the
     sky away** - not as a haze. That is what earns this hazard a name of
     its own rather than a place among the dunes, and a photograph of light
     airborne dust over a visible landscape loses it.
  2. **Bare, sparsely-leaved or dead branches** in the foreground, so the
     viewer can see the ground the dust came off rather than only the dust
     in the air.
  3. **Arid ground**, ideally visible at the base of the frame.
- **No people, no vehicles, no livestock and no buildings may be in the
  frame**, and no dust may be shown harming anything - the slide's claim is
  about the *preparation* (loose, dry, unbound ground), not about damage.
- **No dune, no yardang and no oasis** may be identifiable in the frame: L08
  owns those, and the slide is about a failed field rather than a desert.

## ndma-landslide-safety-poster.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s07.json` (`image.src`).
- Stands for the source's `NDMA Landslide Poster` (p.26 / book p.38).
- **This one is not a photograph of a landform, and the sourcing rule is
  different.** A **government advisory poster is published for public use**;
  it is not a textbook illustration, so it is a fair substitute rather than
  a reproduction. Download it **from NDMA's own site or a government
  mirror - `ndma.gov.in`, or a state disaster-management authority mirror -
  and never from the textbook page.** Record the URL and the terms.
- **A file already exists for this poster elsewhere in the bundle:**
  `media/L03/ndma-landslide-safety-poster.jpg`, referenced by
  `blocks/L03/blk-l03-s04.json` under `reveal.images.ndma`. **Both blocks
  should point at one downloaded file**, and this lesson deliberately uses
  its own `media/L10/` path rather than reaching into `media/L03/` so that
  each lesson's provenance record stays self-contained. **Deciding which
  path survives is a human call** - it is one poster, not two, and
  collapsing the duplicate is the tidier outcome.
- What the slide needs: three columns headed **Before**, **During** and
  **After**, with the NDMA logo, the hazard emblem and the helpline
  (`011-1078`) visible. The slide's whole point is the *shape* of that
  three-column arrangement and the fact that the middle column is almost
  entirely things you cannot do while the ground is moving, so a poster with
  the columns cropped or unreadable is useless here.
- The slide carries **no scene on purpose** - this is a real Government of
  India document and it should be shown as itself, not redrawn.

## Images this lesson does NOT need

- **`Fig. 2.27`'s three exercise prompt lines** ("Prone areas:",
  "Mitigation measures:", "Recent examples:"). They are printed under the
  photograph in the source as prompts for a written exercise; they are not
  part of the photograph, must not be cropped into it, and must not be
  recreated as overlay text. `blocks/L10/blk-l10-s08.md` and the game on
  s09 carry the mitigation content in the form the chapter actually uses.
- **A second photograph of any of the four disasters.** Each hazard has
  exactly one real photograph in this lesson, and a second image of the same
  event would add scale information the scene already has to supply rather
  than any claim the slide makes.
- **Any generated or synthetic image.** Every file above is a record of a
  real thing. Where a later pass renders one, the prompt belongs in a
  `<same-base-name>.prompt.txt` beside it - and none of these five is a
  candidate for that treatment.
