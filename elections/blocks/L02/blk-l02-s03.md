# Three Countries, Three Rules

## Haritbhumi: First Past The Post

Tap a country to compare the three. **Haritbhumi** has 25 seats. The candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins. Party A has 40 per cent, Party B 35 and Party C 25, and Party A forms the government.

<!-- STEP -->
## Ratnadweep: Majority System

**Ratnadweep** also has 25 seats. To win, a candidate must receive more than 50 per cent of the votes. If nobody does, the top two compete again.

Party X has 45 per cent, Party Y 40 and Party Z 15. No one got 50 per cent, so Round 2 is held. Party Y wins it with 55 per cent and forms the government.

<!-- STEP -->
## Swarnalok: Proportional Representation

**Swarnalok** has 100 seats on a nationwide list. Voters vote for a party, not a person, and seats are allotted in proportion to the total votes.

Parties M, N, O and P polled 40, 35, 15 and 10 per cent, so they get 40, 35, 15 and 10 seats. **Power is shared proportionately.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Compare the Three

Look at what decides the result in each country:

- FPTP: the most votes in each constituency
- Majority system: more than half of the votes, in a second round if needed
- Proportional representation (PR): each party's share of all the votes

**Party X came first in Round 1 and still lost. Under FPTP, X would have won.**
