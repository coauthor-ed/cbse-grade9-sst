# Highest and Lowest Prices

<!-- STEP -->
## A ceiling makes a shortage

At ₹50, buyers want 18 kg but sellers offer only 6 kg, so 12 kg is missing. In a free market the price would rise. Under the ceiling it cannot, so the shortage stays. A ceiling changes things only when it is set below the market price.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why a ceiling on medicines?

The government sets a highest price for essential goods like medicines, so that sellers cannot overcharge. Families who could not pay the market price can now buy them. But at the lower price sellers may offer less, so some buyers may still go without.

<!-- STEP -->
## A lowest price: the floor

A price floor is the lowest price that may be charged. A minimum wage is a price floor: an employer must pay at least that wage, so that workers earn enough for their work. **A ceiling has an effect only below the market price; a floor has an effect only above it.**
