## Start From What a Delta Is Made Of

A delta is not a piece of old land that the sea has reached. It is **new land, made entirely of sediment the river dropped** at the one place on its journey where it ran out of the energy to carry anything further.

Two things follow from that, and the whole question hangs on them.

- It is **low**. It is built at sea level, out of the finest material the river carries.
- It is **flat**. There is no slope to it, because it is being built outward sideways as well as forward.

<!-- STEP -->
## Then Ask What Water Needs From the Ground

Flood water is not dangerous everywhere. On a hillside it runs off; on a floodplain it spreads out and drains back into the channel.

A delta has neither a hill to shed water or a slope to drain it. **There is simply no higher ground nearby for the water to go instead.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Two Directions At Once

The question gives you the surge from the seaward side and the river flood from the landward side. Neither is unusual on its own. What makes this arrangement is that both arrive on the same flat, low, sediment-built ground at the same time, and the channels between the islands are the same channels the surge comes up.

**The risk is not the water. It is the ground there is nothing to rise above.**

<!-- STEP -->
## And the Reason People Are There Anyway

The same sediment that makes the delta dangerous to live on is what makes it worth living on. It is the **fertile alluvial soil** the chapter names, and it is why delta islands carry dense settlements and are centres of trade.

That is not a contradiction, and it is not a mistake by the people who farm there. A delta offers very good land at the cost of very little height.

**Living on the newest, flattest ground a river can build, and paying for it whenever the water rises, is the trade a delta always offers.**
