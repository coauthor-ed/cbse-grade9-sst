# A Lake and Its Dam

<!-- STEP -->
## A dam for the fields

Agriculture was closely dependent on irrigation, so reservoirs, canals and dams mattered greatly.

An inscription records that **Puṣhyagupta**, a governor appointed by Chandragupta Maurya, built a dam on **Sudarshana Lake** near Girnar in Saurāṣhṭra. That was around the 4th century BCE.

<!-- STEP -->
## Rudradaman's repair

Tap **Breach**, then **Repair**. Around 150 CE, the ruler **Rudradaman I** recorded a long Sanskrit inscription describing the repair of the lake.

Look at the date strip: this repair came more than 400 years after the dam was built.

<!-- STEP -->
## Repaired again

In the 5th century CE, the Gupta emperor **Skandagupta** engraved a record on the same rock, noting the restoration of the lake after severe damage.

The dam had now been looked after under three different dynasties.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Grand Anicut

In the south, **Karikala Chola** built the Grand Anicut (**Kallanai**). The Cholas built canals, lakes and tanks with connecting channels, which stored and distributed water.

Later rulers kept using and repairing the Grand Anicut for many centuries. Remarkably, it remains in use even today.

## Think about it

Why were structures like dams and canals considered so important that their construction was recorded in inscriptions?

One clue: facilitating agriculture was a major concern of the state, because agriculture was one of the major sources of revenue. What other reasons can you think of?
