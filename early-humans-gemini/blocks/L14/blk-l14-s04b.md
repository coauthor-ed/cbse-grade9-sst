# Why Did Rulers Appear?

A shared canal needs organising, but that alone does not need a king. Neighbours could meet each season and agree who digs where. So why did rulers appear? Nobody saw it happen, so what follows is one explanation and not a fact.

<!-- STEP id="freerider" -->
## The One Who Skips the Work

A farmer who stays at home while others dig still gets the water. If many people do this, the canal silts up and everybody loses. A meeting of neighbours may find it hard to make him dig.

<!-- STEP id="standing" -->
## Someone Who Guards the Store

In the lesson on Mehrgarh you read that a store of food could give someone power, because whoever guards it can say who eats. A person who did that could also make people do their share of the work.

<!-- STEP id="entrench" -->
## Power That Stays

Once a family held the store, it might not hand it back when the work was done. Remember that Mehrgarh had stored food and no palace, so a store alone does not explain rulers.

**One explanation: organising the work may slowly have turned into lasting power.**

Force alone rarely lasts, so rulers also needed a reason why people should obey. We will come to that.
