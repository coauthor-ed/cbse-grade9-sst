# Takṣhaśhilā

<!-- STEP -->
## What is left

These are the ruins of **Takṣhaśhilā**: low stone walls in open grass. Only the bases of the buildings remain, yet the plan of the place can still be read.

<!-- STEP -->
## Follow the water

Tap **Run the channel**. Water enters at the top, follows the cut channels between the buildings and leaves at the low point.

This was a large campus with very well-organised urban systems, including various types of housing, drainage and waste management.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the stones tell us

We know that this place had housing, drainage and waste management. From that, we can reason that many people lived here and that someone looked after the place.

The ruins cannot tell us what was said in a lesson, so for that we need written sources.

<!-- STEP -->
## Who came here

Institutions of higher learning such as this one attracted students from both India and other regions.

Think about what those students needed each day: a room, clean water and a way to take waste away. Takṣhaśhilā had planned for such needs.
