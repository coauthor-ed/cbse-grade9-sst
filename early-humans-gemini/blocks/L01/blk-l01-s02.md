# How a Trench Reads Time

<!-- STEP id="dig" -->
## Where a Thing Lies Matters

The stone on the table could not tell you its own age, but the soil it came out of can help.

So archaeologists do not go out collecting objects. They remove earth slowly, and they record exactly where each find was lying when it appeared.

<!-- STEP id="section" -->
## Bands in the Wall

Cut a clean vertical face down through a site and you see that the earth is not one colour. It lies in bands.

Each band is a stretch of time: wind-blown dust, flood silt, ash from hearths (fireplaces), fallen mud brick, rubbish.

A band can only settle on top of one already there.

**Unless the ground has been disturbed, deeper means older.**

## Used, Dropped, Buried

Tools were made, used until they wore out, and thrown away. Earth kept settling on top of them, season after season and flood after flood, until each one was sealed in.

A trench goes back down through that whole sequence, from the newest layer to the oldest.

<!-- STEP id="fossil" -->
## Bone That Turned to Stone

The remains of some of our early ancestors were buried in the same slow way. Over thousands or even millions of years they turned into stone, keeping the shape of the original. A fossil is the preserved remains, traces or impressions of plants, animals or humans from the distant past.

**Tools, fossils and the layers they lie in are our main evidence.**
