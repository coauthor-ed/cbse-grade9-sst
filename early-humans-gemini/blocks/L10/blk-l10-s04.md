# The Black Land

<!-- STEP id="name" -->
## A Land Named After Its Soil

Egyptians called their land Kemet, which means "the black". The name comes from the black soil of the river valley, not from the desert on either side.

<!-- STEP id="soil" -->
## Named for What Feeds You

Every summer the Nile flooded and left rich mud, excellent for growing crops. So the country was named after the soil that fed it.

<!-- STEP id="zones" -->
## Two Parts, One River

Egypt had two parts: the fan-shaped Delta beside the Mediterranean Sea, and the narrow valley to the south.

<!-- STEP id="centres" -->
## Where the Centres Grew

City-states emerged around 3000 BCE, as the population grew and resources became available. The book's map shows Giza, Heliopolis, Memphis, Saqqara, Turah and Hermopolis, all close to the river.
