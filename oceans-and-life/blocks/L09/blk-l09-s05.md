## Rules of the road at sea

Just as there are traffic rules on land, ships follow rules to avoid accidents. These are the **COLREGs**, the Convention on the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea. They include:

- keeping a proper lookout
- deciding which side to pass on
- identifying the give-way and stand-on vessels
- proceeding at a safe speed
- using lights, sound signals and shapes
- taking the right action to prevent a collision
- sharing information by signals and radio

## Lights in the dark

At night a ship is seen by its lights. A ship shows a red light on its left side (port), a green light on its right side (starboard), and white lights on its masts. So the colours you see tell you which way a ship is heading. Choose a meeting and compare the view from above with what you would see from your own ship.

## Give way or stand on

When two ships meet, one may have to act. The **give-way vessel** acts early to keep clear. The **stand-on vessel** holds its course and speed.

- other ship on your right: you give way
- other ship on your left: you stand on
- head-on: both turn to their own right
- overtaking: the ship coming up from behind keeps clear

The crossing rule is for two ordinary ships with engines. Sailing and fishing vessels have rules of their own. Each ship follows the same convention, whatever its flag.
