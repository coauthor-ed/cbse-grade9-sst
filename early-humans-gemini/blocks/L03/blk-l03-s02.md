# Why the Past Is Divided into Ages

Nobody gave the human past its chapters. Archaeologists divided it, mostly by what people could make and how they lived. Each age is named for the material or technique that marks it.

The names look hard, but they are built from small pieces. *Palaeo* means old, *meso* means middle, *neo* means new, and *lithic* means stone. So the Palaeolithic is the Old Stone Age, and the Stone Age covers the first three stages below.

<!-- STEP id="palaeolithic" -->
## Palaeolithic: the Old Stone Age

People lived by hunting and gathering, and they used simple stone tools such as handaxes, cleavers and choppers.

<!-- STEP id="mesolithic" -->
## Mesolithic: the Middle Stone Age

People used microliths, which are tiny stone tools. This age is a stage between hunting and gathering on one side and farming on the other.

<!-- STEP id="neolithic" -->
## Neolithic: the New Stone Age

People began farming and settled in villages. They domesticated animals, which means they kept and bred them, and they used polished stone tools.

<!-- STEP id="chalcolithic" -->
## Chalcolithic: the Copper and Stone Age

*Chalco* means copper. People used copper along with stone tools, which was the start of metallurgy: getting metal and shaping it.

<!-- STEP id="bronze" -->
## Bronze Age

Bronze is made by mixing copper and tin. Trade and towns grew in this age, and early civilisations appeared.

<!-- STEP id="iron" -->
## Iron Age

Iron came into wide use once people could smelt it, which means heating rock to get the metal out. Tools and weapons became stronger.

<!-- STEP id="junction" -->
## The Turning Point

Look back along the line and find where it turns. The shift from gathering food to producing it is called the **Neolithic Revolution**.

Producing food could give a surplus, which means more than people need at once. Your textbook says that surplus laid the foundation for civilisation. That path runs through this whole chapter: hunting and gathering, farming, surplus, the first civilisations.

**The ages follow what people could make, and the Neolithic is the turning point.**
