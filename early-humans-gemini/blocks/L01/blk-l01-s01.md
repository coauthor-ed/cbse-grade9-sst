# One Object, One Hour

<!-- STEP id="face" -->
## A Stone with No Label

Someone puts a stone on the table in front of you. There is no card beside it and no museum number, and nobody will say where it was picked up.

The pictures in this course are drawings, made to show each object clearly. Your textbook has photographs of many of the real ones.

**You have one hour with this stone. How much of its past can you work out?**

<!-- STEP id="profile" -->
## Somebody Shaped This

Turn it on its side. Flakes, which are thin chips of stone, have been struck off both faces, all the way round, until the whole piece comes to a point.

A stone knocked about in a river gets rounded, but it does not get worked on two faces to one plan.

**The first thing you can say is that a person made this.**

<!-- STEP id="edge" -->
## It Was Used

Now look along the cutting edge. It is nicked and blunted in patches, and one corner has been battered flat.

This damage looks different from the neat flaking that shaped the stone, so it probably came later, from work. Soon we will see how archaeologists test an idea like that.

<!-- STEP id="butt" -->
## Made for a Hand

The blunt end was left thick and unworked, and it curves.

There is no groove or socket for a handle, so it was most likely held in the palm.

<!-- STEP id="scale" -->
## Where the Stone Stops Helping

Held up, it fills a hand. It is quartzite, a hard, heavy, coarse-grained stone that breaks in a predictable way when struck.

That is a real result from one hour of looking. Now try four more questions on it:

- How old is it?
- Who made it?
- What was lying beside it?
- How far down was it?

**The answers to these lie in the place where it was found, not in the stone.**

<!-- STEP id="case" -->
## Four Questions for This Chapter

Objects made by people are called artefacts, and this chapter depends on them. It asks four questions:

- How did humans live before civilisations began?
- How did they communicate before writing was invented?
- How does archaeology help us understand the past?
- How did early civilisations interact with each other?

**For about 3 million years of that story nobody wrote anything down, so objects are nearly all we have.**
