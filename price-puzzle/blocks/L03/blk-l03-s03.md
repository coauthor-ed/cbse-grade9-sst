# Higher Price, More Mangoes Offered

<!-- STEP -->
## What Seller A did

He offers more. At ₹50 per kg he offers 1 kg, at ₹100 he offers 2 kg, and at ₹150 he offers 3 kg. The amount one seller offers at different prices is his **individual supply**, and this table is his supply schedule.

| Price per kg | Seller A offers |
|---|---|
| ₹50 | 1 kg |
| ₹100 | 2 kg |
| ₹150 | 3 kg |

<!-- STEP -->
## The supply curve

Plot the three rows on a graph, with price up the side and quantity along the bottom. A is 1 kg at ₹50, B is 2 kg at ₹100 and C is 3 kg at ₹150. Join them, and you get Seller A's **supply curve**, SS'. It slopes up from left to right.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Law of Supply

A higher price brings the seller more profit on each kilogram, so it is worth bringing more. A higher price also brings new sellers into the market.

**This is the Law of Supply: when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises, and when the price falls, it falls.** Price and quantity supplied move in the same direction.
