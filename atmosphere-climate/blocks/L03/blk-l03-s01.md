# One Beam, Two Patches

<!-- STEP -->
## The question the last lesson left standing

Two places, one map, one hot and one cold. The wind is already explained. The pressure is already explained. What is left standing is the lamp 150 million kilometres away.

So send it in. One beam of sunlight, a sphere, and two patches of ground for it to land on.

<!-- STEP -->
## The same light, two different patches

Turn the sphere so the beam strikes it in two places at once. The light arriving at each is the same light - the same beam, the same second, the same energy.

It does not arrive as two patches of the same size. Where the beam strikes straight on, the patch is small and round. Where it strikes at a slant, the patch is wide and flattened out.

The scene gives you the area of each patch as a number. That is all it gives you.

<!-- STEP -->
## The answer most people walk in with

Before the numbers, the guess. **The poles are cold because they are further from the Sun.**

It is a reasonable thought, and it is not what the two patches are showing you. The next slide is where it gets answered.

## Naming the thing

The energy the Earth intercepts from the Sun has a name, and the source puts it in the margin at exactly this point.

It is **insolation** - the incoming solar energy from the Sun that is intercepted by the Earth.

**Same beam. Same second. Two different patches of ground.** The next slide asks you which one is being worked harder.
