## Bay and Headland

Look at this coast from above. A **headland** is rock that sticks out into the sea, and a **bay** is the curved shore beside it. Beaches often form in bays, where the waves are gentle.

<!-- STEP -->
## Sand Moves Along the Shore

Waves often reach the shore at an angle. The swash runs up at that angle, and the backwash runs straight down the slope, so sand zigzags along the coast.

Press **Send waves** and follow one grain.

## A Sand Bar With a Free End

Sand collects along the shore of a bay as a **sand bar**. It can build out as a long tongue, with a river behind it. One end stays joined to the land, and the other is the **free end**. A sand bar joined to the land at one end is called a spit.

Some bars grow right across a bay. Chilika lagoon in Odisha lies behind one.
