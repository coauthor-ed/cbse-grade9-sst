# What the Later Hunters Made

The handle with its row of blades was one new idea among several. The people who made it left other new things in the ground.

<!-- STEP id="burin" -->
## The Burin, or Engraver

They made a new stone tool called a **burin**, or engraver. Its small point was used to engrave symbolic marks on bones and on shells, such as ostrich eggs. The zigzags on the bone points in this picture may be marks of that kind.

<!-- STEP id="beads" -->
## The First Beads

They were the first people to make beads of stone, bone and shell. They used pigments, which are coloured powders, to decorate their bodies, and they painted the walls of caves and rock shelters. The book calls this **symbolic communication**, which means passing on ideas through signs and pictures.

<!-- STEP id="arrow" -->
## The Bow and Arrow

They also invented the bow and arrow. Their parallel-sided blades and microblades were struck from rocks with a glassy texture, which are very sharp when freshly made. This drawing shows stone pieces tied along a wooden shaft. With new weapons like this they also hunted small game animals.

<!-- STEP id="spread" -->
## Across the World

The book's map calls this stage the Upper Palaeolithic. The book links these developments with our immediate ancestors, *Homo sapiens*. Between 50,000 and 12,000 years ago they gradually spread all over the world, including Australia and the Americas.

**Then, about 12,000 years ago, the climate itself began to change.**
