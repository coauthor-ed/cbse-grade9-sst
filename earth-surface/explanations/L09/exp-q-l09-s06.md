## The One Process, Held Across Six Observations

The whole question turns on whether you can keep a single idea in your head while six separate things are described to you.

That idea is **removal**. Slightly acid water travels through limestone, dissolves it, and carries the dissolved rock away. Everything on the plateau is what is left behind after that.

| Observation | What removal did there |
|---|---|
| Open cracks, no channel | Dissolution ran along the joints and widened them |
| Steep funnel open to the sky | A cavity below grew until its roof failed and the ground dropped in |
| Wide chamber underground | Passages along several cracks merged into one hollow |
| Narrow passage of squeezes | Dissolution is fastest where the rock is most broken, so that is where the route is |
| Cavern with daylight in one wall | The route's exit, and the reason the surveyors below are specks |
| Flat-topped hill beyond the plateau | Not on this ground at all - it is outside the process being asked about |

**A surface river cuts downward and leaves a valley with a floor. This agent cuts outward through the rock and leaves a set of hollows, passages and openings.**

The tell that no stream ever ran here is in the very first observation: the surface is flat and dry, with cracks and no channel. Nothing on it went anywhere.
