# Evidence of Social Mobility

<!-- STEP -->
## Rulers from different backgrounds

Varṇa and jāti were not always rigid social categories. Historians find considerable **social mobility**, which means movement within and across these categories.

Several rulers came from diverse social backgrounds. The seven ruling houses named in the scene are examples.

<!-- STEP -->
## Weavers with other skills

The **Mandsaur Stone Inscription** (473 CE) mentions a guild of silk weavers who migrated from Lata in Gujarat to Dashapura in Madhya Pradesh during the Gupta period. The guild was proficient not just in weaving but also in archery, astrology and other occupations.

<!-- STEP -->
## Brāhmaṇas as land managers

Several inscriptions, such as the **Karitalai copper-plate inscriptions** of Mahārāja Jayanātha (late 5th century CE), record brāhmaṇas as land managers.

<!-- STEP -->
## Evidence from Sangam literature

The **Tolkappiyam** refers to a four-fold division of society and mentions Arasar (kings), Vanigar (traders and merchants) and Velar (farmers), along with Antanar (brāhmaṇas). This classification seems to be based on occupation.

The **Pattinappalai** describes merchants, salt-makers, fishermen, artisans, agriculturists and warriors interacting actively.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the cases show

The ruling houses, Mandsaur and Karitalai show mobility within and across the categories. The Tamil texts go further: they suggest that society there was organised more on the basis of occupation and economic functions than on a rigid varṇa classification.

A limit also applies: movement by some people does not show that everyone could move.
