# The Election Commission of India

<!-- STEP -->
## Who Runs an Election?

India has over 96.8 crore eligible voters, and laws cannot run an election by themselves. The Constitution gives the **Election Commission of India (ECI)** the superintendence, direction and control of the whole process. In plain words, it plans, gives the orders and checks them.

The ECI was set up on **25 January 1950**, and it is:

- **autonomous**: it manages its own work
- **permanent**: it stays between elections
- **constitutional**: Articles 324 to 329 provide for it

<!-- STEP -->
## Six Elections

The ECI conducts elections to six bodies and posts:

- the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha
- the Vidhan Sabha and the Vidhan Parishad
- the President and the Vice President of India

Panchayats and municipal bodies are not on this list. Their elections are run by **State Election Commissions**, which are separate bodies.

## From the ECI to the Polling Station

One body cannot stand at every polling station, so its work passes down a chain.

In each State, the **Chief Electoral Officer** supervises election work. The ECI appoints this officer from among the civil servants, in consultation with the State Government.

Below that, State Government officers such as District Magistrates and Tahsildars are given election duties, with titles such as District Election Officer and Returning Officer.

## Working With Other Countries

The ECI also works with election bodies abroad. Its **International Election Visitors' Programme** is for cooperation with the Election Management Bodies (EMBs) of other countries.

The ECI has signed agreements called MoUs with 28 EMBs and three international organisations: the International Foundation for Electoral Systems (IFES), International IDEA and the United Nations.
