# What Is Under Your Feet?

## The surface keeps changing

The Himalaya, a river valley and a sea coast all look fixed, but each one is changing. Each is a **landform**: a natural feature on the Earth's surface, formed by weathering, erosion, deposition or the movement of the Earth's crust.

Some of the forces that shape landforms act on the surface, and some act from inside the Earth.

## How do we know what is inside?

The centre of the Earth is about 6375 km below you, and the deepest hole ever drilled goes only about 12 km. So people have never seen the inside.

Scientists learn about it mainly from earthquake waves, which bend and change speed as they pass through different layers.

<!-- STEP -->
## Three main layers

The Earth has three main layers.

- **Crust:** the outermost layer, on which we live. It is 30-40 km thick under continents and only 5-7 km under the ocean.
- **Mantle:** about 2900 km thick, very hot, and mostly solid.
- **Core:** the innermost layer, extremely hot and heavy.

The crust is drawn thicker here so that you can see it. True to scale, it would be thinner than a line.

## Two parts of the core

The core has two parts, and they are very different.

- **Outer core:** about 2200 km thick, a fluid layer mainly of iron and nickel.
- **Inner core:** about 1250 km thick, a solid, hot, spinning metal ball. It is the densest part of the Earth.
