## What a Sea Stack Actually Looks Like

Every drawing so far has made a stack look like a chess piece.

Here is the real thing: **Rocher Perce**, on the Gulf of St Lawrence in Quebec, Canada. A limestone cliff with a natural arch cut clean through it - the same arch you just scrubbed through on the last slide - and standing just off its seaward tip, the tall detached pillar of rock that is the same rock left over.

Look at the scale of it. The pillar is a slab of rock many storeys high, and it stands alone in open water with no rock behind it at all.

**A sea stack is not a doodle in a diagram. It is a piece of a continent's coastline, standing in the sea on its own, and the reason it is still standing is a race the sea has not finished yet.**

Every other landform in this lesson is worth looking at in a photograph too. This one is worth it most, because the whole sequence - hollow, arch, stack, stump - is compressed into a single frame of one coastline somewhere, and you can pick out each stage of it yourself.
