# How Voters Get to Know Candidates

<!-- STEP -->
## Through the Parties

Voters learn about candidates mainly through the **political parties**. A ballot carries names and symbols, not plans, and the ECI runs the election without writing anyone's promises.

Most candidates belong to a party, though some contest independently. Through the parties, voters learn about each candidate's policies, vision for development, and positions on social, economic, cultural and political issues.

**A party tells voters what a name on the ballot stands for.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Three Things a Party Does in a Campaign

- It presents its **agenda**, the list of things it wants to do.
- It elaborates on its promises in **election rallies**.
- It discusses and debates issues through **media platforms**: issues of local, state, national and international concern.

<!-- STEP -->
## You Have Heard Them Already

You will become eligible to vote in another four or five years. Even now, you must have heard or read promises that parties make during election campaigns.

Think of one promise you remember from a poster, a rally or a newspaper. Who made it, and who was it for?

A party does much more than print names, though, and its work reaches well beyond the street where a rally is held.
