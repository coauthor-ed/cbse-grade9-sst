## Wind Lowers the Ground

Wind also takes loose material straight off the desert floor. Press **Blow the sand away** and the ground drops.

The shallow bowl it leaves is a **deflation hollow**, also called a **blowout**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Down to the Water

Below a certain depth the ground is soaked with water. The top of this wet layer is the **water table**.

Wet sand sticks together, so the wind cannot lift it and the hollow stops deepening here. Water now lies close to the surface and plants can grow. This green place in the desert is an **oasis**. Many oases also get their water from springs and wells.

<!-- STEP -->
## Where the Hollow Stays Dry

Other patches never reach water. There the wind blows away the fine grains between the stones, and the stones settle closer together.

The flat, stony surface left behind is a **desert pavement**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why People Settle Here

In a dry land, people live and farm where they can reach water. So these landforms decide where villages stand and where crops grow.
