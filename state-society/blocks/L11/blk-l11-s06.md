# Guilds

<!-- STEP -->
## People of one trade

A **guild** (**śhreṇī**) was an association of people in the same profession, craft or occupation. Guilds helped trade and commerce to expand, especially after the rise of the mahājanapadas in the 6th century BCE.

Metal coins, beginning with silver punch-marked coins, came into use in this period too.

<!-- STEP -->
## Eighteen types

The **Jātaka** literature refers to eighteen types of guilds. This suggests that guilds were well-established and influential in the economic life of early India.

<!-- STEP -->
## Quality, price and conduct

Tap **Price** and **Court** to see more of a guild's work. Guilds regulated the quality of goods and often fixed prices to protect both artisans and consumers.

Guild courts supervised the conduct of members and enforced professional standards.

## Banks

**Bank** is lit. Guilds also worked as banks, financiers and trustees. A trustee is someone who looks after money on behalf of others.

**So people left money with guilds, as a cave inscription at Nāśhik shows.**
