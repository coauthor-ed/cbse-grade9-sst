# Where India Uses Proportional Representation

<!-- STEP -->
## Not Every Election Uses FPTP

Elections to the **Rajya Sabha**, and those of the **President** and the **Vice President**, are conducted using proportional representation.

So India uses two kinds of rule, and each one is used for different elections.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Vidhan Parishad

Six States have two houses in their legislature: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh. The Upper House is the **Vidhan Parishad**. Its members come by three routes:

- elected by members of the Vidhan Sabha and local bodies
- elected by graduates and teachers in the State
- nominated by the Governor from fields such as art, science, social service, literature and co-operatives

<!-- STEP -->
## The Quota

The Vidhan Parishad uses a **single transferable vote**. Voters mark their preferences: first choice, second choice and so on. To win, a candidate needs a number of votes called the **quota**.

**Quota = [Total valid votes ÷ (Seats to be filled + 1)] + 1**

With 300 votes and 2 seats, the quota is 101. Three candidates would need 303 votes, so only 2 can reach it. The quota lets every seat fill, with no extra winners.

## First Preferences

Here is a made-up count with 300 votes, 2 seats and a quota of 101. First preference votes are counted for all candidates.

Candidate A has 110, B has 80, C has 65 and D has 45. A has reached 101, so **A is elected**. One seat is still empty, and nobody else has reached the quota.

## Transfer of Votes

In this simplified count, the candidate with the lowest votes is eliminated. That is D. D's ballots now go to the second preference marked on them.

Here 25 of D's votes go to B and 20 go to C. B rises from 80 to 105 and passes the quota, so **B is elected**. Both seats are filled, and the count ends.
