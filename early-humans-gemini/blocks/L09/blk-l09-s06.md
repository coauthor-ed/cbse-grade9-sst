# Goods on the Move

<!-- STEP id="tablets" -->
## What Sargon's Tablets Say

One of the most important Akkadian kings was Sargon. His cuneiform tablets talk about trade with three places to the east: Dilmun, Magan and Meluhha. Dilmun and Magan are today's Bahrain and the Oman peninsula, and Meluhha is generally identified with the Sindhu-Sarasvati civilisation.

<!-- STEP id="goods" -->
## What Moved Between Them

The book lists what was traded with the Harappans: beads of semi-precious stone, ivory, timber, gold dust and probably copper. Beads are small and valuable, so they were worth carrying a long way.

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## Why We Know This

Why "generally identified"? No tablet draws a map, so scholars match the name to the goods and the route, and that is reasoning, not proof.

**This trade is named in Mesopotamian writing. The Harappan script is unread, so we have only one side of the story.**
