# A River That Runs North

<!-- STEP id="north" -->
## Upper Means Upstream

The land is higher in the south, and the Nile flows downhill from south to north. So Upper Egypt is the upstream part, and Lower Egypt is the downstream part near the sea. It is a fair guess that "upper" means higher on a map, but the names follow the river.

<!-- STEP id="flood" -->
## The Yearly Flood

Every summer the river rose and spread over the plain. These annual floods are called the inundation, and farmers could not stop them.

<!-- STEP id="silt" -->
## What the Water Left

When the water went down, it left a layer of rich black mud on the fields. This is the kemet that gave the country its name, and it came back fresh every year.

<!-- STEP id="ditches" -->
## Steering the Water

About 5,000 years ago, farmers found that by digging ditches they could lead Nile water into their fields. They also stored water in reservoirs for later use.

**The flood came by itself, but people decided where the water went.**
