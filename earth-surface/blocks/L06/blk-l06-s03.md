## Waves Cut the Foot of the Rock

Waves also wear land away. This is **coastal erosion**. Between **low tide** and **high tide**, waves undercut the foot of a headland, and the rock above breaks off. This leaves a **sea cliff**, a steep rock face.

Press **Waves work** to watch it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Weak Rock Gives Way

Rock is not equally strong everywhere. Waves wear away the weak parts faster and open a hollow called a **sea cave**. A headland has two sides, and caves can form on both.

Look at the view from above.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two Caves Meet

When caves on opposite sides of a headland meet, they make a **sea arch**: a hole through the rock with a roof above it. Waves keep cutting at it and thinning the roof.
