# Media provenance - L04

Per `docs/copyright-and-sourcing.md`: never extract a textbook's own
illustrations or photographs; source an openly-licensed equivalent and
record its source and licence here.

**Neither of these two image files exists yet.** Both blocks that reference
them (`blk-l04-s04` and `blk-l04-s05`) carry them only as `reveal.images`
entries, pointing at files a later pass has to download. Nothing was
downloaded and nothing was generated at this stage. Both are **real
photographs of real places** - neither may be generated or synthesised, and
neither may be a scan of the textbook page.

The two stand in for the source's `Fig. 2.9` (p.8 / book p.20), the
chapter's own two-panel contrast between erosion by water and erosion by
wind. The textbook's printed photographs are **not** to be extracted.

## gully-erosion-running-water.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L04/blk-l04-s04.json` (`reveal.images.gully`, beat 1) and
  `blocks/L04/blk-l04-s05.json` (`reveal.images.gully`, beat 1). The same
  photograph appears on two consecutive slides on purpose: s04 shows it as
  one of the two states erosion produces, and s05 shows it again as the field
  underneath - the topsoil that fed a crop, now in the bottom of a ravine.
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.9` panel (a) - water erosion.
- To produce: download a Creative Commons or public-domain photograph of
  gully erosion / sheet erosion on a bare hillside from Wikimedia Commons or
  another open-licence repository. Save it here and record below: source
  page URL, file name on the source, author if credited, and the exact
  licence.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.9: a close view of **deep
  incised gully erosion** - a steep-walled ravine cut through bare brown soil
  and weathered rock slopes, with **washed sediment lying along the gully
  floor**. Two details are load-bearing: the walls must be visibly *steep*
  and *incised* (this is what "erodes" looks like, and s04's prose contrasts
  it with wind's lifted dust), and **nothing may be growing on either
  slope** (s05's slide is about a field that no longer has soil on it, so a
  vegetated frame would contradict the prose).

## wind-dust-haze-arid-plain.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L04/blk-l04-s04.json` (`reveal.images.dust`, beat 2).
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.9` panel (b) - wind erosion.
- To produce: download a Creative Commons or public-domain photograph of a
  wind erosion dust storm / dust haze over arid or semi-desert country from
  Wikimedia Commons. Save it here and record below: source page URL, author
  if credited, and the exact licence.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.9: a **wide landscape**
  photograph of arid savanna or semi-desert plain with **scattered
  flat-topped (umbrella acacia) trees engulfed in a dense haze of wind-lifted
  dust and sand**. The haze must be **dense enough to obscure the trees
  partly** - that is what makes "wind lifting the lightest material" visible
  rather than merely asserted - and the frame must be **wide**, not a close-up
  of one tree, because the slide's point is a whole surface being worked over
  at once.
