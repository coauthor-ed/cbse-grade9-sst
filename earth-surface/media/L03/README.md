# Media provenance - L03

Per `docs/copyright-and-sourcing.md`: never extract a textbook's own
illustrations or photographs; source an openly-licensed equivalent and
record its source and licence here.

**None of these four image files exists yet.** Three blocks carry an
`image.src` and one carries a `reveal.images` entry, all pointing at files a
later pass has to download. Nothing was downloaded and nothing was generated
at this stage. All four are **real photographs of real events and places** -
none may be generated or synthesised, and none may be a scan of the textbook
page.

## bhuj-2001-earthquake-damage.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L03/blk-l03-s04.json` (`image.src`)
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.5` (p.5 / book p.17) - the urban damage
  in Bhuj / Kachchh, Gujarat after the earthquake of 2001. The textbook's own
  printed photograph is **not** to be extracted; a comparable openly-licensed
  photograph of the same event is required.
- To produce: download a Creative Commons or public-domain photograph of the
  2001 Gujarat earthquake damage in Bhuj from Wikimedia Commons or another
  open-licence repository. Save it here and record below: source page URL,
  file name on the source, author if credited, and the exact licence.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.5: a street filled to the
  rooftops with shattered masonry and twisted reinforcement in the
  foreground; multi-storey concrete buildings behind, still standing, with
  exterior walls collapsed and floors hanging over open rooms.

## ndma-landslide-safety-poster.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L03/blk-l03-s04.json` (`reveal.images.ndma.src`)
- Stands in for the source's `NDMA Landslide Poster` (p.26 / book p.38) - the
  National Disaster Management Authority's "LANDSLIDE - Ready Now to stay
  secure" poster, with a rockslide photograph above three columns of guidance.
  A **government advisory poster is published for public use**; it is not a
  textbook illustration, so it is a fair substitute rather than a reproduction
  - but **download it from NDMA's own site or a government mirror, not from
  the textbook page**, and record the URL and the terms.
- To produce: fetch the current NDMA landslide-safety poster from
  `ndma.gov.in` (or a state disaster-management authority mirror of it), save
  it here whole - do not crop the NDMA logo, the hazard emblem or the helpline
  number - and record source URL and terms below.
- What the slide needs: three columns headed **Before**, **During** and
  **After**, with the helpline visible. The slide's whole point is the shape of
  that three-column arrangement, so a poster with the columns cropped away is
  useless here.

## volcanic-ashfall-village.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L03/blk-l03-s07.json` (`image.src`)
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.6` (p.6 / book p.18) - a village under a
  uniform blanket of fine grey ash, a tree's branches bowed down under it.
  The textbook's own photograph is **not** to be extracted.
- To produce: download an openly-licensed ashfall photograph (Pinatubo 1991,
  Sinabung, Merapi or similar) from Wikimedia Commons. Save it here and
  record source URL, author and licence below.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.6: a village street with
  wooden and tin-roofed houses and surrounding trees completely submerged
  under grey ash, one evergreen in the foreground with branches bowed down, a
  hillside behind coated the same grey, and a dim ash-choked sky. The bow in
  the branches is load-bearing - it is what makes "heavy enough to bend a
  tree" visible - so pick a frame that has it.

## baratang-mud-volcano.jpg

- Used by: `blocks/L03/blk-l03-s08.json` (`image.src`)
- Stands in for the source's `Fig. 2.7` (p.7 / book p.19) - the mud volcano
  on Baratang Island, Andaman and Nicobar Islands. The textbook's own
  photograph is **not** to be extracted.
- To produce: download an openly-licensed photograph of the Baratang mud
  volcano from Wikimedia Commons. Save it here and record source URL, author
  and licence below.
- What the slide needs, from `FIGURES.md` Fig. 2.7: a broad low dome of grey
  mud with a **central bubbling vent at its summit** - the vent is the slide's
  argument, so a wide shot without it will not do - fresh wet mud flowing down
  the slopes, a dry deeply cracked polygonal mud crust around the perimeter,
  and a rustic wooden fence with palms beyond.
- Note: the slide's claim that this is India's only mud volcano is the
  **source's own caption**, carried in the prose as the chapter's claim. A
  reviewer who knows of a counter-example should check the wording; the prose
  says "the chapter's caption claims something worth checking", which is
  deliberately hedged.
