# Who Organises the Digging?

<!-- STEP id="alone" -->
## Too Big for One Family

One household can dig a short ditch. But a ditch that brings water from the Nile to many fields, and the dams that go with it, take many people working together.

<!-- STEP id="government" -->
## Where Local Government May Have Begun

Someone had to decide who digs, where and when. The book says these shared efforts must have led to local government and an administrative class, a group whose work is to organise. This was probably the earliest form of local government in Egypt.

<!-- STEP id="early" -->
## A Long Timeline

Here is the order of time from the book. The Neolithic began around 5500 BCE, the Chalcolithic around 4000 BCE, and the Bronze Age around 3100 BCE. The ditches and the first city-states came at about 3000 BCE, near the start of the Bronze Age.

<!-- STEP id="kingdoms" -->
## The Three Kingdoms

Historians divide the long years after that into three great periods:

- the **Old Kingdom**, 2686 to 2181 BCE
- the **Middle Kingdom**, 2030 to 1650 BCE
- the **New Kingdom**, 1570 to 1069 BCE

Over time, powerful rulers called pharaohs emerged. The next lesson asks why they spent so much labour on keeping one dead body whole.
