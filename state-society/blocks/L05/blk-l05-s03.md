# A Kingdom Divided for Convenience

<!-- STEP -->
## What decentralisation meant

The second answer is right. **For administrative convenience** the kingdom was divided into several provinces, and these into smaller units.

Each level had its own officers and its own work, and the king remained the supreme head.

Decentralisation means that work and decisions are shared out among levels, while the king stays supreme.

<!-- STEP -->
## Province and division

The north and the south used different names for the same level. Tap a level to see both.

- **Province**: **bhukti** in the north, **manḍala** or **manḍalam** in the south.
- **Division**: **viṣhaya** or **bhoga** in the north, **koṭṭam** or **valanāḍu** in the south.

<!-- STEP -->
## District, group of villages, village

Three lower levels follow, and the village is the lowest unit of all.

- **District**: **adhiṣhṭhāna** or **paṭṭana** in the north, **nāḍu** in the south.
- **Group of villages**, like a modern **tahsīl**: **vithi** in the north, **paṭṭalā** or **kūṛram** in the south.

To govern all these units, kingdoms built up a large body of officers, with provincial governors and district officers among them.

## Compare it with India today

Think about two questions. How would dividing a kingdom into provinces, districts and villages have helped rulers manage their empires? What similarities can you see with the way India is governed today?

- **For the ruler:** an officer who lives in a district can see its problems, which the king cannot do from far away.
- **For the people:** a nearby office may be easier to reach than a distant capital.
- **For today:** India has states, districts, tahsīls and villages. Compare the shape, because the powers and the methods of choosing officers are not the same.
