## One stock, one fleet

This model is made up, and it uses the same fish stock as before: 100,000 fish, growing by 30% a year before the catch. Now the stock lies in open sea, and any fleet may fish it.

One fleet takes 30,000 a year. The stock grows to 130,000, 100,000 are left, and the line stays flat.

<!-- STEP -->
## Three fleets

Three fleets arrive, and each takes the same 30,000. Together they take 90,000, so only 40,000 are left after the first year. In the second year the stock grows to 52,000 and the fleets take all of it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why shared fish run out

Each fleet took an amount that would have been safe alone. Together they emptied the stock, and no single country could tell them to stop.

This is why equal rights over a shared sea are not enough. Countries have to agree on rules, and that is what the rest of this lesson is about.
