# Naming the Line-up

<!-- STEP id="habilis" -->
## The Handy Man

*Homo habilis* means "handy man". It lived in Africa, especially around Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania and Kenya, more than 2 million years ago, and it made simple chopper tools. A chopper is a heavy stone with one rough cutting edge.

<!-- STEP id="erectus" -->
## The Upright One

*Homo erectus* means an upright human ancestor, one that walked on two legs. It lived in the eastern African Rift Valley about 2 million years ago, and it invented handaxes and cleavers.

A handaxe is a stone shaped to a point on both faces, for cutting. A cleaver is a stone with a broad, straight edge, for chopping and splitting.

<!-- STEP id="neanderthal" -->
## A Close Relative

*Homo neanderthalensis* lived in Europe and southwest Asia until about 40,000 years ago. It made Middle Palaeolithic flake tools, which are tools struck from thin flakes of stone.

Neanderthals are close relatives of ours rather than our direct ancestors.

<!-- STEP id="sapiens" -->
## Us

*Homo sapiens* is our own species. It developed complex technologies, spread all over the earth, and is the only one of the four still living.

<!-- STEP id="overlap" -->
## Not a Relay

You might expect each species to end just as the next begins, but look at the bars on the time scale. For long stretches, several of them were alive at the same time.

**Evolution is not a relay race, with one runner handing over to the next.**

<!-- STEP id="earlier" -->
## The Line Starts Earlier

Before *Homo habilis* came the australopithecines. The name has two parts: *australis* means southern, and *pithecus* means primate, the group that includes apes and humans. Names like this are hard for everyone at first.

The earliest stone tools are about 3.3 million years old, which is older than any of the four skulls. So the drawer holds only the last part of a much longer line.

<!-- STEP id="twoclocks" -->
## Two Kinds of Change

Two kinds of change run side by side in this story.

- Biological evolution: bodies changing shape slowly, over millions of years.
- Cultural evolution: tools and ways of living changing as humans adapted to a changing climate during the Quaternary Period, the last 26 lakh years up to the present.

**Bodies changed slowly, while tools and techniques changed much faster.**
