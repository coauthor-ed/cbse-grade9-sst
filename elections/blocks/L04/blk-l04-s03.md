# You Have to Be on the Roll

<!-- STEP -->
## Your Name Comes First

Your name must be added to the electoral roll, because only those whose names appear on the roll are allowed to vote.

It is natural to think that turning 18 is enough, but it only makes you able to join the roll. No police station or political party decides this.

<!-- STEP -->
## Who Can Be on the Roll?

Only citizens who meet the eligibility criteria can vote. You studied these in Grade 8. A voter must be:

- a citizen of India
- 18 years old or above
- not disqualified by law

You meet all three, so nothing stops your name from going on.

<!-- STEP -->
## How a Name Gets There

The ECI sends official **enumerators** to every household to collect data about eligible electors.

From this data, a roll is prepared for each constituency and organised **polling station-wise**. Each polling station has its own part of the roll, so the officer there can find your name.

You will turn 18 in a few years. Find out the steps to register and the documents you need, and ask someone who has just registered how easy it was.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two Mistakes a Roll Can Make

A roll can go wrong in two ways. If it misses an eligible citizen, that citizen cannot vote. If it keeps a name that should not be there, it holds an entry for someone who has no right to vote.

**A good roll leaves out no eligible citizen and includes no ineligible person.**
