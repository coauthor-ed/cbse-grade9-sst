# One Part in Six

<!-- STEP -->
## A village from above

The Mauryan state developed an elaborate system to regulate and supervise economic activities. Look at one village: the farmland is divided into individual holdings, pastures are kept in common for grazing cattle, and groves stand on the outskirts.

<!-- STEP -->
## The tax on land

The basic tax on land was a fixed proportion of the produce, generally **one-sixth**. So out of every six sacks of grain, one went to the state.

**Good year** is lit, so count the sacks.

<!-- STEP -->
## A bad year

**Bad year** is lit. The harvest falls, and the state's sacks fall with it, because the tax is a share and not a fixed amount.

The share stays at one part in six, but the state now receives less grain. This gave the state a strong reason to care about water.

<!-- STEP -->
## Clearing and protecting forests

The state encouraged agricultural expansion by clearing forests. More farmland meant more produce.

Certain categories of forests, however, were protected by law.
