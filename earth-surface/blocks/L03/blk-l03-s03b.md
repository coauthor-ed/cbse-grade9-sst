# What Happens in an Earthquake?

<!-- STEP -->
## Rock locks, then breaks

Plates keep moving, but the rock at a fault is locked. Strain builds up for years. When the rock can hold no more, it breaks and slips.

This sudden slip is an earthquake.

<!-- STEP -->
## Focus and epicentre

The place underground where the rock breaks is the **focus**, also called the earthquake origin. The point on the surface straight above it is the **epicentre**. Shaking is often strongest near the epicentre.

<!-- STEP -->
## Waves and magnitude

Waves spread out from the focus in all directions, and they shake the ground.

The size of an earthquake is its **magnitude**. Each step up on the scale means about 32 times more energy. Press **Step up** to compare.

<!-- STEP -->
## Can we predict an earthquake?

No. Scientists cannot say the day or hour of an earthquake. They can say which places are likely to have one over many years, and these places must prepare.
