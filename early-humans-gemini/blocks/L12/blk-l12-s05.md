# Hopes, Desires and Fears

A record of questions has a real use. The book says the oracles tell us a lot about the hopes, desires and fears of the early Chinese.

<!-- STEP id="cracked-bone" -->
## What the King Asked

An inscription might ask something like this: will the rain come in time for the millet? Will the enemy raid the western border? People ask about what they do not know, so each question marks something uncertain.

<!-- STEP id="char-closeup" -->
## What Shows Through

Read many of these questions and a pattern appears. There is hope for rain and a good harvest, desire for a safe birth, and fear of drought, illness and attack.

<!-- STEP id="modern-transcription" -->
## Why the Gap Helps

The bones rarely say how anything turned out, and you might think that makes them weak evidence. In fact the gap has a use. A question asked before the event shows a worry, and later writers who knew the ending had little reason to keep it.

<!-- STEP id="historiography" -->
## Two Kinds of Record

Later, official historians recorded events in China, so the country has an abundance of written records. Those records tell what happened, in the way the writer chose to tell it. You saw why that needs care in the story of the Qin.

**The bones show what rulers feared before anything happened.**
