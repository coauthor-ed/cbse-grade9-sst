## Yes, because of the fish

India may stop the boat from fishing, although the water there is not India's. At 100 nautical miles the boat is in India's **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**. India has special rights over the fish there, but it does not fully own the water.

To see how this works, cut through the sea and look at it from the side, with the land on the left.

<!-- STEP -->
## The baseline

Every distance at sea is measured from one line, the **baseline**. Many people picture the shoreline, but the shore moves with the tide. On most coasts the baseline follows the low-water line, and in places it runs straight across bays and between headlands.

The water on the land side of this line is called **internal waters**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two bands near the coast

The **territorial sea** reaches 12 nautical miles from the baseline. There the country has near-complete control, as it has on land.

The **contiguous zone** reaches 24 nautical miles from the baseline. There the country has limited control, over customs, fiscal matters and illegal immigration.

<!-- STEP -->
## Out to 200 nautical miles

The EEZ reaches 200 nautical miles from the baseline, under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), a global agreement. Control of the sea and rights over its resources are two different things. In the EEZ a country has rights over fish, oil and gas, often called sovereign rights, while other countries' ships may pass peacefully.

**Rights over the fish do not make the water India's.**
