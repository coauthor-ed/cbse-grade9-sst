# Wealth with a Condition

<!-- STEP -->
## Four goals of life

The texts name four goals of life, the **puruṣhārthas**: **dharma** (righteousness), **artha** (material well-being), **kāma** (fulfilment of desires) and **mokṣha** (liberation from all worldly ties). Their fulfilment was held to be important for every human being.

Do dharma and wealth pull against each other? Two cases will show.

<!-- STEP -->
## Case one: fair trade

**Fair trade** is lit. The trader uses true weights, so his buyers get the grain they paid for and he still makes a profit.

Both lamps burn here. He gains wealth, and dharma guides how he gains it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Case two: false weights

**False weights** is lit. The same trader hides a light weight in the pan, so every buyer gets less grain than they paid for.

He earns more money, but the artha lamp goes out, because this wealth harms the people around him.

**The pursuit of wealth is desirable, provided it is guided by dharma, that is, aligned with the welfare of society.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Direction and purpose

Dharma and mokṣha hold particular significance from an ethical point of view, as they give direction and purpose to human life.
