# Clues That Add Up

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## No Single Find Proves It

The bins look like the answer, because it seems that only farmers would store food. That is a fair first guess, but foragers can store food too: the last lesson showed that hunter-gatherers gathered wild grain. So bins show that people stored food, and they do not show where the food came from.

**Each find tells us something, and each find has a weakness.**

<!-- STEP id="zebu" -->
## The Bones

Bones alone could be left by hunters. The clue is which animals they came from and how old the animals were, because herds and hunts leave different mixes. The bones here are of sheep, goats and Indian cattle, including the humped zebu bull.

<!-- STEP id="beads" -->
## The Beads

Ornaments of lapis lazuli, carnelian and shell tell a different story. These materials probably came from other places, so they point to contact with other people. They say nothing about how anybody got food.

<!-- STEP id="grain" -->
## The Grain

Charred grain shows what people ate. It also carries a stronger clue, because farmed wheat and barley look different from wild kinds. A wild ear drops its grains when they are ripe, and a farmed ear keeps them until harvest.

<!-- STEP id="compound" -->
## The Whole Village on One Plan

Now put the finds back where they lay. Houses of sun-dried brick, bins beside them and graves for the dead all sit on one plan. Tap each find to see what it shows and what it does not show.

<!-- STEP id="together" -->
## Clues Taken Together

Each clue alone is weak, but together they point the same way. They support the book's statement: the people of Mehrgarh cultivated wheat and barley, and raised sheep, goats and Indian cattle.

**Several weak clues can add up to one strong answer.**
