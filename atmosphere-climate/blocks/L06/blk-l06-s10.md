# Delhi, 30 June. Shillong, 1 June.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two places, either end of the sweep

Look back at the first isochrone the map drew.

**1 June** runs along the southern tip - and, at the other end of the same line, into the north-east: Assam and **Meghalaya**, where Shillong sits. The date you wrote at the top of this slide is the date the line carries.

Now find the other one. **30 June** runs across Haryana, **Delhi**, Punjab and Himachal Pradesh.

Almost a month apart. On the map, Shillong and Delhi are as far apart as any two places in the country.

Now look at what the table beside the map says about the rain each of them actually gets in a year.

**Shillong gets more than three times what Delhi gets.** Shillong is the wettest of the ten stations in that table; Delhi is near the dry end.

## The isochrones cannot answer this

Everything so far has been about a wind moving - where it came from, which way it went, which day it arrived.

This pair says that is not the whole story. **Two places on the same map, on the same side of the country, arriving a month apart, and one of them is the wettest station in the table.**

Whatever makes that difference is not in the isochrones, and not in the direction the wind was blowing.
