# How China Was Run

China was now far too large for one family to manage by itself. How do you run a country like that? The book mentions four ideas.

<!-- STEP id="mandate" -->
## Heaven's Appointee

The Zhou rulers were both kings and priests. People believed that heaven appointed them, but a ruler could be dismissed when his people did not prosper. This idea is called the **Mandate of Heaven**. It explained why the Zhou had the right to replace the Shang, and it gave people a test for any king: are the people doing well?

<!-- STEP id="exam" -->
## Officials Chosen by Examination

Public officials were chosen with great care. They were examined in five things: archery, horsemanship, calculations, writing and music. The book mentions this in its account of the Zhou. It also says China was the first country to develop civil services through public examination, and it gives no date for that.

<!-- STEP id="money" -->
## Metal and Paper Money

A metal-based medium of exchange appeared during the Zhou dynasty, and a money economy developed by the 5th century BCE. Paper money came much later, and the book says China was the first country to introduce it.

<!-- STEP id="society" -->
## Who Stood Where

Society had layers long before this. By 1500 BCE the ruling class, nobles and aristocrats were at the top, followed by farmers and labourers. The book says this laid the foundation of the later Chinese social order.

**A test for kings, examinations for officials, and money: these three helped to hold a large state together.**

Four civilisations now stand side by side, and they have much in common. Did they copy each other? The last lesson asks that question.
