# Heated Until It Cracks

This is the flat shoulder blade of an ox, in a drawing made for this course. Columns of small signs are cut into the bone, and they are more than 3,000 years old.

<!-- STEP id="incised-columns" -->
## The Oldest Source

These signs are not decoration, and they are not a list of goods. The book says such pieces are the earliest source of information about China.

<!-- STEP id="tortoise-plastron" -->
## Ox Bones and Tortoise Shells

People also used the flat underside of tortoise shells. The book calls these marked pieces "oracles". An oracle is an answer that people believe comes from a god or a spirit, so historians call the pieces **oracle bones**.

<!-- STEP id="crack-pattern" -->
## Heat and Cracks

A diviner is a person whose work is to ask such questions. Diviners cut shallow hollows into the back of the bone and then pressed a hot rod against a hollow. The bone cracked with the heat.

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## Reading the Cracks

The diviner then read the pattern of the cracks as an answer. The book says these oracles were often used to foretell the future.

**China's oldest written records are questions asked through fire.**
