# The Weight You Cannot Feel

<!-- STEP -->
## Every centimetre of it, on your head

You have just climbed a hundred kilometres of air. All of it is still there, and all of it is still sitting on you.

Stand a column one square centimetre across on top of your head and it weighs about **10 newtons** - roughly a kilogram pressing down on a fingernail. That is not the whole weight of you; it is the weight of the air above one small patch of you, and there are a great many small patches. Add up the whole column and your shoulders are carrying about a tonne.

<!-- STEP -->
## There is a switch, and it is unpleasant

The scene has one control. Throw it and the **push from inside** goes - the force your own body exerts outward on its own fluids, which has been there your whole life without ever being noticeable.

Whatever that switch does, it does not do it gently.

## The question this chapter has been walking towards

Everything so far has described the air as a *stuff*: a blanket, a jar, a set of bands, a hundred kilometres of it. And you have been carrying the whole column, this entire time, without noticing once.

**Why can you not feel any of it?**
