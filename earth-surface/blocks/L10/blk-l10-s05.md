## A Lake Behind Loose Rock

As a glacier melts back, water can collect behind a natural dam of ice or loose moraine. Press **Warmer years**. Faster melting makes the lake bigger and deeper, and it presses harder on the dam.

<!-- STEP -->
## When the Dam Fails

Heavy rain or snow can add more water. An earthquake, avalanche or landslide may strike the lake or weaken the dam.

Press **Dam fails**. The stored water leaves all at once. This is a **glacial lake outburst flood**, or **GLOF**, and it does its damage in the valley downstream.

<!-- STEP -->
## Where, and What Helps

Glacial lakes in the high Himalaya are the places at risk. In October 2023, South Lhonak Lake in Sikkim burst its moraine dam and flooded the Teesta valley. In June 2013, heavy rain and the breach of Chorabari lake sent a flood down on Kedarnath in Rudraprayag district, Uttarakhand.

To lower the risk, people map risky lakes, drain them a little with pipes or channels, and set up warning sirens in the valley below.
