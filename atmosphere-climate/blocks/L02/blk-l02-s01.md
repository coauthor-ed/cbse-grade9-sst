# One Number, Going Up

<!-- STEP -->
## A climber with an instrument

A climber is going up through the air on a rope, carrying a thermometer. You can drag the rope yourself, or press **Run** and watch.

The only number on the display is **temperature**. So far it does exactly what you would expect: the higher the climber goes, the lower the reading.

<!-- STEP -->
## Then it stops going down

At about twelve kilometres the reading turns around. It does not level off. It starts climbing, and it keeps climbing for tens of kilometres while the climber is still on the way up.

You have been told your whole life that up is colder. That is true here. It is also not the whole truth, and the whole truth is what this lesson is for.

**Something is heating this air, and it is not the ground.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Your turn on the rope

Drag the climber back down to the ground, then up again, slowly. Watch for the altitude at which the number stops falling and starts to rise.

Mark it. You are going to need it.
