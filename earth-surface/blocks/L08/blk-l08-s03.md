## The Soft Rock Goes First

Wind can shape solid rock. The soft bands wear away into low hollows, and the hard bands stay standing as long ridges that run along the wind.

Each streamlined rock ridge carved by wind is a **yardang**. Press **Run the wind again** to watch it happen.

<!-- STEP -->
## Sand Does the Cutting

Moving air on its own hardly marks rock. The cutting is done by the sand it carries.

Grains hop low and strike the rock again and again, like sandpaper rubbing the same spot for thousands of years. Since most sand stays near the ground, the foot of a ridge is cut the most.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Stone Shaped by Sand

The same sandblasting works on a single stone. A rock polished and shaped this way is a **ventifact**. Its side facing the wind is rubbed smooth and flat, and its sheltered side stays rough.

Yardangs and ventifacts bring tourists and geologists to desert lands.
