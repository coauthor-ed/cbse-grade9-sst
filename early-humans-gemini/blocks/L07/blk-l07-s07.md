# What If Every Town Had Its Own Weight?

<!-- STEP id="haggling" -->
## Every Deal Becomes an Argument

The book asks you to imagine long-distance trade without a standard weight. Think of a kirana shop where every shopkeeper uses a different kilogram.

If two towns use different weights, "ten units of copper" means two different amounts. Each deal starts with an argument about whose weight is right, so people trade only with neighbours they already trust.

<!-- STEP id="distance" -->
## Orders From Far Away

Distance makes this worse. A trader in Harappa cannot ask for copper from far away if the sender and the receiver do not agree on what one unit means. By the time the goods arrive, it is too late to argue.

<!-- STEP id="sealed" -->
## One Weight Everywhere

With one system, the trader at the far end can check a load on a balance and get the same answer. Strangers can then trade with each other.

**A shared weight lets people trust goods from far away.**

We cannot read what Harappan traders wrote, so this is a reasoned guess at how trade worked. The weights themselves are the evidence.
