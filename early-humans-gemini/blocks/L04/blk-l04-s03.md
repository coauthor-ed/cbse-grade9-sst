# Parts, Not Tools

<!-- STEP id="verdict" -->
## The Answer: A Row in a Groove

These small blades were not held in the fingers. The book says that microblades were fixed in a handle. One reconstruction: cut a groove along a piece of wood or bone, set a row of blades in it, and fix them with gum from a tree.

**The blades were parts of a tool, not whole tools.**

<!-- STEP id="seat" -->
## Twelve Blades, One Edge

Watch the blades go into the groove one by one. Each blade adds its own length to the edge, so twelve blades of two centimetres give a cutting edge about 24 centimetres long.

<!-- STEP id="swap" -->
## Change One Part

When one blade goes dull or breaks, you pull out that blade and set a fresh one in the gap. The handle stays and the other eleven blades stay, so the tool keeps working. A big handaxe that snaps in two cannot be mended like this.

**You replace a part, not the whole tool.**
