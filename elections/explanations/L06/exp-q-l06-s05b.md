# Two Clauses, Two Consequences

<!-- STEP -->
## The definition has two halves

Read the chapter's sentence as two clauses joined by **or**:

- **abandoning or switching from** the political party **under whose banner the candidate was elected** - which may involve joining another party
- **or choosing to act independently of the party's decisions**, particularly in matters such as **voting in the legislature**

Her vote against the party's instructions is the second clause, word for word. She is inside the definition without ever leaving the room.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the law then does with it

The **Anti-Defection Law, 1985, 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act**, names two triggers for a member of the **Parliament** or a **State Assembly**:

- **voluntarily gives up party membership**, or
- **votes against the party's instructions** - the **whip**

Either one means the member **can be disqualified from the respective House**.

And the last clause is not a formality: **the Speaker or Chairman of the House decides** what needs to be done. The law hands a presiding officer the judgement, so the same vote can end differently in different Houses.

**Two clauses catch her, one officer decides.**
