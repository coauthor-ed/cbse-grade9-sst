# A Mountain Over One Grave

<!-- STEP id="mastaba" -->
## A Bench Over the Grave

After their death, pharaohs were buried deep underground. A rectangular structure called a mastaba was placed over the burial chamber. Mastaba means bench in Arabic, and that is what it looks like.

<!-- STEP id="step-pyramid" -->
## Mastabas One on Top of Another

Gradually, mastabas were placed one on top of the other to form a pyramid. The book's example is the Step Pyramid at Saqqara.

<!-- STEP id="tier-detail" -->
## Built in Stone

Mud brick wears away in time, but cut limestone can last for thousands of years. A tomb built for a ka that would live on after death needed to last.

<!-- STEP id="pyramid-sim" -->
## From Bench to Pyramid

Watch the tiers go up. Each one is a smaller mastaba on top of a bigger one, and six tiers make the Step Pyramid.

<!-- STEP id="security" -->
## Why Build It?

The book says pyramids were built mainly because of the ka: the body had to be kept safe. All that stone did not always work, because robbers tunnelled into most pyramids in the end.

**A pyramid was a huge effort to protect one ruler's body, and it often failed.**
