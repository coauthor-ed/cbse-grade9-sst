# Why Did People Obey?

Rulers took a share of other people's harvest and labour, year after year. Why did people accept this? Force is part of the answer, and belief is another part. Each answer below made sense to the people who lived with it.

<!-- STEP id="mesopotamia" -->
## Mesopotamia: The City's God

People believed that gods held power over floods and winds, so keeping a god content seemed vital for the whole city. The ziggurat was the god's house, and it also held the stored grain and goods. Giving to the temple could feel like protecting everyone.

<!-- STEP id="egypt" -->
## Egypt: The Pharaoh

Egyptians treated the pharaoh as a god on earth, and his duty was to keep the order of the world. A share of the harvest given to him could feel like a duty to that order.

<!-- STEP id="china" -->
## China: A Test for Kings

Zhou kings were believed to be appointed by heaven, and they could be dismissed when their people did not prosper. So the right to rule had a condition attached, and the condition could be checked in the fields.

<!-- STEP id="open-question" -->
## The Harappans: We Do Not Know

No palace or royal tomb has been found at Harappan cities, and no building that everyone agrees was a temple. We do not know who ruled them, and we cannot read their script.

**In three civilisations we can read a reason for obeying. In the fourth, the answer is still unknown.**
