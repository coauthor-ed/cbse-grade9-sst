# Four Big Questions

<!-- STEP -->
## How did society and state organise themselves?

You began with a clan, a chief and a fire. Early political organisation evolved from clans (janas) led by a rājā to larger kingdoms and republics, and later to powerful empires such as the Mauryas, Guptas and Cholas.

The rise of such states led to structured systems of administration, taxation, military organisation and governance.

<!-- STEP -->
## How did states and societies spread?

The structures of social, cultural and political life evolved from the Vedic period and took various forms across space and time. You saw this in the Ganga plains, the Deccan and Tamilakam.

<!-- STEP -->
## How did dharma and the chakravarti idea help create unity?

The ideal of a chakravarti samrāṭ led kings to think in terms of the whole subcontinent, even when their rule covered only a part of it. Dharma gave rulers and subjects a shared idea of duty and right conduct.

<!-- STEP -->
## How did groups take shape and come together?

Varṇas, jātis, village assemblies and guilds took shape over time. The economy was largely based on agriculture, supported by irrigation, while trade networks, ports and guilds helped commerce and crafts to grow.

Despite political changes, knowledge traditions, trade networks, and cultural and devotional practices such as Bhakti continued to flourish across regions.

## Before we move on

Indian civilisation demonstrates a remarkable continuity in its cultural, social and political traditions.

The developments of this period laid the groundwork for many enduring traditions that continue to shape the subcontinent's social and cultural life even today.
