# What "Domestication" Means

<!-- STEP id="notuse" -->
## Using Is Not Controlling

Hunter-gatherers used wild plants and animals for a very long time. They gathered wild grain and hunted game, but they did not decide how those plants and animals bred. **Domestication** is the step of bringing a plant or animal under human control. People chose which ones to raise, and over time this produced new breeds.

**To domesticate a plant is to control how it grows, not only to use it.**

<!-- STEP id="ring" -->
## Tools for Growing Food

Hunter-gatherers made tools for getting food. Neolithic farmers made tools for producing and processing it. A ring stone with a hole through it probably weighted a digging stick, and a polished celt could clear trees. None of these is shaped for cutting meat off a bone.

<!-- STEP id="quern" -->
## The Saddle Quern

A saddle quern is a large lower stone with a curved hollow worn into its top. Beside it lies a smaller hand-stone, which is rubbed back and forth over the grain.

<!-- STEP id="pot" -->
## The Clay Pot

Neolithic farmers also made a variety of earthenware pots in many shapes and sizes. Pottery is clay shaped by hand and hardened in a fire. A harvest is too large to eat at once, and a pot keeps the grain away from rain and rats. Pots were also used for carrying and boiling.

<!-- STEP id="grind" -->
## Grain to Flour

Watch the work of a farming day. The grain goes on the quern, the hand-stone moves back and forth, and flour slowly gathers at the edge of the stone. This work was done kneeling, for hours, and it had to be done again the next day.

<!-- STEP id="village" -->
## What These Tools Made Possible

Grain kept in pots lasts through the months when nothing grows. A family with stored food, fields to tend and heavy stones to grind with has good reasons to stay in one place. The book says that these farmers established the first village settlements.
