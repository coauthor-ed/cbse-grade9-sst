## Why the chapter keeps him

Varāhamihira's account has two halves, and they are worth more apart than together.

<!-- STEP -->
## The half that survives

Write down what happens when the ground shakes, before deciding why. That is what he did: changes in the wind, in the rain, in the clouds, in how animals behaved.

Every one of those is something a person could watch, and later someone else could check. The chapter keeps this half because a record of what happens is worth having even when the reason given for it turns out to be wrong.

<!-- STEP -->
## The half that does not

Vāyu, Agni, Indra, Varuṇa - four elements, each tied to a constellation. Nothing here can be tested by going outside and looking, and that is exactly the problem: an explanation you cannot check is not an explanation that can be improved.

The chapter calls the blend what it is, an attempt to combine real observation with cosmological reasoning.

<!-- STEP -->
## The lesson in the slide

**A careful record of what happens is worth keeping even when the reason given for it does not survive.**

Both halves are honest work. Only one of them was still useful six hundred years later.
