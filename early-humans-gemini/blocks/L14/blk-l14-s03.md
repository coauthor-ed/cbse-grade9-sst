# Who Was in Contact With Whom?

<!-- STEP id="gulf" -->
## Strong Contact: Mesopotamia and the Harappans

These two regions lie close to each other, and the book says this helped strong contacts and trade. You have seen the evidence: Akkadian tablets name Meluhha, and beads and other goods moved through Dilmun and Magan.

<!-- STEP id="silence" -->
## Little Evidence: Egypt and China

The book says Egypt and China have little tangible evidence of direct contact with the Sindhu-Sarasvati civilisation. Tangible means something real you could hold, such as a Harappan seal or weight found in Egypt.

<!-- STEP id="absence" -->
## Little Evidence Is Not No Contact

An object may be lost, or may not be dug up yet. So historians say "little evidence", not "no contact".

Four civilisations make six pairs, and the book speaks about three. This chapter does not discuss the other three. Egypt and Mesopotamia did in fact know each other, as other sources show.

<!-- STEP id="convergence" -->
## Separate Beginnings, Then Contact

Trade carried goods, but it did not make these civilisations copies. Their scripts look nothing alike, and their great buildings differ. You saw the same pattern with farming: several beginnings with local plants, and then spreading.

**The book says all four emerged independently. It also says Mesopotamia and the Harappans traded.**
