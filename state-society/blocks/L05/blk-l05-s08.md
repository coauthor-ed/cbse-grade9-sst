# The Village at the End

<!-- STEP -->
## An order that arrives

Press **Send the order** once more. The messenger leaves the capital, and at each level an officer takes the order and passes it on until it reaches the village.

The kingdom of this period was divided from province down to village, and the king stayed the supreme head from start to finish.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the village did for itself

The village did more than receive orders. Across kingdoms of this period, villages functioned relatively independently of the royal administration, through small committees called **variyams**.

Later kingdoms show the same pattern. The village stayed the smallest unit, and it was largely self-reliant in major practical work:

- infrastructure
- social welfare
- education

<!-- STEP -->
## Who ran the village?

If the village did so much of its own work, it must have had a way to choose the people who did it. Inscriptions from the Chola period describe one such way in detail, and we will read them in the next lesson.
