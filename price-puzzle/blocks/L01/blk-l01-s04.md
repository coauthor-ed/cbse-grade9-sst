# From a Table to a Curve

<!-- STEP -->
## A demand schedule

Here are Srivalli's three choices as a table. The amount one buyer wants at different prices is her **individual demand**, and a table of it is called a **demand schedule**.

| Price per kg | Srivalli buys |
|---|---|
| ₹150 | 1 kg |
| ₹100 | 2 kg |
| ₹50 | 3 kg |

<!-- STEP -->
## Three points on a graph

Now draw a graph. Price goes up the side, in rupees per kg, and quantity goes along the bottom, in kg. Each row of the table becomes one point: A is 1 kg at ₹150, B is 2 kg at ₹100 and C is 3 kg at ₹50.

<!-- STEP -->
## The demand curve

Join A, B and C, and you get a line that slopes down from left to right. This is Srivalli's **demand curve**, DD'. When only the price changes, she moves along this curve, from A to B to C, and the curve itself stays where it is.
